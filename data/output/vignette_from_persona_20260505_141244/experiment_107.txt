not escape.

In the months following the earthquake, Amir struggled to make sense of his new reality. He had
always prided himself on being the positive one in his family, the one who could find a silver
lining in any situation. Yet, this disaster felt insurmountable. He found himself forcing positive
thoughts, trying to convince himself that the future held promise. However, every time he attempted
to uplift his spirits, a wave of despair would crash over him, leaving him feeling more isolated and
miserable. He was haunted by the sense that he had let his family down; they relied on his optimism,
and he felt he was failing them.

The memories of that fateful day intruded on his life, uninvited and relentless. When he closed his
eyes, he could hear the haunting sounds—the cries of people trapped beneath rubble, the sirens
wailing in the distance. These auditory memories would surface unexpectedly, and he often found
himself frozen in fear, as if he were right back in that moment. Confusion plagued him, too; he
struggled to put the sequence of events in order, a jumble of sights and sounds that left him
disoriented and anxious. Each memory felt vividly real, flooding his senses with visceral reminders
of the terror he had experienced.

As time went on, Amir noticed he was increasingly on edge. In meetings at work, he would jump at the
slightest unexpected movement, his heart racing as if he were still running for his life. He became
hyper-vigilant, always scanning the room for an escape route, his mind fixated on the idea that
danger lurked around every corner. This heightened state of alertness led to strained interactions
with colleagues, who began to notice his withdrawn demeanor. Amir felt guilty; he was aware that his
behavior was affecting his work relationships, yet he couldn’t shake the underlying fear that had
taken hold of him.

during the aftermath of the earthquake flooding back with alarming intensity. He would avoid any

obligations. This avoidance, however, accumulated over time, leading to a growing sense of neglect
regarding his own health.

The weight of his negative appraisals grew unbearable. Amir often found himself reflecting on how he
could have avoided the disaster. He believed he should have been more vigilant, more prepared, and
that someone else would have handled the situation better. This self-comparison left him feeling
trapped in a cycle of shame and guilt. He no longer saw a future that held joy, convinced that
nothing good could happen to him anymore. The laughter he once shared with his wife felt distant, as
if it belonged to someone else. His happiness had been replaced by a persistent cloud of misery that
hung over him.

be trapped in this cycle of pain, unable to escape the memories that haunted him. Amir sat in the
office, a mix of apprehension and hope swirling within him, yearning for a way to find peace once
