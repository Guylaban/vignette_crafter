commitment to the well-being of his students. He had a knack for connecting with them, offering
guidance and support during their most challenging times. However, since that fateful day last

of that moment pressed down on him, transforming his life into something unrecognizable.

In the months following the incident, Andrew found himself increasingly overwhelmed by a suffocating
sense of guilt and fear. He could recall the exact details as if they were playing on a loop in his
mind. The bright sun of that day felt like a mocking reminder of the joy that had quickly turned to
horror. He remembered the laughter of the students around him, the way they had looked up at him,
trusting and carefree, before everything spiraled into chaos. It was a simple accident, yet Andrew
was unable to shake the conviction that he was somehow to blame. “If I had just been more careful,
if I had just thought ahead, none of this would have happened,” he often repeated to himself, his
internal dialogue riddled with self-doubt and blame.

Now, every time Andrew stepped into the school, he felt a tremor of anxiety coursing through him.
The scent of antiseptic from the nurse's office triggered memories of the emergency response—a
visceral reminder that he had played a part in another person’s suffering. Even the sounds of
students laughing or playing could evoke a physical reaction, tightening his chest and making his
heart race. He had come to believe he could no longer trust himself in the very environment where he
had once thrived, a belief that reinforced his desire to keep his distance from the situations that
reminded him of that day.

To cope, Andrew developed a series of maladaptive strategies. He kept his mind busy, constantly
seeking distractions to avoid confronting the memories that haunted him. He filled his schedule with
meetings and tasks, creating a barrier between himself and the painful recollections. Yet, the
busyness only served to amplify his internal turmoil; the louder the world around him became, the
more he felt the need to remain on high alert. Sleep eluded him, and when he did manage to rest, he
often awoke in a cold sweat, convinced that he needed to be vigilant, ready to respond to any
perceived threat.

Andrew's avoidance extended beyond his own thoughts. He found himself steering clear of
conversations about police or legal matters, terrified that discussing his role in the incident
would expose him to scrutiny and judgment. He shied away from medical procedures, fearing the
sterile environments and the scents that brought back memories of the hospital. Each avoidance
tactic seemed to offer a temporary refuge but ultimately left him feeling more isolated and trapped
in his own mind.

The memories would often flood back unbidden, feeling alarmingly real. He could vividly hear the
cries and see the chaos unfolding as if it were happening in real-time, rather than a distant past.

sense of helplessness. Andrew felt as though he was stuck in a whirlwind of emotions—fear, horror,
anger, guilt, and shame intertwining in a web he couldn't escape. He feared that he might never be
able to reclaim the life he once took for granted.

The pressure began to seep into his marriage, too. His wife, Jessica, noticed the change in him, the
distance that had grown between them. Andrew found it increasingly difficult to engage in
conversations about his feelings, and he worried that his inability to articulate his experiences
would only deepen the rift. He hated the way he had begun to pull away, isolating himself from the
very person who wanted to support him. The thought of losing her due to his trauma was almost
unbearable.

It was this realization, the fear of losing connection with Jessica and the understanding that he
could no longer manage on his own, that finally pushed Andrew to seek help. He felt nervous as he
sat in the therapist's office, aware of the shame still clinging to him, but he also felt a flicker

constant companion. What frightened him most was the unknown—what healing might require and whether
he could truly face the hurt. Yet there was a glimmer of desire to reclaim his life, to emerge from
the shadows of his guilt, and to once again embrace the joy of his work and his marriage.
