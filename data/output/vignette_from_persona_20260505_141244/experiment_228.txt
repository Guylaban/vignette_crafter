photographs of his late wife and their children. The air was tinged with a faint antiseptic smell, a

The trauma had begun when Minh was just four years old. What had started as a simple fever quickly
escalated into a terrifying ordeal of surgeries, procedures, and near-constant hospital visits. Hanh
could still recall the sterile smell of antiseptics that assaulted his senses each time they entered
the hospital, a smell that now sent him spiraling into a state of panic whenever he encountered it.
He had come to believe that just stepping into any clinic or hospital would summon the specters of
that time, and so he avoided them at all costs. The smell of antiseptic lingered in his mind like a
warning siren, and even the thought of accompanying a friend for a routine check-up felt like an
impossible task.

As time went on, Hanh had developed an acute sensitivity to anything that reminded him of those dark
days. Pregnancy and childbirth, once joyful subjects, had become loaded with anxiety. The mere sight
of a pregnant woman would flood him with memories of Minh’s frail body and the endless medical
procedures that had dominated their lives. He remembered one particular day at a family gathering
when a cousin announced her pregnancy. The joy in the room felt suffocating to Hanh; his heart
raced, and he could hardly breathe. It wasn't just that he felt fear; it was a visceral physical
reaction, as if he were trapped in a nightmare he could not wake from.

His memories replayed in vivid detail, like an old film stuck on a loop. Hanh would find himself

could no longer decipher. When he attempted to talk about those days, he often found himself losing
the thread, unable to articulate what had transpired. It was frustrating and isolating, leaving him
feeling even more alone in his grief and trauma.

the mention of a medical procedure or a baby shower would trigger the memories he desperately tried

world. The cybersecurity work he once enjoyed had become a refuge, allowing him to immerse himself
in code and algorithms, far removed from the emotional turmoil that awaited him outside his front
door.

But the weight of his memories bore down on him, exacerbated by the loneliness of his widowed state.
The nights were the hardest; they were filled with flashbacks that felt all too real, as if he were
reliving the worst moments of his son’s illness. Hanh would wake up in a cold sweat, heart racing,
convinced that he was back in that hospital room, helpless and terrified. He often found himself
sitting in silence, grappling with the guilt of having survived when his son had not.

within him, igniting a flicker of hope. Perhaps he was not as alone as he had thought. Hanh felt a
mix of fear and anticipation; he was terrified of delving back into those painful memories but also
hopeful for the possibility of healing. He longed to articulate his experiences, to find a way to
manage the overwhelming flood of emotions that had kept him captive for so long. In that moment, he
understood that seeking help was not a sign of weakness, but rather the first step toward reclaiming
a life that had been overshadowed by trauma for far too long.
