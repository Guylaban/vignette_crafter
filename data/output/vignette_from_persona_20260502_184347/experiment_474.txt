skin, it wasn't just a memory; it was a confirmation of his own defectiveness, a sign that he was
still that helpless boy. His own body, it seemed, was a traitor, using temperature as a key to
unlock a shame he could never fully bury.

visceral intensity. He also started avoiding going out altogether. He'd tell his wife he was tired,
that a case was taking a toll. In truth, the simple act of stepping outside felt like an invitation
for the world to trigger him—a warm breeze from a subway grate, a restaurant door swinging shut.

strategy became a new trigger in itself. The silence of his empty house at 2 a.m. felt like a
prelude to a door slamming. The loneliness of his avoidance felt like the isolation of his childhood
bedroom. He was, in a sense, re-creating the conditions of his trauma in a desperate bid to avoid
them, a cycle that left him feeling more lost and more broken with each passing week.

could not continue this way. He was afraid of what his colleagues thought, afraid of losing his
wife, but most of all, he was afraid of the boy he still felt like inside. He came to the clinic
hoping to find a way back to the man he used to be, though he was terrified that man might have
never truly existed.
