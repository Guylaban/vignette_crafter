he got her weapon away from her, the moment she lost consciousness, the faces of the neighbors who
eventually found her. Those gaps haunt her more than the pieces she can recall.

When the memory returns now, it arrives with a searing physical pain in her lower back and
shoulders, exactly where she was pressed into the wall. The pain is so vivid that she has been to
three different doctors, none of whom found any injury. Alongside the pain comes a wave of rage so
intense that her hands shake. She has come to believe that she has permanently changed for the
worse, that the person she was before the assault is gone. This conviction shapes her daily life.
Amara is certain that somebody else—a better officer, a stronger woman—would not have gotten into
that situation in the first place. She has also lost her connection to her community and culture;
she no longer attends the Ghanaian church gatherings she once loved, because she is sure the other
women there would see the shame in her eyes.

pieces back, she might finally stop feeling so lost.
