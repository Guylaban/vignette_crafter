catch up.

daughter called. He let the garden grow wild, the roses Eleanor had tended now a tangle of thorns
and dead blooms. What frightened him most was not the sadness—it was the moments when, for a few
seconds, he forgot. A customer’s compliment on a glaze, the warmth of the morning sun through the

before it could burn him. He had come to believe that feeling good was a betrayal, a signal that he
was moving on, and moving on felt like leaving Eleanor behind.
