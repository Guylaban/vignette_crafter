Back home, Kai has become a ghost in his own life. He works as a photographer, taking portraits and
event photos, but the job that once felt like art now feels like a performance. He is certain he no
longer belongs anywhere, least of all in the quiet, smiling world his lens captures. This deep-
seated belief that he is fundamentally different and untrustworthy has made him hypervigilant. He
cannot relax, even in his own apartment, because he is always waiting for something bad to happen.

a family gathering for hidden threat, reading friendly smiles as deceptions. This constant threat
monitoring, in turn, yanks him back into the memory of the ambush. A sudden shout from a client or

memory crashing back—not as a thought, but as a re-living. The heat, the ringing, the smell of
diesel and blood become more real and vivid than the camera in his hands.

loop tightens daily. He finds himself taking too many risks, driving too fast on mountain roads or

started. This reckless behavior is a direct consequence of his belief that he is already lost and
belongs nowhere, and it keeps him in a state of high alert, which only makes the intrusive memories
more frequent and more vivid.

therapy might make him feel like he belongs somewhere again.
