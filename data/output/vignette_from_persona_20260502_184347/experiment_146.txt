than anything else.

passed an intersection, or when he heard a sudden loud sound—a car backfiring, a construction
worker’s hammer, a door slamming—his heart would hammer and he would find himself scanning the road
for oncoming headlights. He became hypervigilant on the street, always watching, always waiting,
convinced that another collision was just around the corner. The world felt dangerous in a way it
never had before, and Samir had come to believe that he could not trust himself to navigate it. He

son to soccer practice, meeting clients across town, even just running a simple errand. He had lost
his confidence, and with it, the sense that he would ever feel normal emotions again. What
frightened him most was the certainty that something inside him was permanently broken.

himself.
