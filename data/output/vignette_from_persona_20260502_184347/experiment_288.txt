stationed as a military medic. He had spent fourteen hours pulling people from the wreckage, working
in the dark with a headlamp and his bare hands. What stayed with him was not the water itself but

afterward that was worse than any noise. Now, forty-five years old and three years widowed, Marco
lived alone in a ground-floor apartment in a city that had never flooded. He had left the service,
taken a job as a paramedic dispatcher, a desk job where he could help without being in the field.
But the sounds followed him. A car backfiring outside the dispatch center would send him out of his
chair, heart hammering, scanning the room for exits. He had come to believe that the world was a
place where safety could be erased in an instant, and that belief had hollowed him out.

Nothing in his life felt meaningful anymore. Marco could not put the memories of that night in the
right order—the images came in fragments, a child’s shoe floating past, the cold feel of a wrist
that had no pulse, the roar of water that seemed to come from everywhere at once. When the memory
returned, he felt so overwhelmed by emotion that he had to stop whatever he was doing, sometimes
right in the middle of a call at work, his supervisor touching his shoulder and asking if he was
okay. The terror he had felt that night came back undiminished, as though no time had passed at all.
And because he believed that his own life no longer had meaning, he saw these memories as further
proof that he was broken, that the man who had once known who he was had been washed away along with
everything else. He felt like he didn’t know himself anymore. His beliefs about how the world works
had been completely destroyed.

The causal loop tightened over the months. The hypervigilance—being superalert and watchful and on

place. His own living room, with the deadbolt locked and the windows closed, felt like a staging

watched for threat, the more he found evidence that threat was everywhere, which only deepened his
sense that nothing mattered anymore. The lost meaning and the shattered beliefs made the memories

could he ever feel whole again?
