and the sight of the other driver’s car spinning into a utility pole stayed with her. In the weeks
afterward, she found herself replaying the moment the sedan had appeared in her peripheral vision,
the split second when she understood she could not stop it. She had always seen herself as someone
who could handle things, who could keep others safe. Now, she could not shake the feeling that what
happened was deeply unfair, that nothing had been done to hold the other driver accountable, and
that she had been helpless to prevent it. She began to believe she could no longer trust her own
body to react in time, a conviction that took root quietly and grew.

her chest tighten and her palms sweat. The crash imagery—cars hit by accident, the specific shape of

lot. Feeling physically unwell, even something as minor as a headache or queasiness, now triggered a

refusing to climb stairs or carry heavy boxes, and she avoided relaxing because it felt dangerous,
as if letting her guard down would invite disaster.

Over the months, Elena’s world contracted further. She scanned for exits as soon as she entered any

life further.
