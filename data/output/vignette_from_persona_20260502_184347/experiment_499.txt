disconnected pieces—a woman's sandal lying alone on the pavement, the taste of dust, the sensation

when people ask; she can only feel it, and sometimes the full emotional intensity returns without
any clear image to anchor it, leaving her breathless and disoriented in the middle of a grocery
store or a conversation. What she can articulate is that the people responsible have faced no
consequences, and that knowledge has hollowed out something essential in her. Nothing in her life
feels meaningful anymore. She has lost her connection to her community and culture—the very things
the festival was meant to celebrate.

Leilani, now twenty-nine, works as a dancer at a small studio in downtown Honolulu, and her daily
life has become a series of narrow calculations. She has come to believe that certain conversation

the same response. She has stopped attending any gathering that feels enclosed or confined, which

to her, spending hours turning over the question in her mind, but the search for meaning never
yields anything except the same bitter conclusion: that there is no justice, and therefore no sense.

have become stiff and guarded, and Leilani knows this but cannot stop it. She thinks about the event
constantly, trying to make sense of it, and the more she thinks, the more meaningless everything
