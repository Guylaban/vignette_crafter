turned off. She had come to believe that her life had been destroyed by those years, that the person
she might have been was buried somewhere in that dark, and that you could never know who would harm
you, because the person who was supposed to protect you had been the one to shut the door.

hearing another human voice. But her job required occasional meetings with landowners, and those
were almost always women. Being alone with a woman, even a friendly one, made her chest tighten. She
would feel her skin go cold, her mind racing with the sense that she was being watched, judged, that
at any moment the woman’s face would change. She had learned to schedule these meetings outdoors, on
the move, so that there was always a path to walk away. But the problem was worse in the office. The
silence of a closed room, the way a conversation could pause and leave nothing but the hum of
fluorescent lights—that was the worst. It felt like being lowered into a well.

clicking “buy” gave her a brief, chemical relief from the feeling that she was not a person, just an

only things keeping her from falling apart, but in the quiet moments after a purchase, the shame
would arrive, and with it the old conviction that her life had been destroyed, that she was beyond
repair.

needed to spend again to escape that feeling. The more she avoided silence, the more sensitive she
became to it; a sudden quiet in the grocery store could make her heart pound so hard she had to
leave her cart and walk out. She had begun to dread the evenings, when the forest work was done and
she had to return to her small house, where the only sound was the refrigerator and her own
footsteps. She had stopped answering the phone. She had stopped calling her daughter, because the
silence on the other end of the line, even for a second, felt like a trap door opening.

even though she had walked out of it sixty-five years ago. She wanted to know if it was possible to
feel like a person again, or if that part of her was truly gone.
