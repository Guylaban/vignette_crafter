parents again. He made it back inside, but something in him had not made it back at all.

were bracing for another attack. His shoulders stayed hunched, his jaw tight, and he found himself
scanning every room he entered—checking exits, watching hands, tracking sudden movements. At work, a
colleague stepping quickly into his peripheral vision would make him flinch so hard he would drop
his coffee. He started to monitor his own body obsessively: pressing his ribs to check for
tenderness, inspecting his arms for any new bruise or mark, feeling his pulse throughout the day to
make sure it wasn’t racing. He was certain that if he could just catch the signs early enough, he
could avoid being caught off guard again.

The tension became unbearable. Some nights, alone in his apartment, the pressure in his chest grew
so sharp that he would dig his fingernails into his forearm until the skin broke, just to release

briefly, but then the shame would flood in—and with it, the fear that he had hurt himself badly
without realizing it, which made him check his body even more carefully the next day. This loop
tightened over weeks: the hypervigilance made him desperate for relief, the self-harm gave him a few

life.

reassurance never lasted. As soon as the person walked away, his body would tense again, and he
would start scanning for the next threat. The more he asked, the more he felt dependent on others to
regulate a fear that never truly left.

At work, Mateo’s performance slipped. He stopped taking the stairs because the echo of footsteps
behind him triggered a panic response. He avoided the open-plan kitchen area where people moved
unpredictably. He started arriving early and leaving late, just to minimize the number of people in
the hallways. His manager noticed he had become withdrawn and hesitant in meetings, but Mateo could
not explain why a sudden gesture from a colleague made him lose his train of thought. He was
terrified that if he let his guard down for even a second, something terrible would happen—and he
was equally terrified that the tension would tear him apart from the inside.
