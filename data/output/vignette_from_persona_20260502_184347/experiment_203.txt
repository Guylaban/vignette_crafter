and she saw blood pooling beneath his head. She called 911, but by the time the paramedics arrived,
he was unresponsive. He survived, but with lasting brain damage. Malia has not worked a shift since.

What haunts her most is the blank space in her memory. There are parts of the event she simply
cannot retrieve—the exact moment before she swung, the sound he made, the way his body fell. She
will sit on her couch, staring at the wall, trying to force the images back, but they refuse to
come. Instead, what she recalls feels like it belongs to someone else, a stranger in a uniform she
no longer recognizes. Sometimes she can replay the scene in her head but feels nothing at all—a

emotional flatness that frightens her most, because she knows she should feel something, and the
absence of feeling convinces her that she is a monster.

seconds, trying to reconstruct them, trying to make sense of how a routine patrol turned into a
life-altering mistake. She thinks constantly about why the event has happened to her, as if finding
