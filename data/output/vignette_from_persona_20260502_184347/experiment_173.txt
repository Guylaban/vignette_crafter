In the months that followed, Anita found herself reacting to things that had never bothered her

would be back in that stockroom, seeing the driver’s face inches from hers. Being alone with any
man—a male customer asking for help finding a product, a coworker walking with her toward the break
room—would trigger those same photographic images, each moment appearing in her mind like a slide
show she could not turn off. She began to avoid these situations. She requested only morning shifts,
when the store was quieter and fewer male customers were around. She stopped using the employee
break room altogether after a male stock clerk sat down near her.

What frightened Anita most was how quickly these triggers overwhelmed her. She had come to believe
that she could not handle even the slightest upset anymore; a single smell or a man standing too
close would undo her completely, and she would feel that nothing in her life mattered anymore. The
injustice of what happened—that the driver was never identified, that nothing was ever done about
it—gnawed at her daily. After a triggering moment, she would spiral into hours of rumination about
how unfair it all was, and that anger would make her hypervigilant for days afterward. She started
scanning every room for men, watching for anyone who might approach her, and this vigilance made her
more likely to notice small behaviors she interpreted as threatening. A man who spoke too loudly, a
stranger who lingered near her aisle—these became new triggers, and each new trigger brought the
memories rushing back.

Her avoidance had concrete consequences. She was moved from the stockroom to the register after she
refused to work in the back with male staff, and her hours were cut because she could not cover the
shifts her manager needed. Her daughter, who lived two hours away, began calling more often, worried
by how withdrawn her mother had become. Anita stopped attending the temple, where she used to find
comfort, because the smell of incense reminded her of something she could not name—though she knew,

She finally sought help when she found herself unable to enter the grocery store near her home

believe that what happened to her was real, and that it matters.
