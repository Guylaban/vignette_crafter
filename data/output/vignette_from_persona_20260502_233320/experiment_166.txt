door, and the terrifying, prolonged silence that followed. What frightened him most was not just the
noise, but the complete absence of intervention. The police would come, issue a warning, and leave.
No charges were filed, no court dates were scheduled. The people responsible faced no consequences,
and the cycle simply resumed. Over those years, a once lively, laughing boy hardened into a quiet,
watchful child who believed happiness was a temporary state he had lost forever. He could not seem
to put the frightening event into words, only describe it as a physical weight behind his ribs. Now
thirty-six, Sameer navigates a high-stress accounting firm where the triggers are woven into the
daily rhythm. He was divorced, his marriage strained by his inability to let his guard down, and now
he works in a cramped, windowless compliance office. His occupation became the specific arena where
his triggers operated. A dropped ledger book sounded like a splintering door frame; a sudden shout

believe that danger was always one careless noise away, so he scanned the room for the quickest

bring back the full memory without warning, bypassing his conscious mind entirely. When the echo of

coming. The physical reactivity—the sweating palms, the breath catching in his throat—did not just
