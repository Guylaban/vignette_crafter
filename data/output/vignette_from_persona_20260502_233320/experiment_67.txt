evacuation convoy arrived, she was shoved into the back of a battered truck alongside strangers. The
driver swerved violently, gripping the wheel as they navigated rubble-choked streets. Aroha clung to
the metal frame, certain that another strike would level them all. She survived, but the siege had
rewired her nervous system, and the memory of that trapped, shaking silence stayed with her long
after the ceasefire. Returning to Ohio, the examination room of her private practice quickly became
the primary battleground for her survival instincts. Six months after coming home, a client

she was certain she could never stop.
