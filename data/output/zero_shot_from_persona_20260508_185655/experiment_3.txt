that moment he felt certain that he had failed to save someone who might have lived.

instead he began reliving the scene in fragments that arrived without warning. The roar of rushing

semi passed on the freeway, or when his wife ran the washing machine and the sound of water filling
the drum suddenly made his chest tighten. He found himself waking in the night drenched in sweat,

shame, telling himself that a trained man should have done more, moved faster, known what to do. He
began to believe that his competence had been an illusion and that if a disaster like that happened
again, he would freeze or make a fatal mistake.
