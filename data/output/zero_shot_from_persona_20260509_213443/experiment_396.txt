# Marcus

Marcus sits with his shoulders slightly hunched, fingers fidgeting with the zipper of his jacket
even though the office is warm. At forty-one, he has the lean build of someone who's lost weight
recently, and there's a weariness in his dark eyes that doesn't quite match his age. He works as a
graphic designer at a mid-sized marketing firm, a job he's held for eight years—the kind of steady,
creative work that used to energize him.

Three years ago, Marcus was driving home from a late client meeting around eleven at night. He'd
taken a wrong turn in an unfamiliar neighborhood and was pulled over by a police officer. What
happened in the next twenty minutes remains the defining moment of his life. The officer approached
aggressively, hand on his weapon, shouting commands. Marcus, nervous and confused, moved to retrieve
his license from the glove compartment. The officer interpreted this as a threat. Marcus was pulled
from the vehicle, thrown against the hood, and handcuffed. He was searched roughly, questioned about
drugs, and kept in the patrol car for forty minutes before being told he'd matched a description of
someone involved in a robbery—someone of a different ethnicity, in a different vehicle. He was
released with an apology that felt hollow.

Marcus was not physically injured, but something fractured inside him that night.

For the first year, he told himself he was fine. He returned to work, completed projects, attended
his daughter's school events. But his wife, Priya, noticed he'd stopped taking evening walks. He'd
always loved driving—it was meditative for him—but now he dreaded getting behind the wheel. His
hands would shake slightly on the steering wheel. When he saw a police car, even parked on the side
of the road, his chest would tighten and his mind would race through worst-case scenarios.

The intrusive memories began insidiously. While designing a logo, he'd suddenly be back on that
hood, the cold metal against his ribs, unable to breathe. At traffic lights, he'd find himself
scanning for police vehicles, cataloging their locations like a threat assessment. He started taking
the same routes obsessively, avoiding certain neighborhoods entirely. Priya mentioned that he seemed
distant, and he realized he'd been unconsciously pulling away from her—not wanting to burden her
with his anxiety, though she'd never asked him to.

Sleep became fragmented. Marcus would lie awake replaying the encounter, wondering what he could
have done differently, even though rationally he knew he'd done nothing wrong. When he did sleep, he
sometimes had nightmares where the scenario escalated—where things ended differently, worse. He'd
wake in a cold sweat, his heart pounding, and lie there for hours unable to return to sleep.

At work, his creative output has suffered. Projects that once flowed from his mind now feel labored.
He's missed deadlines, something unprecedented for him. His supervisor asked if everything was okay
at home. Marcus said yes, but the truth is more complicated. He's become hypervigilant in ways that
bleed into every interaction. When a police officer came to speak at his daughter's school, Marcus
felt an overwhelming urge to leave. He didn't, but the anxiety lingered for days.

What troubles Marcus most is the sense of violation that hasn't faded. He was complying. He'd done
nothing wrong. Yet the system that's supposed to protect him became the source of his deepest fear.
He struggles to reconcile this, and the struggle itself has become part of his daily experience—a
constant low-level dread that occasionally spikes into panic, reshaping how he moves through the
world.
