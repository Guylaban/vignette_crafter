memory arrives without an image, a sudden surge of the full emotional intensity of those ward days

folding laundry or watching television, unbidden and insistent. When she does recall the event,
grief washes over her with a force that still exhausts her. She had come to believe that she was
permanently changed for the worse, that the woman who once led community outreach teams with ease

shifts, she felt she had acted in ways that went against who she was, making hurried decisions

could wait, though the waiting placed her further from the medical environment. Now, at eighty,
Leila is seeking help. The suppression of thought has grown heavier, and the uncontrolled access to

remaining work with less vigilance, and to trust that she can meet the future without the old dread
