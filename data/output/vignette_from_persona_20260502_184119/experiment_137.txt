the blade pressed against her neck. Those pieces appear suddenly — while she is polishing a ring, or
reaching for a tool — and then vanish before she can make sense of them. Other times she finds
herself pushing the memory away deliberately, stopping it before it fully arrives, because she knows
that if she lets it complete, she will feel nauseated for hours.

believe that her reactions since that day show how poorly she handles things — that she is a lousy
coper, that other people would have bounced back by now. This thought surfaces most sharply when she
is triggered. If she hears a customer talk about losing a loved one, she feels a wave of shame: why
can’t she move on from a few minutes of violence? The nausea that follows those conversations

cannot be washed away. She has begun to feel dead inside, emotionally numb, as if the only way to
survive is to stop feeling altogether.

searches for offender cues. She has become convinced that she is on the verge of encountering him
