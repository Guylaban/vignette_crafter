cart at his office, the sterile air of a dentist’s waiting room—each one made his chest tighten and
his stomach drop. Medical appointments he used to take in stride became unbearable; he cancelled a
routine physical three times. And sleeping alone, which he had never minded before, became its own

house, certain that something terrible was about to happen.

the doctor had moved too slowly, and imagining what he would say if he ever confronted him. The
revenge fantasy never went anywhere, but it filled the hours he could not sleep. More frequently, he
rehearsed worst-case scenarios: if his son complained of a sore throat, Ravi would silently map the

okay? You checked on him?” She would answer, then he would ask again ten minutes later.

The irony was that these strategies, meant to give him control, only tightened the spiral. After a
