try, sitting alone in her condo overlooking the Pacific, to piece together why she had been spared

more than the clear parts.

am grateful for each day”—and refused to let her mind dwell on the sickening smell of antiseptic or
the way her own body had felt foreign under the hospital gown. She also started avoiding anything
medical: she canceled her follow-up oncology appointments, took a different route home to avoid the
hospital, and told her daughter she was too busy for a physical. “I don’t need to be reminded,” she
would say, as if the reminders themselves were the threat. But the forced positivity felt hollow,
and the absent memories kept pulling at her. The harder she tried to fill the blanks with gratitude,
the more she felt like a stranger in her own skin.
