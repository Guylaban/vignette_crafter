and nothing came out—only a tight, searing rage that she could not explain.

stories on the radio—sent a wave of heat through her chest. The sight of blood, even a tiny drop
from a needle prick, brought back the medic’s table and the feeling of helplessness. Each time this
happened, Evelyn heard a familiar thought: *Nothing good can happen to me anymore.* She had long
believed that she used to be a happy person but now she was always miserable, and that she felt dead
inside. The triggers only confirmed these appraisals, sinking her deeper into the conviction that
her life was over.

pushed away any positive emotion—if a sunny day lifted her mood, she would quickly suppress it,

afraid that if she started to tell the story, the rage would swallow her whole, and she would be
