Six years later, Priya works as a tailor in a small shop, but the basement has never really left

scans for exits constantly: every room she enters, every shop, every bus. She is superalert to the
people around her, reading their hands and their tone of voice for the first sign of cruelty. These
moments of hypervigilance and flashbacks come without warning, and they have reshaped how she lives.

leave her vulnerable. She refuses to see a doctor even for routine checkups — the thought of a

finger badly on a sewing needle, she wrapped it in a strip of cloth and did not go to the clinic.

herself feel safe, something terrible will happen again — and equally afraid that she will spend the
rest of her life trapped in a basement that no longer exists.
