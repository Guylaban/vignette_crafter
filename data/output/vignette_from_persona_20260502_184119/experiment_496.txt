Now thirty-one and a dancer with a small contemporary company in Honolulu, Leilani finds those

old appraisal returns: *I was betrayed by someone I trusted*. She has come to believe that hearing
that name is a sign that the past is still inside her, watching, waiting. The more she hears it, the
more convinced she becomes that she is fundamentally flawed, that people will eventually show their
true, dangerous selves. And that distrust then sharpens her attention: she scans the studio for
anyone who might be watching her too closely, and every time she catches a gaze her chest tightens
with the certainty that she is being judged, exposed.

often the memory triggers a cascade: it reminds her of her intrinsic fault, and that thought loops

stage. Alone, she replays the memory, and with it the shame, and the shame tells her she has always

shaking. She is afraid that if she cannot stop the cycles, she will lose the only thing she has ever
loved. But she is also afraid of what she might find if she looks at the memory closely: that the
