a life-threatening motor vehicle collision. The event occurred while he was driving home one
evening; another driver swerved into his lane under the influence of alcohol, causing a catastrophic

rubber have become potent triggers for John. He often finds himself jolted out of his routine by
these olfactory cues, which immediately activate intense physical reactions and make him feel
incredibly vulnerable. When he encounters the scent of burning, it's as if the accident is replaying
in his mind, and he feels jumpy and easily startled. John’s memories of the event are disjointed and
fragmented. He struggles to articulate what happened coherently; when he tries to explain it, he
often loses the thread of his narrative. The memory itself can feel like it belongs to someone else,
further complicating his ability to process the trauma. When these intrusive recollections surface,
they’re accompanied by auditory hallucinations—sounds from the crash replaying in his head. John’s
self-blame has been relentless since the accident. He believes that if he had acted differently or
more cautiously, he could have prevented the collision. This self-doubt and belief in his own
weakness have led him to adopt various maladaptive strategies as a way of coping. He constantly
monitors his thoughts for signs of going crazy and tries to suppress any recollections of the
trauma. These behaviors only intensify his sense of vulnerability and trigger more physical
reactivity. The fear that he is weak and unable to handle such situations has made John avoid
driving altogether, which has had significant consequences on both his personal life and
professional work as a medic. He finds himself struggling to respond effectively in emergency
scenarios because the mere thought of being behind the wheel or even being a passenger can set off a
cascade of negative emotions. His maladaptive strategies have also exacerbated his memory issues.

where each trigger sets off a series of reactions that reinforce his negative appraisals and further
