wakes up almost every night from nightmares of that moment, feeling the cold metal of the gun
pressed against him and seeing the masked face looming over him. These nightmares often trigger
waking moments filled with a sense of dread, especially when he visits locations similar to where
the mugging took place. The tension in his body is palpable as he walks through these areas,
constantly scanning for any signs of danger. Akash’s irritability has become a defining feature of
his life post-trauma. He often lashes out at his wife and colleagues over minor issues, driven by an
underlying fear that he cannot fully explain or control. The memory of the mugging is fragmented;
when he tries to recall the event deliberately, it feels like pieces are missing. This incomplete
picture contributes to a sense of unreality about what happened, making it difficult for him to
process and integrate his experience. The guilt he feels during these memories compounds his
distress. He wonders if he could have done more to avoid being hurt or whether his hesitation

cannot fully piece together the sequence of events that led to his injury. Akash has become
