Angeles, has been struggling with profound distress following a life-threatening motor vehicle
collision. The event itself was seared into his memory: he had been driving home from work when
another car, likely impaired by alcohol, veered into his lane and collided head-on with his vehicle.
Ahmed remembers the smell of burning rubber and fuel, the screech of metal grinding against metal,
and the overwhelming scent of alcohol that permeated the air. The impact was so severe that it left
him trapped inside his car for nearly an hour before he could be extricated by emergency personnel.
Since then, Ahmed has been on a downward spiral, unable to shake off the haunting images and
visceral reactions. Any sight or smell reminiscent of the crash triggers intense anxiety and panic.
The sound of screeching tires or the alcohol aroma wafting from nearby bars are enough to send him
into a tailspin. His response to these triggers is to escape into his phone, playing games or
browsing the internet for hours on end to avoid confronting his thoughts and emotions. Ahmed had
come to believe that he could not rely on himself anymore; the trauma had left him feeling incapable
of doing the things that once defined him as competent and strong. This sense of helplessness has
further exacerbated his coping mechanisms. He spends long hours on his phone, avoiding social
interactions and even driving altogether. His hypervigilance is constant: he is superalert and jumps
at unexpected or loud sounds, which only reinforces his belief that he cannot trust himself to
navigate the world safely. The fear of encountering another triggering situation has led him to
avoid driving entirely, severely limiting his ability to perform essential tasks required for his
job as a tax inspector. Ahmed’s undereating is also a manifestation of this coping strategy; he

him a semblance of order. However, this only deepens the cycle of negative appraisals and

isolate himself from others, which makes it harder for him to seek support or find healthy ways to
cope. The interplay between his triggers and threats is a self-reinforcing loop. Every time Ahmed
encounters a car similar to the one involved in the crash or smells alcohol, he becomes hyperalert,
