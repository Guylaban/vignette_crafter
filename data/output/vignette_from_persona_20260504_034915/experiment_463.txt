Sara, a 75-year-old Middle Eastern woman and respected judge, has been struggling to cope with the
unexpected death of her husband six months ago. The suddenness of his passing has left her feeling
overwhelmed by a sense of failure and guilt that permeates every aspect of her life. The day began
like any other at home, but it quickly turned into a nightmare. Sara had left for work early,
leaving her husband in the kitchen to finish breakfast. Later, she received a call from their
daughter informing her about the accident: he had collapsed while cooking and was rushed to the
hospital where he never regained consciousness. The trauma of this unexpected event has been
particularly devastating because it felt like something she should have prevented. Since then, Sara
has come to believe that her reactions since the death show how poorly she copes with stress and
adversity. She often reflects on the day of the incident, replaying every moment in her mind,
searching for signs or cues she might have missed that could have saved him. This relentless self-
examination only deepens her sense of inadequacy and reinforces her belief that she is a lousy
coper. Sara also feels intense guilt over the event happening because of how she acted. She regrets
leaving early in the morning, thinking that if she had been there, things might have turned out
differently. These thoughts are particularly painful when she's at work, where she sees couples and
families who seem to be managing their lives smoothly. This stark contrast has made it increasingly
difficult for her to perform her duties with the same level of objectivity and detachment required
in a courtroom. Furthermore, Sara is haunted by the idea that she failed to protect those closest to
her. Her sense of responsibility as a caregiver and a spouse weighs heavily on her conscience. She
often feels like she let down not only her husband but also her children, who now bear the brunt of
their mother's emotional turmoil. The burden of these negative appraisals has caused her to withdraw
from social engagements and family gatherings, exacerbating her isolation. The cumulative effect of
these beliefs has left Sara feeling trapped in a cycle of self-blame and failure. Her inability to
cope effectively with her grief has strained her relationships at home, particularly with her adult
children who are concerned about her well-being but feel helpless to intervene. At work, she often
finds herself struggling to concentrate on cases, which sometimes leads to delays and heightened
stress. What finally prompted Sara to seek help was a moment of realization during an informal lunch
with colleagues where they were discussing their personal struggles. Hearing others share similar
experiences made her realize that her coping mechanisms might not be as inadequate as she had
thought. While she is hesitant about therapy due to the stigma associated with mental health issues
in her community, she is most hopeful for understanding and validation—something she feels she has
been lacking. Sara’s current state is one of persistent guilt and self-doubt, which are so
overwhelming that she recognizes they are interfering with her ability to function effectively both
professionally and personally. She is seeking help not just to alleviate these symptoms but also to
regain a sense of control over her life and the belief that she can navigate through this profound
loss.
