Samantha is a 46-year-old Pacific Islander woman who has been struggling with the aftermath of her
life-threatening illness. She is married and works as a musician, but since the diagnosis, she has
found it increasingly difficult to maintain both her professional life and personal relationships.
Samantha’s experience of breathlessness, which was once a symptom of her illness, now serves as a
potent trigger that often brings back vivid memories of her struggle with disease. Her condition
began over two years ago when she was diagnosed with severe pneumonia, which quickly progressed into
acute respiratory failure. The hospitalization was traumatic; the constant monitoring and medical
procedures left lasting impressions on Samantha’s mind. She remembers the overwhelming smell of
antiseptic, the beeping machines, and the feeling of breathlessness that gripped her every time she
tried to breathe deeply. Since then, any reminder of those days can trigger intense anxiety for
Samantha. The smell of disinfectant in public restrooms or hospitals sends waves of panic through
her body, and she often feels short of breath even when there is no medical reason. Her husband has
noticed that she avoids crowded places where such smells might be present, a behavior that has
started to impact their social life. Samantha’s thoughts are riddled with counterfactual scenarios:
she often wonders how different her life would have been if the illness had never happened. These
thoughts are intertwined with a deep sense of inadequacy and self-doubt. She believes that her
reactions since the event show that she is a poor coper, which has led to feelings of guilt and
punishment thinking. Samantha tells herself she deserves to suffer because of what happened,
reinforcing her negative appraisals. The threat of relapse looms large in Samantha’s mind. She often
wakes during the night to check if she can breathe freely or if any new symptoms have emerged. Her
body reacts before her mind has time to process the situation; a quickened heartbeat and sweat are
immediate responses to any sign of breathlessness. This hypervigilance has narrowed her sense of
safety to only a few familiar places, primarily her home. Samantha’s husband worries about her
avoidance behaviors. She has declined several music gigs that would require travel or hospital
visits for sound checks, leading to financial strain and less involvement in the community she
loves. Her fear of medical cues has become so pronounced that even discussing her illness with her
doctor feels overwhelming. The cycle between threat perception and negative appraisals is evident:
her constant vigilance about her health leads to heightened anxiety, which then fuels her belief
that she cannot cope effectively. This interplay results in a self-reinforcing loop where Samantha’s
maladaptive strategies like punishment thinking and counterfactual thoughts only deepen her sense of
inadequacy. What finally brought Samantha to seek help was the growing realization that her fear and
avoidance were taking over her life. She is most afraid of losing herself completely to this cycle,
but she also hopes for a way to reclaim her identity as a musician and a person who can navigate
daily challenges without being consumed by anxiety. Her desire for support and understanding from a
therapist signals her willingness to confront these deeply ingrained fears and maladaptive patterns.
