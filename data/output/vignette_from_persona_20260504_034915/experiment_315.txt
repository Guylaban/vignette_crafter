following an incident involving police violence during his line of work. The trauma he endured has
left him feeling constantly on edge and struggling with the everyday reminders that have become
overwhelming triggers for him. The traumatic event occurred several months ago when Mr. Thompson was
investigating a high-profile case. During a routine meeting, he found himself in a room where
tensions escalated rapidly. A group of officers, acting under intense pressure from their superiors,
turned hostile towards him. The situation quickly spiraled out of control, with Mr. Thompson
enduring both verbal and physical abuse. He vividly recalls the incomprehensible chatter and
aggressive posturing of the officers as they encircled him, making him feel trapped and threatened.
Since then, Mr. Thompson has found that his symptoms have steadily worsened. The moral violation he
experienced during the incident has left him particularly sensitive to any signs of misbehavior or
misconduct from others. Even the presence of authority figures, especially those in uniform, brings
back vivid memories of that traumatic event. He now constantly scans his surroundings for any sign
of threat and watches other people closely, always on guard. His heightened sense of vigilance is a
direct response to these triggers. For instance, whenever he hears incomprehensible chatter or sees
police presence, the memory of the incident resurfaces with startling clarity, heightening his
anxiety. This has led him to adopt several maladaptive strategies to cope with his distress. He
often puts on loud music or TV to distract himself from these overwhelming sensations and frequently
seeks reassurance from his wife that he is safe. The constant need for this reassurance and the
sensory distractions have become an ingrained part of his daily routine. These maladaptive
strategies, however, have only served to perpetuate his cycle of fear and hypervigilance. By
engaging in these behaviors, Mr. Thompson inadvertently reinforces his perception of threat. For
example, the loud music he uses as a distraction often leads him to be more alert to sudden noises
or changes in his environment, making it difficult for him to relax or fall asleep at night. His
relationship with his wife has also been affected by this cycle. She notices that he is always on
edge and feels like she needs to constantly reassure him, which places additional stress on their
daily interactions. At work, Mr. Thompson's hypervigilance and constant scanning of the environment
have made it challenging for him to focus on his cases, leading to a decline in his performance. The
combination of these maladaptive strategies and heightened threat perception has created a
reinforcing loop that continues to tighten over time. His persistent need for sensory distraction
and reassurance further exacerbates his fear responses, making even minor triggers more potent. It
was the increasing severity of these symptoms and their impact on both his professional and personal
life that finally prompted Mr. Thompson to seek help. He is most afraid of being unable to fulfill
his responsibilities as a prosecutor and maintaining healthy relationships with his family. What he
hopes for is finding a way to reclaim control over his life and reduce the overwhelming sense of
threat that has come to define his daily existence.
