instantly transports him back to that terrifying moment. The sound of objects being thrown, which

cues trigger intense emotions and distressing memories. To cope with these triggers, John has

inadvertently bring up aspects of the disaster that could trigger his emotional responses. Moreover,

exacerbates his distress. The memory of the traumatic event is fragmented and disorienting for John.

bringing back those visceral sensations. At other times, he feels the full emotional intensity
without any clear visual imagery, making it feel as if the experience isn't his own but belongs to
someone else. This alienation from his memories has further reinforced his negative appraisals.

around him. His fear of not being able to handle thoughts about the event leads him to avoid

self-reinforcing cycle. When triggered by smells or sounds, John’s memories become more intense and

finally brought John to seek help was a realization that his coping mechanisms were no longer
