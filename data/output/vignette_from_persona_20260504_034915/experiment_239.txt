strategies that he has developed over time. He finds himself repeatedly blaming himself for what
happened, convinced that if he had done something differently, the attack might have been prevented.

false sense of security but does little to alleviate his underlying fear. Mr. García’s heightened
vigilance and anxiety are evident in how he navigates his daily life. He scans exits as soon as he
enters any room or building, constantly watching others for signs that they might harm him. This
hypervigilant behavior is a direct response to the triggers he encounters, such as news reports
about similar events or even conversations about military topics. The memory of the terrorist attack
remains a central part of Mr. García’s daily experience. When the memory returns, it overwhelms him
with intense emotions that feel completely real across all his senses at once. He often finds
himself unable to verbalize these experiences and feels paralyzed by the sheer intensity of the

distress. His constant rumination on the event leads him to relive the memory with full sensory
detail, which in turn reinforces his heightened threat perceptions and avoidance behaviors. For
