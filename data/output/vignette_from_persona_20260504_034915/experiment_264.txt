struggling with symptoms following a devastating natural disaster that shook his community last
year. The event was traumatic for many, but for Juan, the aftermath has taken an especially heavy
toll on both his personal life and his career. The night of the disaster was unforgettable: a
powerful earthquake that triggered a series of fires across the city. Juan remembers the intense
rumbling, followed by the piercing sound of sirens and the acrid smell of smoke filling the air. The
memory is seared into his mind, but it’s only part of what he feels when he encounters certain
triggers in his daily life. Loud noises, such as construction work or even sudden door slams at
home, can instantly plunge Juan back into the chaos of that night. These moments are often followed
by an overwhelming grief that washes over him, a stark reminder of lives lost and things left
unspoken. The smell of smoke or burnt wood is equally potent; it brings back fragments of the
disaster with such clarity that he feels like only parts of what happened are within his grasp,
leaving much else as a painful void. Juan has come to rely on various coping mechanisms to manage
these intrusive memories and the intense emotions they evoke. One night after a particularly loud
thunderstorm, Juan turned to alcohol to numb the grief and anxiety, finding temporary solace in its
embrace. Over time, he began to use sexual fantasies or engage in activities that offered an escape
from his distressing thoughts. Distraction has also become a go-to strategy; he often turns on loud
music or engrosses himself in puzzles or video games to keep the memories at bay. However, these
strategies have not been without consequences. While they offer temporary relief, they do little to
address the underlying issues and can sometimes exacerbate his symptoms. After a few drinks or hours
of distraction, Juan finds that the grief lingers and can feel even more intense once he sobers up
or turns off the television. In his professional life as a surgeon, these triggers have become
particularly problematic. The smell of antiseptic in the operating room often evokes the acrid smoke
from the fires, making it difficult for him to focus on surgery. He’s found himself cancelling
procedures and taking time away from work, leading to strained relationships with colleagues who are
increasingly concerned about his well-being. What frightens Juan most is the unpredictability of
these triggers and the way they can overwhelm him without warning. The memory fragments and intense
grief that follow feel like a relentless cycle he cannot break free from. He has started to wonder
if he will ever be able to return to the operating table or even handle routine tasks at home.
Despite his efforts to cope, Juan’s symptoms have grown increasingly unbearable. His wife has
noticed changes in him and urged him to seek help, concerned about his emotional well-being and how
it affects their family life. What brings Juan into therapy is a deep-seated fear of losing himself
completely, but also a glimmer of hope that with the right support, he can regain control over his
life and find peace. Juan’s journey towards healing begins with understanding these triggers and the
cycle they have created in his life. He hopes to learn new ways to manage his distressing memories
and avoid relying on maladaptive strategies that ultimately do more harm than good.
