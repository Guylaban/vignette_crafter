was profoundly affected by the natural disaster that occurred six months ago. The event, a
devastating tornado, not only swept through his neighborhood but also triggered memories of his
military service and bomb disposal operations. On the day of the tornado, John was at home when he
heard a sudden loud roar outside. He immediately recognized it as something dangerous. As he rushed
to secure his family, the unpredictable nature of the event left him in constant fear for their
lives. The moment the sirens began to wail and shouting could be heard from neighbors, his body
reacted before he had time to think. He found himself diving into a safe room, his heart racing as
if he were back in combat. Since then, John has been plagued by unpredictable situations that
trigger intense flashbacks and false alarms. Any loud noise or sudden shout can send him spiraling
into panic, reliving the chaos of the tornado. This unpredictability exacerbates his sense of
danger; his body reacts as though he is still under threat even when there’s no real peril. To cope
with this distress, John often turns to maladaptive strategies. He eats excessively to numb his
emotions and avoids anything that could be stressful. His need for mental distraction keeps him
constantly occupied, often working late into the night on trivial tasks. However, these behaviors
only serve to heighten his triggers. The more he isolates himself or distracts himself with food and

of the tornado is fragmented and incomplete. He finds himself pushing away the full recall before it
can settle, leaving him with only bits and pieces that feel like a puzzle without an answer. When he
does manage to remember parts of what happened, intense rage surges through him, fueling his false
alarms and making him react as though danger is imminent. The cycle tightens further when John
recalls the event, igniting both his sense of threat and his triggers. The fragmented memories and
intense emotions trigger more frequent false alarms and flashbacks, leading to a heightened state of

gatherings, pushing himself deeper into isolation. Despite these efforts to manage his distress,
John’s life has become increasingly difficult. His work as a bomb disposal technician requires him
to be in unpredictable and potentially dangerous situations daily. The unpredictability of his job
now triggers intense reactions that interfere with his ability to perform tasks safely and
effectively. His wife notices the changes too; he is often distant and irritable, struggling to
connect emotionally. What finally brought John to seek help was a near-miss incident at work where
his heightened state of arousal led him to misjudge a situation critically. He realized that if he
didn’t get help soon, he would either lose his job or worse, put himself and others in grave danger.

reacting out of control. He dreams of a day when he can return to work without fear, and more
importantly, connect with his family in a meaningful way again.
