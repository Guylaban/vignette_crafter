She remembers the sound first. Not the rain, but the silence just before the water came. She was

water, and pulled him up. He was coughing, crying. She carried him the rest of the way, her arms
shaking long after they reached the church on the hill.

That night, huddled with hundreds of others, she watched the water swallow her home. Her stove, her
photographs, her children’s schoolbooks—all gone. When the water receded days later, she walked back
to find only the concrete foundation. She never went inside what remained of the walls. She
couldn’t.

Now, she is fifty-two, working the fry station at a fast-food restaurant in a city that never
floods. She has been here for fifteen years. Every shift, the sizzle of the oil sounds like water.

noticed she flinches when the timer goes off. She makes excuses—says she is just tired. But at
night, she lies awake listening to the ceiling fan, waiting for the sound of rain that never comes.
