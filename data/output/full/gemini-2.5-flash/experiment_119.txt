powerlessness he felt as armed men stormed the facility, inflicting unspeakable harm. The images of
suffering, and his own desperate attempts to shield patients that proved futile, left an indelible
mark. What haunted him most was the stark reality that the perpetrators were never apprehended,
never held to account for their actions. He carried a deep conviction that what happened there was
profoundly unfair, a gross injustice that remained unaddressed, cementing his belief that the people
responsible had faced no consequences. This profound sense of injustice and lack of accountability
festered within him, leading him to believe he couldn't rely on himself to protect others or even
himself in the face of such overwhelming evil.

Upon returning to the States, these deeply held beliefs about the world’s inherent unfairness and
the lack of consequences for those who inflict harm began to manifest in his daily life. His
conviction that the people responsible had faced no consequences and that what happened to him was
deeply unfair fueled a constant, low-level hum of anxiety that quickly escalated into a pervasive
sense of threat. He found himself feeling jumpy or easily startled by everyday noises, like a car
backfiring or a sudden notification sound from his work computer. This heightened state of
hypervigilance meant he was perpetually superalert, watchful, and on guard, his senses constantly
scanning for danger even in benign environments.

detach himself from the memories of the event whenever they threatened to surface, creating a mental

altogether, often burying himself in his work as a social media manager, scrolling through endless
feeds, or taking on extra projects to keep his mind occupied. This active avoidance, however, had a
paradoxical effect; while it offered temporary relief, it also prevented any genuine processing of

into a compulsive need to constantly scan his environment for signs of threat, whether he was in a
busy coffee shop or a quiet meeting room at his office.

Unfortunately, these strategies, intended to protect him, only served to tighten the grip of his
distress. The very act of detaching himself from the memories and avoiding thinking about the event
meant the trauma remained raw and unresolved, leaving him vulnerable. His persistent checking of his
surroundings, while a maladaptive strategy to manage his fear, inadvertently reinforced his belief
that danger was always lurking, thus intensifying his hypervigilance and making his startle response
