for Eleanor, it became a terrifying blur of rough shoves, shouted commands, and the feeling of being
utterly helpless as she was pushed against a pillar, witnessing what she perceived as unnecessary
force against both protesters and innocent bystanders.

without warning. Just last week, seeing a crowded place, specifically the hotel’s lobby during a
busy check-in, triggered a sudden, vivid memory of the police rushing through, their boots thudding
on the marble floor. This memory was so clear and detailed, almost a replay, that it brought back
the same terror she had felt at the time, her heart pounding as if she were still pressed against
that cold pillar. These moments of intense recall then fueled her growing conviction that the people

deepening her sense of betrayal.

triggers. A flash of a police siren outside her office window, or even a fleeting mental image of

intrusive thoughts were not just distressing; they actively reinforced her belief that she had
permanently changed for the worse, that the capable, confident Eleanor she once knew was gone,
replaced by someone perpetually on edge. This gnawing sense of identity loss made it harder to shake
off the vivid memories, as each recall seemed to confirm that her old self was truly lost.

powerful trigger in itself, made her feel with increasing certainty that she no longer knew herself,
that the woman who had calmly navigated countless crises in her hotel was irrevocably broken. And

experienced, they further solidified her belief that the institutions meant to serve and protect had
failed her utterly, leaving her vulnerable and exposed. This constant loop – the memories feeding
her negative appraisals, and those appraisals in turn making the memories more unbearable – had
become a self-reinforcing cycle.

avoided the hotel’s main entrance during peak hours, preferring the quiet of her office, a subtle

attempt to escape the crowded places that threatened to unleash her terror. She knew she couldn’t
continue this way, the constant vigilance exhausting her. The fear that she was truly permanently
damaged, that this was simply her new reality, was what finally brought her to seek help, hoping
against hope that she might, somehow, rediscover the woman she used to be.
