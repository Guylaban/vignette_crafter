one day.

The death comes without warning on an ordinary afternoon. He is in class, half listening, half
writing a line of dialogue in the margin of his notebook, when his phone begins vibrating over and

buying coffee and crossing streets.

when the doctor speaks, a sound he says seems to split the air in two. In the days after, the
apartment fills with relatives, prayer, food, shoes at the door. He moves through it as if
submerged. Everyone tells stories, everyone cries, everyone repeats that it was God’s will, that
