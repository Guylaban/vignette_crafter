the calm precision that has shaped most of her adult life. Then the alarms begin. At first there is

performs. She flies the aircraft flawlessly. It is only after landing, when she takes off her
helmet, that her hands begin to shake.

In the days afterward, she does not have the luxury of falling apart. There are investigations,
briefings, memorials, and revised security procedures. She tells herself that staying busy is the
same as coping. People around her praise her professionalism. She accepts it because she does not
know what else to do. At night, though, she lies awake in the dark of her quarters with her body
still braced for impact. If she sleeps, she wakes with her heart racing, hearing the blast again or
smelling jet fuel mixed with smoke. She starts leaving the television off because footage of
attacks, sirens, or even crowded city scenes can suddenly flood her with the same electric panic.
