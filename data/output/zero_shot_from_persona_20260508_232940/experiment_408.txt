She enlisted at nineteen, wanting structure, a paycheck, and a way out of a home life she describes
as “fine, but not really mine.” In the service, she liked the clarity of expectations. She says she

joked to manage the stress. Nobody relaxed.

The event she returns to most happened during what had started as an ordinary convoy. She remembers

trying to move quickly while her body felt slow and unreal. She remembers seeing one of the soldiers
she knew well on the ground, badly injured, and being unable to reconcile that image with the person
she had spoken with earlier that morning. She still gets caught on the detail that his glove had
come off. In her mind, that detail is as vivid as the explosion itself.

She tells herself now that she did what she was trained to do. Other people have told her that too.
But what stayed with her was not only terror; it was the sense that danger could erupt at any

back in order to keep functioning.
