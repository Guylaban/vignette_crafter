### Clinical Vignette for Mr. Hadi Mr. Hadi, a 56-year-old Middle Eastern man, has been experiencing
significant distress following an incident of police violence that occurred several months ago. As a
Social Media Manager, his daily routine is intricately tied to his work environment and the digital
world he navigates. The traumatic event began when Mr. Hadi was stopped by the police during a walk
in his neighborhood. He had come to believe that people are not what they seem, and this belief
intensified during the interaction with the officers. They accused him of something minor but used
aggressive tactics, escalating quickly into an intense confrontation. During this encounter, Mr.
Hadi felt cornered and powerless. The taste he had while waiting for the police officer to approach
was a bitter one, and now whenever he recalls the event, he can vividly taste that bitterness again.
This sensory memory has made it difficult for him to engage fully in his daily life without being
abruptly pulled back into that moment of fear and helplessness. Since then, Mr. Hadi has adopted
several maladaptive strategies to cope with the overwhelming emotions. He avoids anything that could
cause negative or positive feelings, as he finds himself pushing away memories before they can be
fully processed. This avoidance extends to his media consumption; he has stopped watching TV
programs or films and reading about similar events because these reminders bring back intense rage
when he recalls the event. His workplace environment is now a place where he constantly scans for
signs of threat. As a Social Media Manager, Mr. Hadi often finds himself checking every corner of
the office space, always identifying escape routes before feeling comfortable anywhere. This hyper-
vigilance has made it challenging to focus on his work and interact with colleagues without feeling
like he needs to be constantly alert. Mr. Hadi had come to believe that the world is a dangerous
place. He felt violated by the actions of the officers, believing that he acted in ways that go
against who he truly is. This internal conflict has led him to feel intense distrust towards others.
Even though his wife is supportive and loving, there are moments when he finds himself questioning
her intentions, which has caused strain in their relationship. Unable to relax even when he knows he
is in a safe place, Mr. Hadi's mind races with thoughts of potential threats around every corner.
This constant state of alertness has left him exhausted, both mentally and physically. He struggles
to sleep at night because the memories keep resurfacing, leaving him feeling trapped by his own
fears. What finally brought Mr. Hadi to seek help was a culmination of these symptoms affecting
nearly all aspects of his life. His work performance has declined due to his inability to focus, and
he has become isolated from friends and family. He is most afraid that the world will continue to be
dangerous, and he will never feel safe again. However, there is also hope in him, a small but
persistent belief that with help, he can regain some sense of control over his life. In therapy, Mr.
Hadi hopes to find ways to manage his intense emotions and develop coping strategies that allow him
to live without constant fear. He wants to learn how to trust others again and process the memories
that haunt him, so he can return to a life where he feels more in control of his own experiences.
