explosions rocked the building, and chaos erupted around her. The sirens of ambulances and police
cars soon joined the cacophony, adding to the overwhelming sensory overload. Since then, Leela has
become acutely sensitive to auditory triggers such as sirens, which immediately transport her back
to that harrowing day. Every time she hears a siren, it feels like the attack is happening all over
again, and she is once more surrounded by fear and uncertainty. The power imbalance she experienced
during the attack—feeling helpless against an invisible threat—has left her wary of situations where
someone else holds authority or control. The smell of smoke also triggers intense flashbacks for
Leela. Whenever she catches a whiff of burning wood or the acrid scent of fire, it brings back
memories of the smoldering debris and the suffocating smoke that filled the air during the attack.
The olfactory trigger is particularly potent because it evokes not just visual but also tactile
sensations—feeling the heat and choking on the smoke. In her daily life as a retired dentist, Leela
has developed several avoidance strategies to cope with these triggers. She avoids going near the
community center where the event took place, even for routine appointments or social gatherings.

isolated and disconnected from the community she once loved. Leela’s constant scanning of her
environment for signs of threat has become a habitual behavior. Whether she is walking down the

lurks nearby. This hyper-vigilance is exhausting, but it also gives her a sense of control in an
otherwise unpredictable world. Her preference for staying near exits reflects her deep-seated fear
of being trapped or unable to escape if another attack were to occur. At social gatherings and even
during professional events at the dental clinic she once worked at, Leela always positions herself

strained relationships with friends and colleagues who find her constant vigilance unsettling.
Despite these coping mechanisms, Leela’s life has been significantly impacted. She feels
increasingly isolated from her community and struggles with feelings of guilt for not being able to
help during the attack. The avoidance behaviors have also affected her ability to engage in
activities she once enjoyed, such as attending cultural festivals or social gatherings. What finally
brought Leela to seek help was a recent incident where she heard sirens while walking down the
street. She felt so overwhelmed that she had to return home and lock herself in her room for hours
