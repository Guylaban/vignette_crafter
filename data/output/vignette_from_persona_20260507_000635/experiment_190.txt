Clinical Vignette John, a 74-year-old Oceania man, has lived most of his life in New York City. He
is divorced and works as an editor for a small publishing house. John's current distress stems from
the physical abuse he endured at the hands of his former romantic partner. The traumatic event
occurred years ago but continues to haunt him, affecting nearly every aspect of his daily life.
John’s relationship with his ex-partner was marked by escalating violence and criticism. He vividly
recalls a particularly brutal incident that stands out as a defining moment in this abusive cycle.
One evening, after receiving critical feedback at work from a colleague, John came home to find his
partner waiting. The partner lashed out, physically attacking him while continuously criticizing and
belittling him. This experience has left John with vivid snapshot memories—each moment of the
assault is seared into his mind as distinct visual images. John had come to believe that these
moments felt like they belonged to someone else rather than himself. When he remembers the event, a
wave of intense rage surges through him, which only adds to his distress. Despite this anger, John
often feels dehumanized and disconnected from his own emotions. He believes that he will never be
able to feel normal again and that these experiences have fundamentally altered who he is as a
person. To cope with the overwhelming memories and feelings of vulnerability, John has adopted
several maladaptive strategies over time. He distracts himself whenever strong emotions begin to
surface, often immersing himself in work or other tasks. This distraction helps him avoid
confronting his feelings directly but has also led to increased stress at work as he tends to lose
focus on projects that require emotional engagement. John’s avoidance behaviors extend beyond
emotional suppression; he goes out of his way to avoid any reminders of the perpetrator, including
places they frequented together and even certain people who resemble the offender. This hyper-
vigilance has affected his social life significantly; he avoids gatherings or situations where he
might encounter individuals similar in appearance to his former partner. In his professional
environment as an editor, John’s triggers are particularly potent. Being criticized or corrected at
work can quickly trigger memories of the abuse and lead to intense emotional distress. This has
strained his relationships with colleagues, who often find him overly reactive or withdrawn during
meetings and reviews. John’s belief that he is fundamentally flawed due to the abuse has led him to
doubt his own worth and abilities. He sees himself as responsible for the event and carries a deep
sense of character blame, which further isolates him from others. This internalized narrative has
eroded his self-esteem and left him feeling increasingly isolated. The combination of these factors
has finally pushed John to seek help after years of suffering in silence. What frightens him most is
the fear that he will never fully recover from this trauma and that it will continue to define his
life. He hopes for a way to reclaim some sense of normalcy and reconnect with his emotions without
being overwhelmed by memories and rage. John’s current state reflects a complex interplay between
past traumas and present coping mechanisms, illustrating the profound impact of his experiences on
his daily functioning and emotional well-being. His journey toward healing is marked by the need for
validation and support to navigate through the lingering effects of trauma.
