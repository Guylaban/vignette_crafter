aftermath of a traumatic event that occurred over five years ago. Martha’s husband, John, had fallen
asleep at the wheel while driving home from a weekend trip. The accident was devastating: the car
crashed into a tree on a dark rural road, and although she managed to escape with minor injuries,
her husband did not survive. The traumatic event has left deep scars that continue to affect her
daily life. Martha vividly recalls the moment when John's car veered off the road and slammed into
the tree. She remembers the crunch of metal, the smell of alcohol still lingering in the air from
their trip, and the sound of glass shattering as the headlights exploded. The auditory and visual
memories are so clear that she often relives those moments, especially during quiet nights or when
driving herself. One day, while on duty, Martha had to respond to a car accident scene where the
smell of alcohol was overwhelming. As she approached the vehicle, her heart raced, and she found it
hard to breathe, as if she were back at that fateful night with John. The olfactory trigger brought
back the vivid images of the crash, and for a moment, she felt like she couldn’t catch her breath.
This experience marked the beginning of an intense fear of driving and being near any car accident
scenes. The trauma also led Martha to isolate herself from conversations about accidents or
incidents involving vehicles. She avoided news reports, social gatherings where such topics might
arise, and even discussions with colleagues at work. Her avoidance became so pronounced that she
began to dread attending community events or meetings where she feared the topic of car crashes
would come up. Despite her best efforts to avoid these triggers, Martha found herself frequently
haunted by memories of John’s accident. She often heard his last words ringing in her head and saw
flashes of the aftermath. These intrusive thoughts and images were so vivid that they disrupted her
sleep and left her feeling anxious throughout the day. The clarity and detail of these memories made
it difficult for her to move past the event. Martha's self-blame and feelings of helplessness grew
as she grappled with her role in the accident. She constantly replayed scenarios where she might
have prevented John from driving or ensured he didn't fall asleep at the wheel. The guilt and regret
were overwhelming, and she felt a deep sense that she had failed to protect him. These negative
appraisals led her to question her own competence as both a police officer and a spouse, further
exacerbating her distress. As Martha’s symptoms intensified over time, her relationship with her
adult children also became strained. They noticed her withdrawal from family gatherings and social
activities. She stopped attending community events she once enjoyed and even avoided driving alone
or in groups that involved transportation. Her husband's death had turned into a shadow that loomed
over every aspect of her life. Despite her efforts to cope, Martha’s symptoms finally reached a
breaking point when she experienced a severe panic attack while shopping at the local supermarket.
The sound of a car alarm going off triggered intense flashbacks and overwhelming anxiety, leaving
her feeling incapacitated and unable to continue her daily tasks. This incident made it clear that
she needed help. Martha has now come to seek therapy in hopes of finding ways to manage her symptoms
and regain control over her life. What frightens her most is the possibility of reliving the
traumatic event and losing her sense of self-worth entirely. She is hopeful that with professional
support, she can learn to navigate her memories and triggers without being overwhelmed by fear and
guilt. In this journey towards healing, Martha’s goal is clear: to rebuild a life where she can face
the past without being consumed by it and find peace within herself once again.
