wrong, and then the awful realization that someone had been hurt badly because of what had happened
on her watch. When she tried to remember it later, the event did not unfold as a sequence so much as
a few frozen moments, each one like a photograph held too close to her face. At times, fragments of
it would appear without warning and vanish just as quickly, leaving her with a jolt of terror and a
pounding sense that she was back there again. When she thought about that night, the fear felt
immediate and bodily, as if the same dread had been dropped into her chest all over again.

In the days and weeks that followed, Megan returned to work because that was what she knew how to
do, but she did so with an edge to her vigilance that had not been there before. In the ambulance
bay, on the road, and later in the station, she found herself constantly scanning, listening, and
waiting for something else to go wrong. She was especially uneasy at night, when the city felt less
contained and every siren, footstep, or sudden noise seemed to carry more weight than it should.

had to stay more on guard, as if safety had become conditional and fragile. She noticed how

of needing to double-check herself, especially in situations that reminded her of that night.

too shameful. She had begun to rely on alcohol after shifts and, at times, on medication or other

day.

Over time, her world narrowed in subtle but real ways. She remained married and kept working, but
