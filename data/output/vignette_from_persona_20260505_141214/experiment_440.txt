a young adult, and at 56 he now lives alone in the wake of a divorce that has left his evenings
especially quiet. He has spent much of his adult life in law enforcement and currently works as a

become increasingly aware that his vigilance has long since outlived the job itself. What he first

sharpness of rage in his chest, and the sense that his mind would go blank when he tried to tell
anyone exactly what had happened. Even now, when he attempts to explain those years, he loses the
thread and cannot finish, as if the story breaks apart before it can reach the end.

something scorches in the break room at work or a pan is left too long on a stove, he can feel his
body react before he has time to think. The odor does not merely remind him of the past; it can make
the full memory return without warning, complete and immediate, as though he is once again in the
same house and standing on the same fearful ground. He is also unsettled by people who resemble the
victims from cases he has handled, particularly if they share a certain look that his mind has
linked to vulnerability and injury. He notices their faces in hallways, interview rooms, and public
spaces, and the sight can pull his attention sharply out of the present. He has also become wary of
positive emotions in a way he finds difficult to explain to others. Moments of warmth, laughter, or
ease can leave him feeling uneasy and exposed, as if a calm atmosphere is only the prelude to
something going wrong.

Over the years, Mark has responded by narrowing his world. He avoids wearing clothes similar to
those he associates with the time and circumstances he finds himself trying not to think about,
preferring garments that feel different enough to keep him from being reminded. He detaches himself
from memories when they start to surface, forcing himself away from them as soon as he senses their
approach. He has also been avoiding other people more broadly, declining social invitations and

for him to rebuild ordinary companionship. At the office, he can function well enough in the role,
but he is often alone at lunch and tends to leave quickly once the day is done.

happened “proved the world doesn’t work the way people say it does,” and that his beliefs about how
things are supposed to work have been completely destroyed. He also sees his own reactions with
harsh self-judgment, interpreting the years after the abuse as evidence that he is a lousy coper.

the experience — his muscles tighten, his heart races, and he feels on guard before he can name why.

during routine detective work. Recently, the accumulation of these experiences has worn him down

memories will keep breaking into the present without warning, and what he hopes for, though he says
it carefully, is some way to sleep without staying on guard and to move through the world without
feeling as though it has been permanently shattered.
