hide his irritation.

the unpredictable passengers, the stops and starts, and the inability to leave quickly all brought
back the helplessness he felt on the day of the attack. He began choosing other routes or arranging
his schedule so he could avoid buses entirely, even when that meant extra time and inconvenience.

world had quietly become smaller, organized around what he could manage to avoid.

especially troubling for someone whose profession depended on trust, calm judgment, and constant
contact with others. Yet he kept these fears largely to himself, and for a time he continued

collapse but the growing discomfort of realizing that his life had become organized around vigilance
and mistrust. He wanted relief, but what he feared most was that the attack had permanently changed
how he understood the world and everyone in it.
