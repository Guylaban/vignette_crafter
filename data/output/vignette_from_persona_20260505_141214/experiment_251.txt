surgeon, a role that had trained him to stay composed in the face of blood, urgency, and other
people’s fear. Until the accident, he had assumed that experience had made him steady in ways that
would hold up outside the hospital as well. The trauma occurred years into his career, when a
routine drive home ended in a violent collision that left him pinned in a wrecked car after another
vehicle struck his at speed. What stayed with him most was not only the shock of impact, but the
sickening tactile detail of it: the harsh, unfamiliar texture of twisted metal and torn upholstery
pressing against his hands as he struggled to orient himself. Even now, that texture seemed to have
a strange power over him; if his fingers brushed against something rough, frayed, or sharply ridged
in just the wrong way, he could feel a jolt of recognition that seemed to pull him back into the
crushed interior of the car. In the months that followed, Javier found that certain images arrived
with almost intrusive force, especially the sight of cars hit by accident. Passing a crumpled
vehicle on the roadside or seeing a damaged car in a parking lot could seize his attention so
completely that he felt temporarily unable to look anywhere else.

From the beginning, the memory of the event had not felt like an ordinary recollection to him.
Javier described it as more real than normal, vivid in a way that made other memories seem faded and
distant by comparison. He could recall not just the sequence of events, but the angle of light, the
pressure in his chest, the smell inside the car, and the abrupt terror of realizing he was trapped.
Yet when he tried to think through the whole incident calmly, he often found himself pushing the
memory away before he could fully recall it. He would sense the first few seconds of the crash, then
stop himself, as if there were a limit to how much of it he could safely let into awareness. At
other times, the memory forced its way into his mind when he did not want it there. This happened
without warning while he was speaking with colleagues, waiting in line, or sitting quietly at home
with his wife. He would abruptly find himself back in the wreck, alert to the same helplessness he
had felt then, his attention narrowed around the event despite his effort to remain in the present.

As the years passed, Javier began shaping his life around the possibility of being pulled back into
that state. He gave up pleasant activities that had once made weekends feel restorative, gradually
declining dinners out, drives with his wife, and small trips that involved time in a car. He told
himself that these choices were practical and not worth dwelling on, but he also knew he was
avoiding the risk of hearing, seeing, or even smelling something that might bring the accident to
mind. He also avoided physical stress. Activities that put his body under strain became increasingly

profession that already demanded long hours and physical endurance, this was not easy to sustain. At

his body or move quickly for extended periods. He noticed that these decisions narrowed his life in
quiet but consequential ways. His wife urged him to join her in things they had once enjoyed
together, but he often declined, and over time she had come to see him as less present, more
cautious, and harder to persuade.

responsibilities, because once he allowed himself to linger on the event, he feared it would become
harder to shut out. Yet the effort to keep away from the memory was increasingly difficult. The
sight of a damaged car on his commute, a conversation about traffic injuries, or the simple feel of

the time he sought help, he was exhausted by the amount of energy it took to manage his reactions
and by the sense that he had gradually organized his life around what he could no longer bear to
revisit. What finally brought Javier into treatment was the realization that he was losing too much

that would not stay put. More than anything, he hoped to be able to think about the accident without
being overwhelmed by its vividness, and to reclaim parts of ordinary life without constantly bracing
himself for the next unwanted return of the crash.
