followed, Marisol found herself thinking back over the event again and again, turning it over in her
mind as if some overlooked detail might explain how it had happened or how it could have been
stopped. She had come to believe that the assault happened because of the way she acted,
scrutinizing her own choices with a harsh, self-directed certainty, and she also carried a deep
sense of shame about what had happened to her body.

loaded with danger. A friendly hug from a colleague, a hand on her shoulder from an acquaintance, or
even being expected to stand close during a social greeting could make her stiffen before she could
stop herself. Sexual situations, even when they were only implied in conversation or portrayed on

the feeling of being exposed became too much. Being alone with men became especially difficult. If a
repairman, neighbor, or former coworker came by, she arranged to keep a door open, to have a second

as dramatic, only necessary, as if caution had become the price of getting through the day.

Marisol’s world gradually narrowed around the need to feel prepared. Before entering a restaurant,

leave quickly if needed. Open parking lots, broad sidewalks, and exposed outdoor spaces made her

walls, corners, or some kind of shelter. At work, when she was still traveling for environmental

harm could come from someone no one would suspect.

that danger was already on its way.
