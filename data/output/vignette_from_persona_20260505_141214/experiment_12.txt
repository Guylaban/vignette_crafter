part was not just the room itself but the certainty, in those moments, that she had no options.

Afterward, Marisol found herself building habits around keeping the event at bay. At times she told
herself the whole thing did not really happen or that it had not been as bad as it felt, as if
reducing it in her mind might make it less present. More often, though, she went over and over the
details, trying to make sense of every turn of the event, especially the points at which she
believed she should have done something differently. She dwelled on what she had done wrong,
replaying her decisions with a harshness that could go on for hours. She had come to believe that
there was something fundamentally unsafe about people in ways she could not predict, and she would
say, with a tired certainty, that you can never know who will harm you. That belief sat beside a
different one she spoke about with sadness: she used to be a happy person, she said, but now she was
always miserable, and at times she felt dead inside, as if part of her had gone numb and stayed that
way.

project manager, she still had to attend meetings, review plans, and move through office buildings,
but she became intensely uneasy if she was in a locked or confined room. She would notice exits

home, where one might expect comfort, she did not fully relax. She remained on guard even inside her

tense, as though danger had arrived without warning. She recognized that these were false alarms,
yet that knowledge did not stop her body from responding as if she were again in the place where she
had been trapped.

Marisol also noticed how her neck pain could pull the past into the present. When the discomfort
flared, she would immediately think of being held captive, and the pain itself seemed to carry the
memory of how tense and powerless she had felt then. That sensation made it harder for her to
settle, especially at home after work, when she wanted to rest but could not fully let herself. Over

uncertainty, was the chance to feel safe enough to stop bracing for the next danger.
