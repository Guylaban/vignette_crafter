rhythm of her days. One evening, as she was closing the workshop after dark, a man forced his way in
and assaulted her. What stayed with Amina was not a full story that she could put into words with
ease, but a sequence of vivid images: the doorway, the sudden closeness of the stranger, her own

it, she returned to pictures rather than a verbal account, as though the event remained lodged in
her mind as a series of flashes.

had drifted, or when she was standing at the sink after supper. At those moments she could feel
herself pulled back into the workshop so sharply that, for a few seconds, she was certain it was
happening again right then. She described those moments as if the room around her disappeared and

memory came as images, it did not arrive as a calm recollection but as a sudden, intrusive picture

Since then, Amina had come to believe that the world was a dangerous place. That belief settled into
