pull her back into the feeling that she was once again on the edge of something harmful. What
unsettled her most, however, was smell: if she caught a hospital-like antiseptic odor, her body
would react before she could tell herself she was safe. That particular scent seemed to fold the
present into the moment of exposure, and she would become abruptly tense, convinced in that instant
that something in the air was wrong.

Leila described that after the incident she became much more watchful about her surroundings,

released.

first been exposed and where reminders could appear without warning. She still managed her

might be in the air, what might suddenly sound loud, and where she might be caught off balance. The
