something in her shut the door before the whole thing could come into view. At times, one detail

unfolding again in the room with her. She said there were moments when she knew, intellectually,

with her hands when these moments hit, not to hide from another person, but to block out the rush of
what was coming back.

In the months that followed, Marisol found herself responding to ordinary situations with a kind of
internal alarm she could not switch off. Unexpected loud sounds made her jump and left her on high
alert, her attention snapping toward whatever had made the noise. She said she constantly scanned

felt tense and ready to react before she had decided anything was wrong. At work, this showed itself

protected her.

rigid. These efforts sometimes helped her stay in place, but they also left her drained and

them, and that she no longer trusted the institutions and people who had been supposed to keep her
safe. What she hoped for, even if only cautiously, was to feel more like herself again and to stop
living as though danger were waiting in every sound, every smell, and every room.
