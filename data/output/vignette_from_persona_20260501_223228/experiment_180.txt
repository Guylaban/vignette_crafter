most was not only the pain but the conviction, formed in those moments on the pavement, that he
could be overpowered without warning and that nobody nearby would stop it.

his heart racing and a felt sense that someone was there, just outside his vision. Darkness carried
him back to the roadside before he had time to remind himself where he was. Similar cars also caught
him off guard. A sedan with the same silhouette as the patrol car, especially in his rearview mirror

places were almost as bad. In a packed grocery line or on a busy sidewalk, he felt trapped in the
same way he had on the ground with people around him and no room to move.

Those moments shaped what he did to get through the day. Daniel started drinking in the evenings
because it was the quickest way he knew to blunt what nighttime did to him; if he could get himself

recklessness than desperation to make himself unconscious. When he had to go out, he became vigilant

thing happened if a car resembling the patrol vehicle rolled by while he was walking; his hands came
up before he had thought it through.

job. More quietly, he admitted that what he wanted most was to be able to wake up in the dark and
know, immediately and fully, that he was safe.
