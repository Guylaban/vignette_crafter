put them into a narrative, language seemed to fail him.

his mind replayed the threatening acoustics before anything else. At other times there was no
picture at all. He would suddenly be flooded with the same terror and helplessness he had felt then,
his chest tightening and his body bracing, without any clear scene in his mind. What frightened him
was not only the memory of being trapped, but the sense that it could seize him without warning and
without a story he could tell himself about it.

Those qualities of the memory shaped the way he began living afterward. Because trying to think
about the captivity brought back those sounds in his head and, just as often, a wave of raw fear
with no image attached, Kwame worked hard to push the whole thing away. He told himself not to go
there mentally, cut off conversations if anyone came close to the subject, and kept himself busy
rather than risk an unguarded moment. He also began avoiding being outside after dark. Night carried
too much of the feeling of that confinement and too much uncertainty about who might be nearby, so

finding words that blocks his account of the trauma also leaves him with no easy way to justify why
ordinary parts of the job now feel intolerable.

certain that if he lets his guard down he will be overwhelmed by the sounds in his head or by that
sudden, image-less surge of fear. So he keeps trying not to think, not to talk, not to be touched,

touch him without feeling as if he were back there.
