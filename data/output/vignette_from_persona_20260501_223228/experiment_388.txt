face coming down the hall carried the possibility of bad news.

same sense of imminent danger. At work, if two coworkers in the operations room started arguing over

and she felt a jolt of alarm as if she were back outside her son’s hospital room waiting for someone
to tell her he might not make it. It was similar when people connected to that period contacted her

the world feel dangerous again, and she became intensely watchful even after the moment had passed.

That sense of danger no longer stays confined to obvious reminders. Moana describes feeling on guard
in her own apartment, as if bad news could arrive through the door at any moment. If someone moves

a stranger shifting in the waiting room—she startles hard, heart racing, already preparing herself
for something awful before she can orient to what is happening. Living in this keyed-up state has
made the range of things that set her off feel broader and more immediate. Because she is already
scanning for signs of trouble, an argument across the office floor catches her instantly, and the

of gloves or the opening of a tray can seem like confirmation that she is back in the same helpless,
dangerous place.

voices and abrupt motion sends her into the same alarmed state. At home, she notices herself
listening for changes in sound and checking on her son repeatedly even on quiet evenings. She knows
he is currently safe, but she has come to believe that letting her guard down would be reckless, as
though vigilance is the only thing standing between her family and another medical crisis. That
constant readiness leaves her exhausted, and the exhaustion seems to make her even more reactive
when reminders appear.

Moana finally sought help after nearly leaping out of her chair during a routine pediatric follow-up

could not stop trembling in the car afterward. What frightened her most was how small the trigger
had become: not an emergency, just a familiar medical room and one quick movement. She worries that
she is teaching her son that the world is unsafe and that she will never feel calm, even in her own
home. At the same time, she is hopeful in a guarded way. She wants to be able to sit through an

as if his life is in danger all over again.
