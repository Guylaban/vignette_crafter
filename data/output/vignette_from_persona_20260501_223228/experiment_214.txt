coming toward him. Even now, when he tries to recall it, he says the fear arrives at full strength,
and his shoulders pull in and his torso stiffens as though he is back in that position on the floor.

older man sitting in the office trying to explain it.

memories. Because remembering brought back the same terror and the same bodily sense of being
pinned, he stopped allowing himself to feel angry at all; anger seemed too close to the argument
that had preceded the assault, too likely to pull him back into it. He also became careful about
avoiding reminders, especially darkness, and more generally started steering away from anything that
might feel stressful. He described this not as cowardice but as common sense: if stress, conflict,
or certain situations could make the memory come alive in his body, then it felt safer to stay
quiet, keep the peace, and not go near anything that might stir it up.

knows he is safe. At the house where he works, this has become painfully obvious. If one of the
children runs past him unexpectedly or darts into his side vision, he reacts with a sharp jolt,
sometimes stepping back or throwing up an arm before he can stop himself. Sudden movement nearby
feels to him like threat before his mind has time to catch up. He is easily startled by small noises
and quick gestures, and because his body remains so tense, ordinary days never quite feel ordinary.

That constant sense of danger has, in turn, narrowed his life further. Since he feels jumpy and
unable to settle, Roberto tries to manage himself by avoiding circumstances that might leave him
exposed or overwhelmed. He keeps lights on in rooms even during the day if they seem dim, hesitates
to take the children into darker play areas, and avoids outings that seem unpredictable or noisy. He
has become intensely cautious about stress of any kind; even minor disagreements with the parents he
works for are swallowed immediately. He is especially determined never to show anger. In his mind,
if he lets himself feel it, his body will surge into the same state of alarm and he will lose the
little control he has.

Yet these strategies have not quieted the past. The more Roberto pushes anger down and arranges his
days to avoid reminders and stress, the more strange and intrusive the memory has become. Because he
does not speak about what happened and cuts himself off whenever irritation or resentment arises,

the hallway comes back with a force that feels borrowed and alien, and his body folds inward before
he has even fully pictured why. Avoiding darkness helps him for a moment, but it also keeps darkness
charged; suppressing anger spares him the feeling in the short term, but the memory returns
untouched, still carrying terror.

hopes for, quietly, is to be able to stand in a dark room, feel a stir of anger or stress, and still
know that he is here now and no longer there.
