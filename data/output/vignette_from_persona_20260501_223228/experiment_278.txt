or cornered. What remains most painful to Robert is not only the violence itself but the fact that

trusted, and he still speaks with bitterness about how the adults around him never stopped it and
how the person responsible faced no real consequences.

feels more vivid than whatever is in front of him, almost more real than ordinary life. He can
sometimes smell again what he smelled then, and those sensory fragments land with such force that
talking to strangers, hearing sirens outside, or catching alcohol on someone’s breath can feel

they confirm his sense that the original betrayal is still alive inside him and that the people who
did this escaped accountability.

Because the memories come in these sensory bursts, Robert has spent years trying not to leave his

has become an enemy, reacting as though danger is present even when he is sitting safely in his
