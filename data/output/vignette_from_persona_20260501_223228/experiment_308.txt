the months that followed it was often that particular sense of exposure that returned first.

fully thought why. The burnt smell is especially potent. When something singes in the hospital break
room toaster, when there is a faint electrical smell from old equipment, or when a neighbor is

posture, hands, and tone for danger. At home she listens for the smallest sound that might mean
someone is entering; at work she catches herself monitoring footsteps in the corridor outside a

a passing sensation for Mai. It quickly becomes a signal to her that she may be in danger again, and
her body reacts as if the assault is about to recur.

That state of watchfulness now shapes how she moves through nearly every day, and it has made the
world feel full of reminders. Because she studies faces so closely, a tired man in the emergency
entrance with a neutral expression can start to look menacing; because she is always listening for
entry, the rattle of a loose doorknob at home can seize her attention for hours. Her physical
reactions are so strong that once they start, they seem to confirm her fear that something is wrong.

unsafe, but that she is becoming someone who is always braced to be entered upon, watched, or
overpowered. She is hoping for a way to return to her work and her home without constantly
listening, scanning, and waiting for the next sign.
