where long hours and careful attention were prized. The trauma began not as a single dramatic
assault but as a pattern that gradually closed around her. A man she had briefly spoken to several

still and watch her. Once, as she left work after staying late to finish audit documentation, she

better than she did. After that, ordinary parts of the city—an elevator opening into the lobby,
footsteps behind her on the sidewalk, someone lingering too long near the entrance of her
building—no longer felt neutral. They felt like proof that nowhere was protected.

safety depended on never relaxing. What frightened her most was not only that he had followed her,

convinced she had to be on guard all the time. Walking to work, she scanned reflections in windows
and checked who was behind her. At the office, where part of her job involved moving between
conference rooms and records areas, she kept track of exits and noticed every unfamiliar face in the
reception area. Even when she was seated at her desk reviewing spreadsheets, some part of her
attention stayed fixed on whether someone might be outside the glass doors. Alongside that
vigilance, her view of her future darkened. She had started adulthood expecting independence and

ordinary confidence of being young and building a career had been taken from her and not returned.

any lapse could invite more harm, she started avoiding going out except when it seemed absolutely
necessary. She took the most direct route between work and home and cut out errands that were not

fear had reorganized her life, she might not have to keep punishing herself just to feel prepared.
