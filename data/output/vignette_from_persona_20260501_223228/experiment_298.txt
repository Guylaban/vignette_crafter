the kind of woman this could happen to. When her heart raced or her stomach dropped unexpectedly,
she did not read those reactions as ordinary fear responses but as proof that her body had become an
enemy, still acting as if danger were present. Those beliefs colored the way she remembered the
assault. Each attempt to think it through became less like recalling an event and more like
confronting evidence against herself; the memory collapsed into shame, and the more ashamed she
felt, the harder it was to find words or make a clear story even in her own mind.

to men who resembled the guest in age, build, grooming, or manner, and Marisol started reorganizing
her routines to avoid them whenever she could. She would ask a supervisor to handle a complaint from

corridors over the main hall if a man with similar cologne or a similar haircut was at the front
desk. She also began staying later and later at work, telling herself she was being responsible, but
privately knowing that if she kept moving—checking occupancy, covering desk problems, answering late
emails—she could postpone being alone with her thoughts. At home, when she could no longer distract
herself, she turned things over endlessly in her mind, rehearsing fantasies of revenge against the
man who assaulted her. In those hours she felt driven to imagine how to get even, as though this
mental vigilance might undo what had happened or restore something damaged in her.

Yet these strategies tightened the problem. Because Marisol spent so much energy avoiding men who

long enough for it to become more organized. When it did break through, it arrived in the same

switch off. The cost of that overcontrol became most obvious on days when a guest’s voice, cologne,
or physical resemblance caught her off guard. She would feel her body react instantly, and that
reaction would feed her conviction that she was permanently changed in some damaged way. Then she

reluctantly—that she might be able to tell what happened in words, and that doing so might loosen
her certainty that the assault revealed something rotten about who she is.
