certain something in her had been permanently altered for the worse; she no longer felt like the

superalert, watchful in a way that exhausted her. Often her body reacted before she had time to
think; her chest tightened, her shoulders braced, and she was already scanning the room before she
had consciously identified why. In those moments, the old images came back fast. A sharp reflection
in a window, the sight of a man shifting his weight near a doorway, or a heavy object left on a desk
could pull up one of those frozen visual fragments and with it the same terror, as if her nervous
system were retrieving the event ahead of her mind.

flashes of the assault made her feel that danger came suddenly and from just outside the edge of

Over time, those habits strengthened the sense that threat was everywhere. The more she scanned
rooms, tracked exits, and noticed reflective surfaces, the more potential danger she found. In any

was tutoring. Her watchfulness did not calm her; it kept her on guard. By searching constantly for
what could go wrong, she taught herself that there was always something to brace against, and her

prove to her that she is damaged and separate from other people in a lasting way.
