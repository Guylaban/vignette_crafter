most was that other people seemed able to return to normal after these moments, while he could not

naïve, almost dangerous.

learned how quickly ordinary life could turn into blood on the tracks, it no longer made sense to
believe in safety just because nothing was happening at that exact moment. His nights became short

more dangerous.

reassure. He lingered over routine safety checks and kept his eyes moving constantly when passengers
boarded. On especially cold mornings he was tense before the shift had properly begun, and if there
was any visible injury in the crowd, however minor, he struggled to recover his composure. The job

repeat itself at any time. He remained single and said little socially about what had happened, but
he became more inward, spending his off-hours trying to stay in controlled environments where

wanted to know whether he could stop feeling on guard every minute and whether it was possible to
believe, even briefly, that he and the people around him were actually safe.
