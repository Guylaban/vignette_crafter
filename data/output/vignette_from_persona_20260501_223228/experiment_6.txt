her was not only the fear in those moments but the sense that the person causing the harm never
truly answered for it. He was still around for a long time, and Marisol had come to believe that bad
things could happen right in front of her and nothing would intervene.

As she got older, the memories did not settle into the past. They came back with a vividness that
felt “more real than regular memories,” as if the kitchen were opening around her again instead of
being remembered. If she let herself get close to the scene, she could catch the same stale smell
she associates with those nights, and that frightened her enough that she would cut the memory off
before it fully formed. Even so, the fragments that did break through left a mark on how she saw
herself: if a memory could overtake her that completely, she felt she must be weak, unable to handle
even a minor upset. The old scenes also seemed to confirm something she had long feared—that she
could not stop terrible things from happening, and that the people who did them often got away
untouched.

Those reactions are woven into her current life as an animator, where she spends long hours in a

slips from someone’s hand, her body reacts before she can think. She becomes instantly watchful,
scanning the room and tightening through her shoulders, sometimes snapping irritably if someone
comes up behind her. Sounds of objects hitting surfaces, images related to domestic violence that

can all set this off. In those moments she feels on guard and easily angered, as though danger is
already in the room.

sharper. She tries not to look directly at reminders of the perpetrator and will click away from
certain images immediately, but the effort to shut things down often leaves her with partial,
unfinished recollections that keep circling back. The more she pushes the memory away before it is
complete, the less settled it feels, and the easier it is for another sound or movement to bring it
back again.

for the future. Part of this is practical in her mind: when she feels this watchful and easily set

disappointment. When memories break through vividly, she becomes even more determined to narrow her
world to what she can control that day.

ordinary stress and that she is fundamentally helpless when something upsetting happens. Restricting
her eating gives brief relief, but it also leaves her more brittle and irritable, and on edge in the

making her feel once again that the past is stronger than she is.
