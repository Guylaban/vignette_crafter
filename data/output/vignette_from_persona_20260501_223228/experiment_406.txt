the pediatric unit she watched monitors, listened for alarms, and searched the faces of physicians

ended; it stayed suspended in her body, as if some part of her were still waiting outside an ICU
room for terrible news.

that a stronger mother, a better clinician, would not have been so overwhelmed, and this thought
spread beyond the crisis itself into a broader conviction that there was something wrong with her as

weak, that she had been exposed as defective, that nothing good could really be trusted anymore
because catastrophe could arrive without warning and undo everything.

working as a pharmacist, she found ways to minimize contact with medical settings beyond what her
job strictly required. If an ambulance siren passed near the pharmacy, she would go still and then
redirect herself rigidly into a task, as if concentration could seal the memory back up. She

she feared she would prove to herself just how weak she really was.
