damaged. He said, in the flat, definite way he said many things, that he had no future worth
picturing. Among college friends now building careers and dating and making plans, Mateo felt
permanently set apart, as if everyone else had remained inside ordinary life while he had crossed

said he was afraid that this was all his life would be from now on—avoiding, bracing, and keeping
silent—but some part of him still hoped there might be a way to remember what happened without being
condemned by it every time it returned.
