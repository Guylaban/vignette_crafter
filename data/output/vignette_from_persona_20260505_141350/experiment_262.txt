else to turn. But the old vigilance had never left. He had come to believe that the world was
inherently dangerous, that safety was an illusion, and that he had to be on guard at all times
because you never knew what could happen next. At work, he had learned to keep his voice low and his
movements controlled. A door slamming in the courthouse corridor made him jolt so hard that coffee
sometimes spilled on his files. The first time it happened, a senior attorney laughed and said,

learned to anticipate those sounds, scanning hallways before walking through them, checking exits in
