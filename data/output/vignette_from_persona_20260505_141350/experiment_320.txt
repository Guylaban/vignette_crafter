afterward, investigators questioned him. He cooperated fully, but the company’s legal team deflected
blame, and no charges were ever filed. The families of the dead men sued privately, but Jerome heard
nothing more. He was cleared of wrongdoing, but he could not shake the certainty that the people who
had cut corners and ignored his reports had simply walked away.

In the months that followed, Jerome began to change. He found himself flinching whenever a crew
member tossed a rope or a duffel bag across the deck. The sight of any uniform—security guards at
the port gate, police officers on the dock—sent a cold wave through his chest. And bangs: a hatch
slamming, a forklift backfiring, even the sound of a truck hitting a pothole near his home—each one
made him duck, heart pounding, certain that another explosion was coming. He started to avoid
thinking about the event altogether. If a colleague mentioned the accident, he changed the subject

completely, and there would be no putting himself back together.

that would take him to unfamiliar ports. “I can’t assess the danger,” he told himself, though he
