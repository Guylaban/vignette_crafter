immediate than the present. When he recalled the officer’s face or the sound of his own pleas, the
terror returned so vividly that his hands would tremble and his stomach would knot. Yet at the same
time, the whole scene felt impossible—a story that had happened to someone else, too extreme to be
his own life. He found himself startled by ordinary sounds: a car backfiring on his street, a door
slamming in the terminal, even the hiss of the plane’s intercom. Each sudden noise sent a jolt
through his chest, as if danger was about to materialize.

Keoni’s job, once a source of pride, became a landscape of hidden threats. When a flight was delayed
for maintenance, he grew convinced that something catastrophic was unfolding—a hidden mechanical
failure, a security breach—rather than a routine issue. He began to feel threatened by strangers who
approached him in ordinary situations: a passenger asking a question at the gate, a coworker walking
too close in the cockpit. Physical proximity, especially from behind, made his skin crawl. He
started avoiding the crew lounge, eating alone in the cockpit, and found himself irritably snapping
at his first officer for standing near his side.

would quicken, and he would demand they drop him off at home and take the car themselves. Eventually
he stopped driving altogether outside of commuting to the airport, and even that felt like a

backseat. When his girlfriend asked why he had changed so much, he could not explain. He only knew
that the world felt dangerous in ways it had never felt before.

traffic stop, but the divorce had been amicable; now his new partner grew frustrated with his

plans last-minute, claiming exhaustion. At work, his captain noticed he was quieter, more tense, and
he had begun taking more sick days than his accumulated leave allowed. The nausea returned
unpredictably—sometimes when a colleague brushed against him in the galley, sometimes when he waited
for a connecting flight and the departure board ticked past the scheduled time without explanation.

a delay was just a delay, that a stranger’s approach was just someone passing by. But the memory of
asphalt and cold steel told him otherwise, and he needed someone to help him untangle the story that
still felt too unreal to be true.
