changed everything. Her younger brother, a healthy and active man of thirty-eight, had collapsed at

quality of her mother's voice breaking, a sound that now plays in her head without warning. When the
memory returns, it arrives not as a full story but as a series of frozen snapshots: the phone in her
hand, the kitchen clock reading 9:47 PM, the pattern on the linoleum floor. Each image is so sharp
and so overwhelming that she has to stop whatever she is doing—once, in the middle of an evidence
review, she dropped her tablet and sat at her desk with her hands over her ears, unable to speak to
the colleague who asked if she was all right.

Her work as a forensic analyst had once given her a clear sense of purpose. She analyzed crime scene
data, pieced together timelines, and believed that every detail held meaning. Now the lab feels like
a place where nothing matters. She has lost faith in the order she once trusted—if her brother could
die without warning, then the careful logic of her work is just a thin layer over chaos. She has
stopped volunteering for case reviews and avoids the sections of the database that contain autopsy
photos or 911 recordings, though she never said why to her supervisor. Instead she calls in sick

inside, she stays late at her desk after everyone leaves, not because she is productive but because
she cannot bear to go home and lie in the dark. She delays sleep until two or three in the morning,
watching mindless videos or scrolling social media, anything to avoid the moment when her mind is
quiet and the snapshots might return. Her appetite has dwindled to nearly nothing; she eats a piece
of toast in the morning and forgets to eat again until her husband reminds her, and even then the
food tastes like cardboard.

Her husband, a high school teacher, has grown quiet and careful around her. He stopped asking about
her day weeks ago, because every attempt ends in silence or tears. Malia knows he is worried, but

that her life, the one she had before the phone call, has been destroyed. The forensic data she once
