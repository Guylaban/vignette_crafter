multiplication tables or counting ceiling tiles during the worst moments, a tactic that eventually
became a permanent, exhausting habit. To this day, Robert cannot watch a film or read a book in
which a child is harmed; he avoids such media entirely, switching channels or leaving the room

olfactory recall: sometimes, in a grocery store or waiting for a bus, Robert will catch the faint
trace of whiskey and for a moment the world around him dissolves, replaced by the textured reality
of his childhood. Those memories feel more vivid than any ordinary recollection, as if he is
physically there again, his heart racing, his skin cold.

that if he stops, something terrible will happen again. The abuser faced no consequences; Robert’s
father died of cirrhosis when Robert was nineteen, never charged, never held accountable. That

what might happen if he lets his guard down, but he is equally afraid of spending the rest of his
life listening for a key that will never turn.
