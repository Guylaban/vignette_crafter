the damp chill of that room, the way rain leaked through a crack in the ceiling and fell onto her
shoulder. She remembers a guard named Paul who would call her by name each time he entered, his
voice flat, almost bored. She still cannot hear the name Paul without her chest tightening. The
weather that entire period was gray and cold, with a constant drizzle. Now, whenever the sky turns

before her mind has caught up to what is happening.

she flinches so hard that she has dropped parts more than once. Her supervisor has warned her about
her "nerves." She has learned to keep her face still, but inside she is already scanning the room

pan dropping in the kitchen. Her body reacts before her conscious mind can intervene, flooding her
with adrenaline for no reason she can name in the moment.

watched, not something that belongs to her. Sometimes the memory feels as though it belongs to
someone else entirely, and she watches it from a distance. But then her body betrays her: a sudden
sweat, a racing heart, the urge to run. She has come to believe that her body is no longer
trustworthy. It sends alarms that have no clear cause and fails to protect her when real danger is
past. She no longer feels capable of doing the things that used to define her—speaking in public,
standing up for others, even holding a simple conversation without her mind fleeing to the past. The
world, she is certain, is a dangerous place, and she is not equipped to navigate it.

never in that room. She avoids wearing any clothes that resemble the ones she wore during her
detention: a dark blue blouse and black pants. She has thrown away every item of that color and now

screaming, why she would not talk about where she had been. She has lost touch with the few friends

that the person she used to be is gone forever, and that the only thing left is this body that does
