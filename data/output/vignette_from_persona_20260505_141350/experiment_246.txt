still behaved as though the next explosion was seconds away.

a roar that seemed to come from inside his skull. He remembered nothing of the next several

running. The police later told him a man had detonated a vest. Kwame’s memory held only fragments: a

smoke. The gaps unsettled him more than the images that did come, because he could never be sure
what he had seen and what his mind had invented.

of glass, the woman’s shoe. Other times, he could describe the whole thing to a friend or a
therapist (he saw one briefly) and feel nothing, as though he were reciting a story about someone
else. That numbness frightened him more than the fear did. If he could feel nothing about something
so terrible, what kind of person was he?

nervous hand. He began picking arguments with clients over small mistakes in their orders, or with
the delivery driver who was five minutes late. The fights made his heart race, but they also pushed
away the intrusive images for an hour or two. The relief never lasted, and afterward he felt even
more alone.

stillness meant thinking. He could not make plans for the future—not for a vacation, not for the
