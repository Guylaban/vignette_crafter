ear. The assault ended when a car’s headlights swept into the alley, startling the attacker, who
then fled.

In the months that followed, Layla found that the memory of the event refused to settle. It existed
not as a coherent story but as a collection of sensory fragments. Some moments were searingly clear:
the rough texture of the brick against her cheek, the specific, profane words he shouted, and the
sharp, radiating pain in her upper arms where his fingers had dug into her flesh. When these flashes
returned, she reported that the ache in her arms would flare up with such intensity it felt as

blank, leaving her with an unnerving sense that the worst parts were still hidden, waiting to
emerge. This incomplete picture of the trauma reshaped her perception of the world. Because she

had tuned her ears to a specific frequency of danger, making any raised male voice feel like an
immediate precursor to violence.

These sensitivities have made her daily life a minefield. A trigger, once encountered, launches her
immediately into a state of acute threat. Last week, while she was cleaning a client’s home, the
homeowner returned and yelled for his son from the entryway. For Layla, the sudden, booming sound
transformed the quiet house into a space of imminent danger. Her heart began to race, and she
reported backing into the corner of the bathroom she was cleaning, her eyes darting towards the
small window, her mind consumed with scanning for an escape route. Her concentration shattered, and
she spent the next hour unable to focus, simply wiping the same countertop repeatedly, listening for
footsteps on the stairs. She has come to believe that safety is an illusion and that threat is
everywhere, just waiting for a moment of inattention.

alert, this perpetual scanning for danger, has made her more likely to find it. While trying to
watch television, her heightened threat-monitoring causes her to fixate on news reports of other
assaults, which serve as reminders of her own status as a victim and send her spiraling. Her intense
focus, meant to protect her, has instead made her world smaller and more frightening, ensuring that
a trigger is never far away. The strain has become palpable in her marriage; her husband feels he is
