halting sentences, his gaze often fixed on a point just past the therapist's shoulder. He has not
worked in four months, having lost his job at a local distribution centre, and now rarely leaves the
small apartment he shares with his wife, Chloe. The event that changed everything, he explains,
happened six months ago. They were walking home from a late movie, taking a familiar shortcut
through a poorly lit side street, when a man stepped out from a recessed doorway. The memory doesn't
exist for Ethan as a coherent narrative he can recount; he says he can’t seem to put the frightening
event into words. Instead, it lives in him as a series of stark, photographic stills: Chloe’s arm
twisted behind her back, the man’s thick, veiny hands, the terrified look in her eyes. Sometimes,
when these images flash through his mind, he can smell the cheap, cloying cologne the attacker wore.

paralysis during the assault serves as constant, irrefutable evidence that he cannot rely on himself
in a crisis (**Memory → Negative Appraisals**). This deep-seated conviction that he is no longer
capable of protecting anyone, least of all himself, has made the world feel intensely dangerous. It
has primed him to see threats everywhere; he now scans faces in a crowd, his attention snagging on
anyone who seems out of place or moves too quickly (**Negative Appraisals → Triggers**). A simple
comment from a relative asking how Chloe is doing can feel like an accusation, a trigger that sends
a hot spike of shame through him. When these triggers arise—a suspicious-looking person on the
street, a news report about a local crime—the photographic fragments of the assault invade his mind,
sharp and vivid as the moment they happened (**Triggers → Memory**).

This constant sense of being under siege has reshaped his life into a series of defensive
manoeuvres. The feeling of immediate threat, of his body tensing for a fight he feels he has already
lost, is overwhelming (**Triggers → Threat**). This feeling has become so entwined with his
environment that he is always on guard, startling violently at sudden movements by others (**Threat
→ Triggers**). To manage this unbearable state of arousal, he has systematically eliminated anything
that might provoke it. He avoids watching any television shows or films that involve violence,
changing the channel frantically if a crime drama appears (**Triggers → Maladaptive Strategies**).
The intrusive images themselves are so agonizing that he will do anything to stop them; he can no
longer bear to be by himself, convinced that danger lurks just outside the door and that he would be
powerless to stop it (**Memory → Maladaptive Strategies**). His fear is a constant companion, and in
its presence, he clings to Chloe, a decision that only reinforces his belief that the world is
unsafe and he is inadequate to face it alone (**Threat → Maladaptive Strategies**).

These strategies, meant to keep him safe, have instead built a prison. By refusing to be alone, he
has inadvertently taught his nervous system that solitude itself is a threat, which only heightens
his baseline anxiety (**Maladaptive Strategies → Threat**). This avoidance also prevents the
fragmented, nonverbal memories from ever being processed, keeping them frozen and potent, ready to
ambush him at the slightest provocation (**Maladaptive Strategies → Memory**). Paradoxically, the
more he avoids, the more sensitive he becomes, and the list of triggers grows, shrinking his world
further (**Maladaptive Strategies → Triggers**). His body feels like an enemy, betraying him with a
racing heart and shallow breath even when he is sitting on his own couch (**Threat → Negative
Appraisals**). He can’t sleep through the night, jerking awake from nightmares where he is reliving
the attack, frozen and useless. When the pressure of the memories and the feeling of imminent danger
become too much to bear, he has started hurting himself, digging his nails into his own arms until
he bleeds, a desperate act to release the overwhelming feelings and replace the psychological pain
with a physical one.

He finally sought therapy after Chloe found the marks on his arm. The shame was profound, but it was
eclipsed by his terror of losing her. He feels he is no longer the man she married, just a
collection of fears and failures. He sits in the therapy room, a young man trapped in a feedback
loop of his own making, hoping for a way out but deeply afraid that he is permanently damaged, that
the capable person he once was is gone for good.
