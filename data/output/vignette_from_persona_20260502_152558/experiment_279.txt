predictable outcomes derived from careful analysis. This belief was incinerated, along with his

feeling utterly powerless, a sensation he had never before experienced.

In the months that followed, Jian’s analytical mind turned inward, trying to model and control a
new, terrifying variable: himself. The world, he had concluded, was fundamentally unsafe, and he had
to be constantly careful because you never know what might happen next. This core belief began to
dictate his life, making him hyper-attuned to any sign of danger. The scent of a distant chimney or
a neighbor’s barbecue would send a jolt of alarm through him, his mind immediately scanning for an
escape route. At his government office, which was known for its fluid priorities, any last-minute
meeting or sudden change in a project’s scope—things he once handled with ease—now felt like a
personal threat, a sign that chaos was about to erupt again. This constant state of high alert, born
from his conviction that he must always be on guard, made him exquisitely sensitive to the very
triggers that reminded him of his helplessness.

Any of these triggers—the smell of smoke, a sudden change of plans, even the simple sensation of
coming down with a cold—would immediately confirm his deepest fears about his own stability. When he
felt the onset of a seasonal flu last month, the familiar aches and fatigue didn't just feel like

sensation immediately sparked the thought, *I can’t rely on myself. My own body is failing me.* This

was definitive proof that he was going crazy.

This vicious cycle has left Jian feeling trapped inside his own head. The belief that he is fragile
and that the world is unpredictable makes him scan constantly for threats, which in turn makes him
more likely to encounter a trigger. When a trigger does occur, his intense reaction serves as

cannot trust his own responses. He has started to avoid situations he can’t control, declining
invitations to social gatherings and finding excuses to work from home more often, which has created
a subtle but growing distance between him and Mei.

feedback landed like a verdict, confirming his fear that he could no longer function. Jian is
terrified that the competent, analytical man he once was has been permanently replaced by this
fearful, fragile stranger. He came to therapy hoping, he said, to find a way to trust himself again
in a world he no longer believes is safe.
