sequence into words, but the pictures are perfectly, terribly clear. The crushing weight and the
sudden, absolute darkness were followed by a sound he can never forget—the deafening shriek of
twisting metal.

These flashes of memory, vivid and intrusive, are not just frightening; for Arthur, they have become
evidence of his own failure. He sees the image of himself, frozen for a split second before the
collapse, and has come to believe he is fundamentally inadequate, a leader who failed to protect his
crew. The clarity of these visual fragments serves as a constant reminder of his perceived
helplessness, cementing a deep conviction that his reactions prove he is a lousy coper. This belief
that he is no longer the capable man he once was makes the world feel impossibly dangerous. If he
couldn't handle that moment, he feels certain he cannot handle the future, a fear that keeps his
body in a constant state of high alert, scanning every room and bracing for the next catastrophe.

happening all over again.
