pride in her meticulousness and the quiet order she brought to other people’s lives. That sense of

her skin crawl all morning, and now, smelling of stale alcohol, he grabbed her wrist, twisting it
painfully. He said nothing, simply staring at her with a chilling possessiveness before she managed
to wrench free and flee the house. The memory of it never came back as a full story, only as jagged
pieces: the sour smell of whiskey on his breath, the sharp, grinding pain in her wrist, and the
overpowering, coppery taste of her own fear that filled her mouth.

alcohol on a stranger’s clothes at the grocery store would send a wave of nausea through her,

arthritis in her wrist on a cold morning was no longer just pain; it was a direct echo of his grip,
immediately plunging her back into that hallway. Most pervasively, however, was the experience of
being looked at. A man’s gaze holding hers for a moment too long on the bus, or a male client
watching her work, would instantly trigger a profound sense of threat. Her body would tense, her
breathing would become shallow, and she would feel an overwhelming urge to make herself invisible or
run. These moments not only ignited her fear but also seemed to unlock the memory fragments,
replaying the look in the attacker’s eyes and the feeling of being trapped.

The constant sense of being unsafe reshaped Priya’s life, even within the walls of her own home. She
had come to believe that danger was patient and silent, and she felt perpetually on guard, unable to

silence felt heavy with menace, a prelude to something terrible. This hypervigilance made her
acutely sensitive, turning innocuous events into new triggers. The creak of a floorboard was no
longer the house settling but a potential intruder, causing her heart to pound. This state of high

environment for danger, the fragmented images of the assault would intrude with startling clarity,
reinforcing her belief that she was never truly safe.

return to the worst thing that had actually happened.

quiet plea to be able to sit in her own living room without feeling like she was waiting for an
attack.
