police explained that without a direct, physical threat, their hands were tied. This left him with
the chilling certainty that he was entirely on his own.

that ambush him without warning. He will be on the subway and a sudden, vivid image of a particular

other times, it is a sound—the specific crunch of gravel he once heard outside his bedroom
window—that pulls him back. These pieces appear and vanish, leaving gaping holes in the timeline he
can’t fill, no matter how hard he tries. These memory gaps are a source of profound shame for Kenji;
he is convinced they are proof that he is a weak person, someone who must have missed crucial signs
and failed to protect himself. This belief that he is constitutionally inadequate is reinforced
every time a memory fragment returns, feeling less like a recollection and more like a fresh
failure.

This deep-seated conviction in his own weakness, coupled with the bitter knowledge that the

perception of the world. He has come to believe that safety is not a given but something he must
constantly and exhaustively create for himself. This manifests as a relentless, exhausting
vigilance. He finds himself watching other people closely on the street, scanning every face in a
crowd for a flicker of untoward interest. As a musician, his career depends on traveling to new
venues, but he now avoids unfamiliar places entirely, paralyzed by the fear of what he cannot
predict or control. The night is the worst; the quiet darkness no longer feels peaceful but charged
with potential danger, and he finds it nearly impossible to feel safe after sundown.

interprets as further proof of his failure to cope.

again, even if it means facing the memories he is so certain will unmake him completely.
