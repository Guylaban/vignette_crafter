undisciplined, and acting outside any recognizable chain of command. They dragged her from the
vehicle, their hands rough on her arms as they pushed her to her knees on the dusty road. While one
held her pinned, the others ransacked the car, laughing and shouting about their newfound authority.

felt like a piece of furniture they were moving, an object in the path of their chaotic search.

briefing and hearing a colleague discuss military strategy would send a jolt through her, not of
professional interest, but of visceral dread. The dispassionate talk of force and containment
instantly resurrected the memory of those unrestrained young men. This would plunge her into a state
of profound self-doubt and alienation; she felt again like an object, a pawn in a game she no longer
understood. The casual cruelty she had witnessed—the "misbehaving" that violated every rule she
lived by—made her question the very purpose of her work. If everything could be undone by brute
force, what was the point? In these moments, she was certain she could no longer trust her own
judgment to navigate a world that felt so fundamentally broken.

her quiet apartment, going over and over why the event had happened to her, trying to find a reason
or a lesson that would make it bearable. This search for meaning was relentless and fruitless, a
desperate attempt to impose order on a memory defined by its chaos. When the rumination became too

wall in her mind and push the images of that roadside behind it. But the effort was exhausting and
only served to make the memories more intrusive when her guard inevitably slipped.

unexpected physical restraint sending a wave of panic through her. Her internal world, saturated
with the trauma, was making the external world an increasingly difficult place to exist.

meaningful diplomat—had been permanently lost on that dusty road.
