violently he has to brace himself against a wall, his heart hammering against his ribs. The sense of
danger is so pervasive that sleep offers no respite; he often jolts awake in the middle of the
night, his first conscious thought a surge of panic as he strains to hear for footsteps outside his
door, absolutely certain he is not safe.

This ever-present sense of threat has reshaped his life into a series of elaborate detours and
avoidances. Because his mind is already primed for attack, any person in uniform has become a symbol

station. At the office building, he actively avoids his supervisor, whose company-issued polo shirt
feels too much like a uniform, a decision that has started to cause friction with management. This
hypervigilance also makes him acutely sensitive to new triggers; the sound of any car slowing down
on his street is no longer just a noise but a potential threat, a sign that they have come back for
him.

The triggers themselves are everywhere, each one sending a fresh wave of panic through him and

being watched. This overwhelming feeling of immediate danger compels him to act; he will duck into
an alley, change his direction, or light a cigarette with trembling hands just to have something to
do. He has learned to turn off the news instantly if a story involves police or conflict, and he has

friend can be instantly extinguished by a wave of terror, as if feeling good is a luxury he can no
longer afford, a vulnerability that will invite disaster.

manage the anxiety only leaves his nerves more frayed, which his mind interprets as further proof

avoiding reminders, was a major factor in his divorce; his ex-wife couldn’t understand why he would
shut down and leave the room whenever she tried to offer comfort. This self-imposed isolation has
only amplified his sense of being alone and unsafe. The more he avoids, the more his belief that
people in authority are dangerous is confirmed, tightening the coil of fear around his daily life

it real again, but a small, desperate part of him hopes there is a way to stop checking the locks,
to walk down the street without feeling like prey, and to simply feel safe in his own skin again.
