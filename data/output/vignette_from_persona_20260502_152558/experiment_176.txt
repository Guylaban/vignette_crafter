steadily, replaced by a brittleness that was beginning to alarm her colleagues and, more recently,

ghost, the anchor of her current distress.

feature of her storytelling; it defines the memory itself, which rarely surfaces as a coherent
recollection. Instead, it erupts without warning, triggered by the mundane. The faint smell of

instantly bring the full sensory experience rushing back. In these moments, she can taste the acrid
chemical as vividly as if she were back in that sealed room.

intense physiological alarm. The phantom taste of metal is immediately followed by a wave of panic;
her body clenches, every muscle tensing as if bracing for impact, and she begins scanning her
surroundings for an imminent threat. She knows, logically, that she is safe in her office in
Washington D.C., but her body is convinced it is in mortal danger. This profound sense of current
threat, arising from a memory she cannot control, has led her to become relentlessly hypervigilant.

to sharp, irritable outbursts that are entirely out of character. A junior aide using a strongly

shaken.

To manage the horror of these intrusions, Priya has developed a set of rigid, albeit
counterproductive, strategies. Her primary goal is to avoid thinking about the event at all. She

incident no one else seems to remember—a form of magical thinking that feels like a necessary
shield. This deliberate avoidance extends to her environment; she now refuses to take meetings in
certain conference rooms she deems "stuffy" and avoids any situation she anticipates might be

effort of suppression, however, has only made the memories more powerful and unpredictable when they
do breach her defenses, ensuring they remain unprocessed and terrifying.

which keeps her body locked in a state of high alert. This exhausting vigilance fuels her

recover.
