listening to the sounds of the forest press in on her, convinced she had been abandoned. The
experience left an indelible mark, one that has shaped her adult life in ways she is only now
beginning to untangle.

Over the years, Imani came to believe that what happened to her was not merely an act of cruelty,
but a just consequence of her own nature. She developed a core conviction that there is something
intrinsically wrong with her, that the abuse occurred because of the sort of person she is. This
deeply held negative appraisal of herself has conditioned her to experience the world as a place of
imminent danger. Believing she is fundamentally defective and deserving of punishment, her body
remains in a state of constant, exhausting vigilance. She describes this as a persistent tension in
her shoulders and jaw, a readiness to react that never subsides. This hyperarousal makes it
difficult to focus on the complex technical problems her IT job requires, and she often finds
herself rereading the same lines of code, her mind having drifted to scan for some unspecified
threat. Her belief in her own inherent fault also makes her exquisitely sensitive to any reminder of
the original trauma, making her world shrink around a growing list of triggers.

This sense of threat is not abstract; it manifests in sudden, overwhelming physical reactions to
seemingly benign situations. Recently at work, she was called to the main server room, a secure area
that requires a key card and locks automatically. The heavy click of the magnetic lock (`Locked
Spaces`) sent a jolt of pure panic through her. Her heart hammered against her ribs, and the cool,
conditioned air felt suddenly suffocating. She knew, logically, that she was in no danger, but her
body reacted as if the shed door had just been bolted. This powerful physiological response

further evidence that there is something profoundly wrong with her as a person. Each panic attack
becomes another piece of data confirming her deepest fear about her own character.

flawed, and the more flawed she feels, the more danger she perceives. This state of constant threat

of entering a confined space is now enough to make her hands tremble. When her husband recently
suggested a weekend camping trip to a state park, envisioning a relaxing escape, Imani felt a surge
