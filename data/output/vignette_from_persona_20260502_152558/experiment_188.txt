steering wheel, and then turning to see Priya unconscious and bleeding.

newfound terror of conflict, began to dictate her behaviour. Any disagreement, even a minor one with
her husband, felt catastrophic. It would instantly activate a profound and unshakeable sense of
self-blame. In her mind, the logic was inescapable: their argument in the car had distracted her,

happened "because of the way I acted" became the central organizing principle of her distress.

have offered comfort. The guilt she felt over Priya’s injuries, which were severe, was a constant,

appraisal fueled a deep avoidance of her social circle. Invitations from friends, especially those

person she believed herself to be. Her world became smaller, and her sense of mistrust grew—if she
couldn't even trust herself to keep her best friend safe, then no one, and nothing, could truly be
relied upon.

herself in the bedroom to avoid the escalating stress. This pattern of avoiding any situation that
puts her body under stress, including the physiological arousal of an argument, has become her
default. Her work as a database administrator, once a source of satisfaction, now presents its own
threats. The uncertainty of a complex project or a tight deadline mirrors the terrifying
