the hiss of the torch and the solid finality of a perfect seam. That sense of competence shattered

to pay—and in that moment, her grip on the securing clamp was imperfect. The beam shifted, tore free
from the hoist, and fell. It didn’t fall far, but it fell onto her colleague, a young apprentice
named David. The memory of the event is not a coherent story for her; instead, she describes it as a
collection of unbearable snapshots. She remembers the high-pitched screech of metal on metal, the
horrifyingly soft thud, and most vividly, the coppery, metallic taste of sheer panic that flooded
her mouth. This is what returns to her most often: not a full picture, but that sickening taste, as
if the event is happening inside her body all over again.

When these fragments of memory surface, often without warning, Anjali feels an intense wave of guilt
that is physically overwhelming. The taste of copper is followed by a hot jolt that shoots through
her torso, her heart hammering against her ribs as if trying to escape. She reports that these
strong physical reactions can be triggered by almost anything at her job now—the sudden clang of
dropped equipment, the smell of ozone from another welder’s torch. This constant state of being on
guard has shattered her ability to concentrate, making the precision work her job demands feel
nearly impossible. The threat seems to follow her home, where she lies awake for hours each night,
her body still buzzing with an adrenaline that has nowhere to go, leaving her exhausted and
perpetually on edge.

memories bring with them such intense guilt and a cascade of physical threat symptoms, Anjali has
developed strategies to keep them at bay. She actively avoids talking about what happened, changing
the subject if anyone brings it up. More significantly, she avoids her colleagues, especially those
who were there that day. She takes her breaks alone in her truck and keeps her protective hood down

the easy camaraderie of the workshop is gone, replaced by a strained silence that she believes is

convinced that if she just thinks it through one more time, she will find the key to what she

incomplete, painful memory fragments fresh and potent, ensuring they never have a chance to settle
