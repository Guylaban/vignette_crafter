permanently seared into her mind.

fact settled into Eleanor’s mind as a profound and festering injustice. The belief that the person
who had trapped her was still free, unaccountable, began to curdle her view of the world. She had
come to believe that safety was an illusion and that nothing had been done to right this grievous
wrong. This conviction meant she could never truly be safe herself. At home, she began a nightly
ritual of checking locks, her body perpetually tense, listening for any sound that might signal an

own living room, the place she had shared with her late husband for fifty years. Her world had
shrunk to a perimeter of perceived threats.

source of deep and constant misery.

she had become was all that was left of her.
