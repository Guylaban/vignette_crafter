group of children were laughing as they splashed water. For a fleeting second, Javier felt a warmth
spread through his chest, a flicker of the purpose he used to feel. Then, the warmth curdled into
ice. The memory surfaced, unbidden and sharp as glass: a similar moment of shared relief at an aid

around him both a shield and a suffocating trap.

simple act of Elena wrapping her arms around him from behind can feel like an assault, a trap
closing in. In response to these moments of internal alarm, Javier has developed a rigid set of
defenses. After nearly swerving into oncoming traffic when a truck backfired, he began carrying a
small, heavy pistol in a holster at the small of his back. The weapon does not make him feel safe;
it simply makes the world feel appropriately dangerous, confirming his belief that a threat is
always seconds away. When the quiet of an evening at home becomes unbearable, filled with the low
hum of his own anxiety, he will pick an argument with Elena. He seizes on some triviality—a bill

mind: *Why did it happen to me? Why did I survive when that little girl didn't?*

constant state of high alert means that the physical proximity of a customer leaning over a

needs.
