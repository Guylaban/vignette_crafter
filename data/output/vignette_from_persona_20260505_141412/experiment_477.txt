moment, of his own paralysis, had become a permanent fixture in his mind.

but the damage was already done. He found that he could no longer tolerate the sensation of being

knotting up. A hand on his arm from his daughter would send a jolt through him, and he would pull
away before he could stop himself. He told himself it was just because he was getting older, more
set in his ways. But he knew, deep down, that the feeling of another person’s hand on his skin had
become linked to that moment of helplessness. He had also begun to avoid any reminder of Carol. He
threw away her photographs, cleared her belongings from the house, and stopped driving past the bar

alone—that sharp, sweet odor of alcohol—would make his stomach turn and his heart race. He would
find himself scanning a room at a family gathering, checking for wine bottles, and would leave early
if anyone opened one.
