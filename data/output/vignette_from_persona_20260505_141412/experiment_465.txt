non-profit that provided legal aid to immigrant families in her city. She had always thought of
herself as someone who could handle difficult situations, someone who could sit with a client

words away entirely.

glint of the metal under the single parking lot light, the way he swung it once through the air
before using it to smash her driver’s side window. What she could not remember, no matter how hard
she tried, was the order of anything after that. She knew he had forced her into the back seat. She
knew he had made her take off her clothes. She knew he had hurt her. But the sequence was a tangle
she could not straighten, and every time she tried to tell a therapist or her husband Carlos what
had happened, the words simply would not come. She would open her mouth and find only silence, or
fragments that did not connect, and then the rage would rise so fast and so hot that she had to
leave the room.

In the weeks that followed, Elena began to change in ways that frightened her. She had always been
someone who felt safe in her own skin, but now being naked—even in her own bathroom, even with the
door locked—sent her heart racing. She would shower in the dark, or wear a bathing suit under her
clothes, because the vulnerability of her own body felt unbearable. At work, she could no longer
walk past the supply closet where the office kept a baseball bat for emergencies. The sight of any
weapon, even a kitchen knife on her own counter, made her stomach drop. She began to avoid the news,
then the street where her office was, then the neighborhood altogether. She told Carlos she was
working from home now, which was true for a few weeks, until her supervisor called to say she was
running out of sick leave.

What frightened Elena most was what she had come to believe about herself. She was certain now that
she was a weak person. Somebody else—somebody stronger, smarter, more alert—would not have let
themselves get into that situation. She replayed the meeting that night, the decision to stay late,
the choice to park in the back lot. She had failed a basic test of survival, and she could not
forgive herself for it. She had also lost something she could not name until she tried to go to her
cousin’s quinceañera last month. Standing in the church hall, surrounded by the music and the

nothing to fear. But Elena could not believe him. Her body would not let her. At night, she lay
awake listening to every creak and groan of the building, certain that danger lived in the dark
hours. During the day, the smallest unexpected sound—a car backfiring, a door slamming—would send

and she had started to avoid going out after sunset altogether, which meant she had missed three

that this was who she had become. But she made the call anyway, and she sat in the waiting room with
her hands shaking, hoping that someone could help her find the words again.
