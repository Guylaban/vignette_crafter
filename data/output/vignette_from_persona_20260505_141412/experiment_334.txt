when she tried to stand, her legs would not hold her. Someone’s blood was on her hands, she never
learned whose. She crawled toward the side door, certain that another bomb was coming, certain that
she would not make it out, and that certainty—the absolute knowledge that she was about to die in a
room full of her own constituents—had not left her since.

In the weeks that followed, Priya returned to work, but something fundamental had shifted. She had
come to believe that the world was not merely unpredictable but actively dangerous, that danger was
hiding everywhere, waiting for the moment she let her guard down. She found herself watching people
differently now. At council meetings, she scanned the audience row by row, noting who sat near
exits, who kept their hands in their pockets, who looked at her too long. She was vigilant for
dangerous people in a way she had never been before, and she had decided that this vigilance was not
paranoia but necessity. She told herself that nobody else understood how close disaster always was,
how easily it could happen again. She had to be on guard all the time. She had to be especially
careful because you never knew what could happen next.

Her daily life became a series of calculations. She stopped going to unfamiliar parts of the city

chief of staff to attend events there in her place. At restaurants, she always sat facing the door,

her surroundings constantly—every new room, every hallway, every parking garage—and she could not
stop. At night, she found it harder to feel safe. She kept the lights on in her apartment until she
fell asleep, and she slept poorly, waking at small sounds, her heart already pounding. She had
stopped dating altogether. The thought of being vulnerable with a stranger, of letting someone into
her home, of not being able to watch the door, felt impossible.

anger. They did not understand. They had not been there. She had to be careful. She had to survive.

What finally brought her to seek help was a council vote on a new public transit route. The route

remembered what that felt like. She was not sure she believed it was possible.
