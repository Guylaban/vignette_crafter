tried to scream. She remembered thinking that if she just stayed very still, if she did not fight,
she might survive. And she did survive. But something in her had broken open that night and never
healed.

within two weeks, because the thought of staying home alone in her quiet condo felt worse than the
thought of facing her cubicle. But the world had rearranged itself around her. She began to notice
things she had never noticed before—the heft of a stapler on a desk, the sharp edge of a pair of

vigilance would matter because bad things happened anyway. When her supervisor told her the office
had installed new security cameras and that she was safe, Maria nodded politely and felt nothing.

source of shame, that she has lost the person she used to be. But she is also hoping—barely,
quietly—that maybe she can find a way back.
