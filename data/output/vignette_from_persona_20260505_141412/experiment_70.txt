there, standing over her, ready to begin again.

alert. Her body, she said, felt like a live wire—constantly tense, ready to react, even when she
knew rationally that no one was there. She had come to believe that the world was fundamentally
dangerous, that danger could emerge from anywhere at any moment, and that the only way to survive
was to stay prepared. This was not a thought she argued with; it was a truth she lived inside, as

exit, every blind corner, every place where a man could hide.

talk herself out of. She had learned to structure her days so that she was never isolated with a
single male crew member. She scheduled meetings in open areas, kept her cabin door propped open, and

ordeal. When a fellow officer offered her a ride to port, she felt trapped, her eyes fixed on the
door handle, her mind racing through scenarios of what could go wrong. She had started making
excuses—extra work, a late shift—just to avoid getting into a car with anyone.

apart from other people, as though she were watching them from behind a pane of glass. They laughed,
they argued, they relaxed; she calculated. They spoke about their weekends; she thought about how to
position herself in a room. She had begun to believe that there was something fundamentally wrong
with her, that the defect was inside her, not in the world. Other people did not live this way.
Other people did not scan every shadow for a threat that was no longer there. So the problem must be
her.

running on adrenaline for so long that her body had begun to give out—headaches, stomach pain, a
bone-deep fatigue that no amount of sleep could touch. She had also started to notice that her crew
was watching her differently. They had stopped offering rides. They had stopped inviting her to
dinner. She was the captain, and they respected her, but they no longer tried to connect. She had
become untouchable, and she did not know how to find her way back. She was afraid that if she did
not change something, she would spend the rest of her life alone on the bridge of a ship, watching
the horizon and waiting for a danger that never came.
