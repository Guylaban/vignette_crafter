cover rewired his understanding of how quickly life could be taken. That moment did not stay in the

sudden movements as imminent danger. Over the decades, this scanning became automatic. He began
avoiding crowded spaces not because he disliked people, but because the unpredictability of crowds
felt like a return to that open ground, and he learned that stepping away from noise, from groups,
from unexpected conversations kept the tension at bay. The avoidance settled in slowly, week by
week, then year by year, until the house grew quieter, his social circle thinned, and his days
narrowed to routines that felt controllable. Now, at the warehouse, that narrow routine is
constantly tested. The facility is a maze of high shelving units, concrete aisles, and the rhythmic
hiss of pneumatic doors opening and closing. Vikram works the receiving dock, a role he chose
specifically for its predictable flow, but predictability here is fragile. When a forklift backfires
with a sharp metallic pop, his shoulders hike, his breath catches, and for a few seconds he is back
in that valley, certain the ground will shake and the next round will find him. What frightens him
most is not the noise itself, but the way his body betrays him by responding as though he is still

raise their voices over the radio, because proximity to sudden sounds and authoritative commands

knows that staying forces his nervous system to relive the ambush, and the recovery takes hours.
Over the years, this pattern has hardened into a quiet architecture of daily life. He arrives early,

carries the unspoken risk of the unexpected, and each avoidance brings a fleeting, temporary relief
that only deepens the isolation. The isolation has grown heavy. At seventy-nine, the weight of a
life carefully narrowed feels less like protection and more like a slow erosion of what remains. He
stopped going to the veterans’ hall three years ago because the sudden laughter and overlapping

had quietly unraveled under the same weight; he had come to believe that his wife would eventually
