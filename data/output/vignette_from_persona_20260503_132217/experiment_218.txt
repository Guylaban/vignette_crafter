burned, surviving the night huddled in a drainage culvert. She returned home thinking she had simply
endured a harrowing assignment, but the basin’s damp soil, the sharp barked commands, and the
documented camp layouts she had meticulously photographed all lingered beneath her skin, planting
seeds of alarm that would soon take root. Within weeks, the memories refused to settle into a
coherent narrative. Whenever Sari tried to recount the incident to her fiancé or even to herself,
she would lose the thread mid-sentence, her words fracturing into fragments that felt like they
belonged to a stranger rather than to her. That dissonance sparked an intense rage whenever she
thought of the collapse, a hot, suffocating anger that made her jaw clench until it ached. Rather
than process the anger, she instinctively pushed the thoughts out of her mind and kept her mind
occupied all the time, scrolling through municipal blueprints or running errands until exhaustion
took over. She had come to believe that if she simply refused to look at old photos from her
deployment or walk through dense greenways, she could keep the past contained, but the very act of
avoidance had already begun to shape her nervous system. Because Sari’s body remained locked in a
constant state of threat appraisal, every attempt to remember brought back the event with such
visceral force that it fractured into fragments that felt like they belonged to a stranger rather
than to her. Her hyperarousal never allowed her to distance herself from the trauma; instead, the
fear kept the memory vivid and unprocessed. When the pressure finally became too much, she would
turn to self-harm, hurting herself physically to release overwhelming feelings that otherwise
threatened to spill over. This strategy of physical release, however, only sharpened her alarm,
meaning that by the time she encountered ordinary stimuli, she lacked the capacity to regulate, so a

old panic and causing her to drop her clipboard and crouch behind a supply crate. At home, her
