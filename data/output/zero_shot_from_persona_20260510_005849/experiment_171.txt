**Case Vignette: Mr. K.**

Mr. K. is a 66-year-old, married, South Eastern Asian man who has worked as a roofer for over forty
years. He presents for therapy at the urging of his wife, who has noticed a profound change in his

The story begins on a Tuesday afternoon in late July, two years ago. Mr. K. was working on a large

asphalt shingles softening under a sun that felt like a weight on his shoulders. He remembers a
sudden, sharp hissing sound, and then a cloud of yellow-green gas billowing from a corroded pipe
near the air intake. He didn’t have time to run. He inhaled once, deeply, before he could hold his
breath. The air burned his throat and chest, and a metallic, acrid taste filled his mouth. He
scrambled, coughing, his eyes streaming, and fell from the low scaffolding, twisting his ankle. His

later identified as a refrigerant leak. The physical symptoms—the burning, the coughing, the
tightness in his chest—lasted for weeks. But the real change started inside.

Now, Mr. K. cannot go near a roof. He has not worked in eighteen months. The mere sight of a ladder,
even on a neighbor’s house, makes his heart pound so hard he feels it in his throat. He used to be
the first one up, the one who could walk a two-by-four without a second thought. Now, he finds
himself driving three extra miles to avoid passing the commercial building where it happened. If he
sees a yellow truck—the color of the gas cloud—he has to pull over, his hands trembling, his breath
coming in short, ragged gasps.

about to happen again. He cannot relax. He checks the stove three times before bed. He scans the sky
