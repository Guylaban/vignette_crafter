**Patient Vignette: Mrs. A.**

Mrs. A. is a 66-year-old woman of Oceania descent, a divorced shopowner who presents with a deep,

asphalt and her own blood. She was handcuffed, arrested for "resisting," and held for six hours. The
charges were later dropped, but the physical and psychic injuries remain.

In the months following, Mrs. A. has become a prisoner in her own store. The front door, once always

lingers too long. She cannot tolerate the sound of a car door slamming outside. The jingle of keys
sends a jolt of electricity through her chest. She has stopped driving altogether, relying on a
neighbor for deliveries. Her shop's inventory has dwindled; she cannot bear the thought of a trip to
the warehouse, where the roads are wide and the patrol cars are frequent.

Her sleep is a battlefield. She wakes multiple times a night, drenched in sweat, the phantom weight
of the officer's knee pressing into her back. She hears the crack of her skull on the concrete, a
sound that has no visual, only a terrible, visceral echo. She has installed three deadbolts on her
bedroom door and sleeps with a small hatchet under her pillow. She describes a constant state of
"waiting for the next blow," a hypervigilance that has eroded her capacity for joy.

burning, silent rage at the officer, at the system, at herself for not fighting back. This rage is

brother, who brings her groceries and sits with her in the back room, saying little. She has become
a ghost in her own life, a woman who once knew every customer by name now shrinking from a simple
