**Trauma:** Witnessing a fatal workplace accident

for a server crash. She retired from her full-time role as a network engineer three years ago, but
she still consults two days a week for a regional telecom company. Her mind, she says, is still the
fastest thing in the room. But lately, it feels like the whole system is running on faulty wiring.

The event happened six months ago. She was in the main data center, a cold, humming room full of

always brought her black coffee, was working on a ladder near the overhead cable trays. He was

landing on the concrete floor with a sound she had never heard before and cannot unhear. She was the

but his leg was bent at an impossible angle, and he kept whispering, “I’m sorry, I’m sorry.” He died
of a brain bleed three hours later at the hospital.

Evelyn did not cry at work. She helped secure the scene, wrote the incident report, and went home.
That night, she woke up at 3:17 a.m. gasping, her hands numb, her heart slamming against her ribs.
She thought she was having a heart attack. Her husband, Tom, drove her to the ER. Tests were normal.
The doctor said “anxiety.”

conference room, which she knows is inefficient. She cannot look at a ladder without her throat

him she was too tired, then locked herself in the bathroom and cried for twenty minutes.
