**Case Vignette: "The Echo of the Arc"**

The patient is a 38-year-old single woman of South Eastern Asian descent who works as a welder. She
presents with a guarded posture, her hands calloused and steady, but her eyes darting to the corners
of the room as if tracking something unseen. She has been attending sessions reluctantly, referred
by her union’s employee assistance program after a series of near-misses on the job.

She begins by describing the incident, which occurred eight months ago. She was working a late shift
at a fabrication plant, repairing a support beam on a raised platform. It was a routine job—she had
done it hundreds of times. But that night, she was tired. Her foreman had pushed the crew to meet a
deadline, and she had skipped her break. As she welded a joint, a spark caught a pile of oily rags
below. She didn’t notice. The fire spread quickly, and a younger apprentice, a man in his twenties,
was trapped in the scaffolding collapse. She heard his screams before she saw him. By the time she
and others got to him, his leg was pinned and badly burned. He survived, but he lost the leg below
the knee. She remembers the sound of his screaming, then the silence when the paramedics gave him
morphine.

In the weeks after, she could not return to the plant. She tried, but the smell of burning metal
made her vomit. She now works light duty in the tool crib, but even that is a struggle. She
describes a constant, intrusive image: the arc of the welding torch, the color of the flame, and
then the afterimage of the apprentice’s face. She sees it when she closes her eyes, when she tries
to sleep, when she is driving. She has started to avoid anything that reminds her of the event—she
no longer watches movies with fire, she stopped cooking with gas, and she has not touched her
welding helmet since that night.

She reports feeling “hollow” most days. She has withdrawn from her small circle of friends,
canceling plans last minute because the thought of socializing feels exhausting. She lives alone in

refrigerator. She says she feels like she is “watching herself from far away.” She has difficulty
sleeping—she wakes up at 3 a.m. every night, drenched in sweat, her heart pounding. She does not
remember her dreams, only the feeling of falling.

letting anyone close now. She feels that she is dangerous, that she carries something toxic inside
her.

break. If I had checked the area. If I had yelled a warning.* She knows, logically, that the

there. But the feeling does not respond to logic. She feels she has failed as a welder, as a
