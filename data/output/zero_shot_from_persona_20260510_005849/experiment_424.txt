Mr. R. is a 54-year-old married Latin American man who owns a successful regional logistics company.

He was rescued, but the world has not felt the same since.

In the months after, Mr. R. threw himself back into his business. He worked eighteen-hour days,
believing that if he just kept moving, the memory would fade. It did not. He now avoids all news
about the region. His wife recently suggested a documentary about humanitarian work, and he left the
room without a word, his hands shaking. He cannot drive past a military checkpoint without his heart
pounding so hard he has to pull over. The sound of a helicopter overhead—common in his city for

does drift off, he dreams of Elias’s face, the blood spreading across his shirt, the radio crackling

feeling that he is still trapped under that truck, listening to Elias die. He wants to feel like

steer a truck through danger, and sees only the hands that failed to save a friend.
