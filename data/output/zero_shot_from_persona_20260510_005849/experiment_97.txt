**Case Vignette: "Amir"**

Amir is a 52-year-old actor, a man whose presence once filled a room with warmth and a quiet,

composed exterior, there is a profound fragility, a man holding himself together with the thinnest

The trauma did not happen to him, but *through* him. He was on stage, performing a sold-out show in

must go on. The second act was his finest work, he recalls, a monologue about loss. He was electric.

calls from his brother-in-law. Leila, his wife of twenty-five years, the architect of his calm, the

feet. The sound, meant to be adulation, hit him like a physical blow. His vision narrowed, his heart
hammered against his ribs, and he felt a wave of nausea so violent he had to be helped off stage. He
now avoids curtain calls, leaving the theatre through a side door before the house lights come up.

His sleep is a battlefield. He avoids the bedroom he shared with Leila, sleeping on a futon in his
study. He has nightmares, but they are not of the hospital. They are of the first act. He is
reciting lines, but the words are wrong. He is smiling, but the audience is crying. He wakes in a
cold sweat, convinced he can still hear the sound of his own voice echoing in an empty theatre. He
