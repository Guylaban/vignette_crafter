**Case Vignette: "David"**

David is a 32-year-old married man who works as a private academic tutor, specializing in
mathematics and science for high school students. He presents as articulate and thoughtful, though
his eyes frequently dart to the windows and doorways of the office during our sessions.

The trauma began approximately eighteen months ago. David had been tutoring a 17-year-old female
student for about six months. She was bright but struggled with test anxiety, and David prided
himself on his patient, encouraging approach. He noticed she began arriving early to sessions,
lingering afterward, and sending him increasingly personal emails—first about her social life, then
about her feelings of loneliness, and eventually, explicit declarations of romantic attachment.

David, uncomfortable but uncertain how to handle it professionally, responded with polite, distant
replies. He suggested she speak with her school counselor. The student's mother called him, irate,
accusing him of encouraging her daughter's infatuation. The school removed him from their approved
tutor list. He lost that client and several others by association.

Then the stalking began.

It started with dozens of text messages from blocked numbers. Then emails from accounts he didn't
recognize. Then voicemails—long, rambling, sometimes crying, sometimes angry. He changed his phone
number twice, but she always found the new one. She began appearing at the coffee shop where he
graded papers. She waited outside his apartment building. She left notes under his windshield wiper.

David's wife, Sarah, grew terrified. They installed security cameras. He stopped going out alone
after dark. He began checking the street before leaving his car. The police filed a report but said
her behavior—while disturbing—hadn't yet crossed the legal threshold for a restraining order. "Just
document everything," they told him.

The stalking continued for four months. Then, abruptly, it stopped. The student's family moved to
another state. David never received an explanation or an apology.

But in the nine months since, David has not recovered. He no longer tutors female students, citing a
"scheduling conflict." He has reduced his client load by half. He works exclusively from home, in a

deletes any message that feels "too personal" without reading it fully.

He describes a constant, low-grade vigilance that never turns off. In the grocery store, he scans
faces. At a stoplight, he checks his rearview mirror obsessively. He no longer goes to the gym

the lock or footsteps on the stairs. He has nightmares—not of the student herself, but of being

should have been firmer, less kind, more professional from the start. He replays those early
interactions constantly, searching for the moment he "gave her permission." He believes the stalking
was his fault.

weekend. David agrees to these plans, then cancels at the last minute. He cannot explain why he
