tires as he pulled out of the driveway, the repetitive thud of the windshield wipers, the cold

smell that now clings to his nightmares.

that sweater, just below her ribs. He never saw her open her eyes again.

In the weeks that followed, he moved through the funeral and the endless stream of grieving
relatives like a ghost. He was the stoic older brother, the rock for his parents. But at night, the
dam would break. He’d wake up gasping, his heart hammering against his ribs, the image of the blue

His life has since become a complex map of avoidance. He takes a twenty-minute detour on his way to

He sits at his desk, staring at the blank page. He tries to write about anything else—a character
