New England coast. That changed irrevocably thirteen months ago. He was alone, navigating his small

console. He remembered the sudden lurch, the immediate rush of icy water, and the terrifying
sensation of being trapped beneath the capsizing hull before he managed to claw his way to the
surface. He clung to a piece of debris for what felt like an eternity, the silence that followed the
initial violence a profound, chilling emptiness, interrupted only by the lapping waves and his own
gasping breaths. His body, once a reliable instrument of his trade, felt like a traitor, seizing
with cramps in the frigid water. He watched his livelihood sink, feeling a terror so absolute it
seemed to rewrite the very fabric of his being.

but he was unable to find words to describe it, the details slipping away as he struggled to
articulate the horror. What truly troubled him was that the memory felt like it was happening right
now rather than something that was over. Remembering the event brought back the same terror he felt
at the time, a visceral re-experiencing of the cold, the fear, the helplessness, as if he were still
clinging to that debris.

This constant re-experiencing cast a long shadow over his life, particularly as a widower who had
already known profound loss. He found himself increasingly withdrawn, the world outside his small
home feeling hostile and unpredictable. What frightened him most was the pervasive sense of danger.

fish market or simply sitting by his window overlooking the harbour. Even inside his own home, a

unusual silence, like the sudden hush after a distant foghorn, would make him feel something
dangerous was about to happen, sending a jolt of adrenaline through him.

even on television, would trigger vivid, unwanted images of his boat being struck, the sudden,

talking to strangers, even familiar faces in town, felt like an ordeal. He had come to believe that

turned to difficult topics. Any talk about people losing loved ones would bring him back to the
overwhelming sense of vulnerability and loss he felt after the accident, compounding his existing
grief for his late wife and reminding him of his own brush with death.

cheerful, resilient fisherman replaced by a man consumed by apprehension. His body, which had served

seeing fewer people. He had stopped attending the community gatherings that once brought him joy,

and mind, that finally prompted him to seek help. He wasn't sure what to expect, but he desperately
hoped to find a way back to the man he once was, or at least to a life where the memory of the sea
didn't feel like a constant threat.
