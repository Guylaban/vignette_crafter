sharp jolt that sent a searing ache down his shoulder. The mugger fled with his wallet and phone,
leaving Liam shaken and disoriented on the cold pavement.

In the months that followed, the memory of that night played out in his mind with unnerving clarity.
He described it as being "just like looking at photographs of it," each detail sharp and unyielding.

sheer injustice of it. Yet, intertwined with this anger was an intense guilt. Liam found himself
repeatedly thinking, *Somebody else would not have gotten into this situation*. He replayed the
scenario countless times, convinced that a different choice, a different route, or a different
reaction would have prevented it. He had come to believe, with a heavy certainty, that he was simply
inadequate, unable to protect himself, and somehow deserving of what had happened.

unrelenting need "to be on guard all the time," even when he knew intellectually he was in a safe

ostensibly unwinding after a long day, but his muscles remained tense, his mind racing through
potential threats. This vigilance extended to his environment; as soon as he entered any room or
