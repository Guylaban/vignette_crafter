Isabella, a 39-year-old soldier, found herself increasingly adrift in a life that once felt so
purposeful. She had always prided herself on her resilience, her ability to navigate challenging
environments with a clear head, but that certainty shattered during a humanitarian mission three
years ago. Her unit was deployed to a coastal region in Central America just as a Category 4
hurricane made an unexpected turn, slamming into the densely populated area with devastating force.
Isabella vividly recalled the howling wind that sounded like a thousand banshees, the terrifying
rumble of buildings collapsing, and the desperate cries for help swallowed by the storm. She was
part of a small team attempting to evacuate a makeshift clinic when the full fury of the hurricane
hit. The local police, overwhelmed and under-resourced, were everywhere and nowhere, their frantic
efforts often dissolving into chaos. Isabella had never felt such profound helplessness as she
watched the storm tear through homes, knowing there was little she, or anyone, could do.

Since returning home, the world had become a minefield. Isabella found herself constantly on edge,
her senses perpetually scanning her surroundings for signs of danger, a habit that had become second
nature. Any sudden, loud noise – a car backfiring, a dropped tray in the mess hall, even her husband
Marco’s boisterous laughter – would send a jolt through her, causing her to jump or startle
violently. It was embarrassing, making her feel foolish and out of control. When she entered any new
space, be it a restaurant or a briefing room, her eyes would immediately dart around, identifying
every possible escape route before she could even begin to relax. This was particularly challenging
on base, where new assignments often meant unfamiliar buildings and layouts.

The weather had become another potent trigger. A dark, cloudy sky, a sudden gust of wind, or the
sound of heavy rain against the window would plunge her back into the terror of the hurricane. Her
heart would pound, her breath would quicken, and a cold dread would seep into her bones, convinced
that another disaster was imminent. She’d find herself glued to weather reports, obsessively
tracking even minor storm systems, a habit Marco found increasingly concerning. Even the sight of
police officers, whether on patrol or just passing by, could trigger a surge of anxiety. It wasn't
that she feared them, but their presence, especially when paired with any hint of chaos or urgency,
brought back the vivid, terrifying memory of the overwhelmed authorities during the hurricane, a
stark reminder of how quickly order could disintegrate.

Isabella had come to believe that her reactions since the event showed she was a lousy coper, a
harsh self-judgment for a soldier whose identity was so intertwined with strength and resilience.
She felt disconnected, like an object moving through the world rather than a person with agency.
This feeling of dehumanization was particularly acute when she thought about the sheer scale of the
disaster, how individual suffering seemed lost in the overwhelming statistics. What frightened her
most was the pervasive belief that she couldn’t stop bad things from happening to her, a chilling
echo of her powerlessness against the hurricane. She started avoiding social gatherings, fearing her
unpredictable startle responses or her need to constantly scan the room would be noticed and judged.
Her performance at work began to suffer; her hypervigilance, while initially seeming like an asset,
made it difficult to focus on routine tasks, and her constant anxiety about potential dangers led
her to second-guess her decisions, delaying her reports and making her appear indecisive to her
command.

Marco, her husband, had grown increasingly worried about her withdrawal and the palpable fear that
seemed to cling to her. He urged her to seek help, pointing out how much she had changed. The
breaking point came during a routine training exercise when a simulated explosion sent her into a
full-blown panic attack, causing her to freeze and become disoriented. It was then, amidst the
concerned faces of her unit, that Isabella finally acknowledged she couldn’t navigate this alone.
She sought help, hoping to reclaim the woman she once was, but underneath that hope lay a deep fear
that she was permanently broken, destined to live in constant dread.
