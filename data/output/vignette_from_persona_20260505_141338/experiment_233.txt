his wallet, their faces obscured by hoods. Ramesh remembered the metallic taste of fear in his
mouth, the frantic pounding of his heart, and the sickening sense of powerlessness as they rifled
through his pockets before disappearing back into the night as quickly as they had appeared.

In the months that followed, the world Ramesh had known began to warp around him. Sleep offered
little respite, often dissolving into terrifying nightmares that left him waking in a cold sweat.

hours after, the world felt imbued with the same dread and vulnerability he’d experienced in his
sleep. He found it profoundly harder to feel safe at night than during the day, a constant gnawing
apprehension settling over him as dusk approached. He would meticulously check and re-check the
locks on his doors and windows, sometimes multiple times an hour, long after his wife, Priya, had
gone to bed.

unpredictability of others. He was certain that strangers approaching him, even in ordinary

every gesture; sudden movements by others, whether it was a neighbor quickly reaching for a mail
slot or a fellow driver making an abrupt lane change, put him immediately on guard, his heart
leaping, his breath catching.

bustling local market, a cherished Saturday ritual he used to share with Priya, nor did he attend
the community events he once enjoyed. The smell of alcohol, a potent reminder of his assailant,
became unbearable. He would cross the street to avoid walking past bars or restaurants where he
might catch a whiff, and politely declined invitations to gatherings where drinks would be served,
much to Priya’s quiet concern.

him with dread. He started turning down requests that required him to work late, or in areas he

home feel like a fragile fortress.
