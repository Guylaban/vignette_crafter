as a plumber, a trade he’d practiced for over thirty years. His life with his wife, rooted in their
community, felt stable and predictable until an evening six months ago shattered that sense of
security. He had been called to an emergency, a burst pipe in the dimly lit, labyrinthine basement
of an old apartment building on the city's outskirts. As he navigated the unfamiliar corridors, his
tools clanking softly, a figure suddenly emerged from behind a large boiler, striking him from
behind and demanding his wallet. The assault was swift and brutal, leaving him disoriented and
bruised, his tools scattered across the grimy floor as the assailant vanished into the shadows as
quickly as they appeared.

In the aftermath, Samuel found himself struggling to piece together what had happened. His memories
of the frightening event were mostly pictures or images – the glint of metal, the sudden lunge, the
dark figure disappearing. What was most distressing was that he couldn't seem to put the frightening
event into words; the narrative felt fragmented and elusive, yet the visual flashes were vivid and
intrusive. When these memories surfaced, an intense, gnawing guilt would wash over him. He replayed
the scene endlessly, questioning if he could have done something differently, if he should have been
more aware, stronger, faster. This self-reproach fed a pervasive sense of self-doubt; he couldn't
trust that he would do the right thing if faced with danger again, or even in everyday decisions.

respite; he had trouble falling or staying asleep, his nights often punctuated by restless tossing
and turning, or waking suddenly from vivid, disquieting images.

Samuel had come to believe that he was no longer capable of doing the things that defined him. The
man who could fix anything, who was fearless in the face of complex plumbing emergencies, felt
diminished. He saw his hesitation to take jobs as a failure, a sign that the assault had irrevocably
broken something within him. What happened to him felt deeply unfair, a cruel twist of fate for
which nothing had been done; the police investigation had yielded no arrests, leaving him with a
profound sense of injustice. The world felt like a more dangerous, unpredictable place, and he,

seek help. He wasn't sure what he hoped for, perhaps just to understand why he couldn't shake the
feeling that he was perpetually on the brink of another catastrophe, or why the guilt refused to
