accidental drowning, but the presence of an empty whiskey bottle found near the wreckage added a
bitter, confusing layer to her grief. Leilani remembers the frantic phone calls, the growing dread,
and the hollow ache of hearing the news, yet she found herself unable to construct a clear, coherent
story of what happened, even to herself. It was as if the events had shattered into countless sharp
fragments, none of which truly connected.

In the months that followed, these fragments refused to stay buried. Memories of the frightening
event were like a film that played over and over in her mind – the sight of the Coast Guard vessel
returning, the grim faces of the officers, the chilling smell of salt and diesel fuel on Kaimana’s
recovered gear. These images would force their way into her mind when she least expected them, often
when she was trying to focus on managing her shop. The scent of alcohol, even a faint whiff from a
customer’s breath or a spilled drink, could send a jolt of panic through her, immediately
transporting her back to the discovery of that bottle. Conversations in the shop about local
tragedies or people losing loved ones became unbearable; her heart would pound, her breath would
catch, and she would feel an overwhelming wave of grief that felt physically crushing.

docks, waiting for news, the cold fear gripping her chest.
