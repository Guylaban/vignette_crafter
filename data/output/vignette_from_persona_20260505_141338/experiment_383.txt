repeatedly left her feeling exposed and that the system itself couldn't fully grasp the insidious
nature of her fear.

In the aftermath, even after the man was apprehended and a restraining order issued, Thuy found that
the world no longer felt as it once did. The deep-seated conviction that she could not trust safety
had taken root, an unshakeable belief that persisted even when her devoted husband, David, or her
colleagues assured her she was now protected. The very notion of being truly safe felt like a naive
fantasy she could no longer afford.

was always lurking just out of sight.

vulnerability, reminding her of the period when she felt exposed and unprotected despite their
involvement.

furniture so her chair in the corner faced the door. During patient consultations, she frequently
found herself glancing towards the operatory door, even though it was always kept closed.

heavy toll. Her once vibrant social life dwindled as she avoided restaurants and public gatherings

began to suffer slightly; the mental energy dedicated to threat monitoring meant she was less
present, more distracted by her internal scanning. She had begun to decline invitations to

was to reclaim the quiet peace of mind she had lost, to believe, even just a little, that she could
be truly safe again.
