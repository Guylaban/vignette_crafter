inability to intervene or stop what was happening.

In the immediate aftermath, Michael struggled profoundly to articulate what he had seen. When
speaking to the police, and later to his wife, Sarah, he found himself unable to find the words to
describe the sequence of events. He knew exactly what had happened, the horrific details etched into
his mind, yet the narrative felt fractured and jumbled, a confused sequence of images that refused
to form a coherent story. Even now, a year later, the memory would often ambush him, feeling
terrifyingly like he was reliving the event right there in the present moment, complete with the
sounds, the smells, and the sickening nausea.

world around him. He had come to believe that there was something inherently wrong with him, an

happening to him or those he cared about in the future. He began to feel increasingly isolated and
set apart from others, convinced that no one could possibly understand the horror he carried within
him.

see the entire room, a habit that became second nature. He was constantly on edge, feeling jumpy and
easily startled by sudden noises or movements, whether it was a car backfiring on the street or a
colleague unexpectedly entering his office.

of anxiety through him. The faint smell of blood, or even certain metallic or acrid odors that

clenching with familiar nausea. This constant state of alert began to take a toll on his work. He
found himself avoiding public transport, opting for long, expensive taxi rides, and struggling to
focus on projects involving public spaces, constantly distracted by his need to assess threats. His

irritability. Sarah noticed his withdrawal, his jumpiness, and his inability to enjoy their usual

Michael finally sought help after a particularly vivid flashback during a family dinner, triggered
by a news report about a street fight. The nausea and sense of reliving the event were so

isolation. He hoped for some way to regain control, to stop the bad things from happening, and to
feel less like a stranger in his own life.
