the sudden sound momentarily diverted his attention. In that split second, a pedestrian stepped into
the crosswalk. The screech of tires, the sickening thud, and the sight of the person lying
motionless on the asphalt seared themselves into his mind. He remembered the metallic tang of blood
in the air, the acrid smell of burnt rubber, and the blare of sirens as emergency services arrived.

agonizing testament to his momentary lapse. Chris was certain that the event happened because of the
way he acted, a belief that gnawed at him relentlessly, convincing him he couldn't trust that he
would ever do the right thing again.

Since that night, Chris’s world had shrunk, overshadowed by a constant sense of dread and guilt.
Memories of the accident would ambush him without warning, feeling less like recollections and more
like a full sensory reliving. He would be back in that moment, the sounds, the smells, the horrific
visual details assaulting him as if it were happening right then, not something that was over and
done with. When he tried to articulate these experiences, the words would fail him; he knew what
happened but was unable to find words to describe it, leaving him feeling isolated and
misunderstood.

the parking lot, could instantly transport him back to the scene of the accident, triggering the
same intense fear, horror, and profound shame.
