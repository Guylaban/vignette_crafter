happened. When she tried to explain the event to her husband or to colleagues, she would lose her
train of thought, the words escaping her. It was as if the frightening event couldn't quite be put
into words; she could feel the sensations, the panic, but deliberately recalling the sequence of
events felt like trying to grasp smoke. This difficulty talking about the event itself became a
source of profound distress, reinforcing her isolation.

construction site, would jolt her into a state of high alert, her heart pounding as if she were back

aisle or a public building, would trigger an intense wave of fear, a certainty that they posed a
threat.

became a constant source of anxiety. The unpredictability of her daily tasks, of sudden policy
changes, or even an unscheduled meeting, would leave her feeling overwhelmed and on edge. To cope,
Emily developed a meticulous habit of rehearsing what to say before any interaction, whether it was
a simple phone call to a constituent or a discussion with a supervisor. This made her seem stiff and
often slowed down her responses, impacting her team's workflow. She also found herself increasingly
avoiding any form of physical contact with others. A friendly pat on the shoulder from a colleague
or her husband's casual touch would make her flinch, pulling away instinctively, though she couldn't
explain why.

The memory of the chemical exposure also left her with a profound aversion to anything medical. She
began actively avoiding medical cues, changing the channel if an ambulance siren was heard on the
television, and postponing her annual physical, despite her husband’s concerns. The thought of
hospitals, ambulances, or any physical examination would trigger a wave of nausea and panic,
bringing back the helplessness she felt during the leak.

The cumulative weight of these changes eventually became too much to bear. Emily’s once-vibrant life
had narrowed to a tight circle of fear and avoidance. Her husband had grown distant, confused by her
constant flinching and the emotional wall she had erected. Her work performance, while still
competent, lacked the spontaneous engagement it once had. What finally prompted her to seek help was
a particularly difficult day at the office where an unexpected, loud fire drill sent her into a
full-blown panic attack, leaving her unable to function for the rest of the day. She knew then that
she couldn't continue living trapped by these invisible threats, constantly on guard, unable to
truly connect with the world or the people she loved. She hoped to find a way back to a life where
she didn't feel perpetually unsafe, where the past didn't dictate every present moment.
