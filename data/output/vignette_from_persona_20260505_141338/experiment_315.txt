certainty began to unravel one frigid evening two years ago. Walking home after a late trial, he had

ensuing chaos was a blur of flashing lights, agitated commands, and "incomprehensible chatter" from
both the officers and the escalating crowd. In the confusion, perhaps too close to the fray, David
found himself roughly pushed against a wall, his pleas to identify himself ignored amidst the
cacophony. The sheer force, the casual disregard for his person by the very "authority presence" he
respected and worked alongside daily, was a profound shock. He was quickly released once his
identity was established, but the violation of his personal space and his sense of order, the unjust
application of force, left an indelible mark.

In the weeks and months that followed, the world began to feel subtly but profoundly altered. The

assault that could send a jolt of anxiety through him. At home, he found himself increasingly drawn
to "sensory distraction," keeping the television blaring or loud classical music on, not for
enjoyment, but to drown out the quiet, the thoughts, the echoes of that night. His wife, Maria,

their conversations to ask, "Are you sure everything's alright? Do you think we're safe?"—a
persistent "reassurance seeking" that spoke to a deep, unvoiced fear.

"checking surroundings" was relentless, a habit that had bled into every aspect of his life, from
grocery stores to family gatherings.

insisted he seek help. He sat in the waiting room, still "scanning his environment," wondering if he
could ever truly feel safe again, or if this relentless watchfulness would be his permanent state.
