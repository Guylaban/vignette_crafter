by his past as a relief worker in a war zone decades ago. Now, in the structured world of data

village where he had been trapped, unable to intervene as a small group of armed men, ostensibly
there to 'maintain order,' engaged in what he could only describe as abhorrent misbehavior towards
the local populace. He remembered the suffocating weight of knowing there were no options for him,
no way to stop what was happening, his pleas falling on deaf ears. That feeling of entrapment,
coupled with the profound moral violation he witnessed, had etched itself into his very being.

These memories were not distant echoes; they felt like they were happening right now, overwhelming
him with a visceral sense of reliving the event. Often, he would wake with a jolt, heart pounding,
from nightmares that were merely fragmented replays of that specific horror. The dream residue clung
to him, making the transition back to the present day a disorienting struggle. He had come to
believe that his intense reactions, the way his body would seize up with fear and his mind would
race, meant that he was slowly going crazy, a terrifying prospect for a man who prided himself on
his intellectual acuity. What frightened him most, however, was a growing fear of his own rage; the
powerful, negative emotions of anger and horror that surged through him felt barely contained, and
he worried he would not be able to control them, doing something terrible. The world, in his
perception, was an inherently dangerous place, a belief reinforced by every news report he
inadvertently caught.

This conviction shaped his daily life. His body felt constantly on alert, a perpetual tension in his
shoulders and a tightness in his chest, ready to react to danger that might appear from anywhere.

surroundings, his eyes darting from the doorway to the faces of colleagues, assessing every
movement, every sound. He would meticulously rehearse what to say before any interaction, whether it
was a simple query from a junior analyst or a team meeting, creating intricate scripts in his mind,
fearing he might say the wrong thing or appear unprepared in a world he perceived as unforgiving.

the vivid, present-tense replays of his own past.

his age and expertise, sometimes found him distant, his rehearsed conversations lacking spontaneity.

the data on screen. The avoidance of news had left him feeling increasingly disconnected from
current events, making casual social interactions more challenging and reinforcing a sense of

What finally prompted Bao to seek help was a particularly harrowing series of nightmares, leaving

relentless film playing in his mind, hoping to quell the fear that he was succumbing to madness, and
to find a way to navigate a world that, despite his best efforts, still felt overwhelmingly
dangerous.
