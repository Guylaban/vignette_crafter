hospital, and the doctor’s grim face was etched into Mateo’s mind. What haunted him most was the
burning conviction that he should have been there, that he had failed to protect Juan. This belief
became a relentless internal torment, a constant whisper of his inadequacy.

their condolences, a strange, unsettling sensation washed over him. He felt as though he was

This dissociative feeling, a terrifying sense of not quite being present, began to recur. It would
creep up on him during his shifts, particularly in the quiet, reflective moments between cell

that night.

To cope, Mateo developed a rigid system of avoidance. Anything that reminded him of someone he had
