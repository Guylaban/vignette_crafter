environment where he continued his practice. The sudden chime of a medical alert, the sight of a
child being wheeled into emergency, or even the worried cadence in a parent’s voice would instantly
snap him to attention, his heart pounding, his senses heightened as if danger lurked just around the
corner. He experienced intense fear and horror, particularly when he would suddenly feel or act as

seated guilt, a persistent whisper that as a doctor, he should have known, should have prevented it,
even though rationally he understood the illness was beyond his control.

To cope, Ricardo developed a rigorous system of mental defense. He tried hard not to think about the

consequences. His social life, already sparse since his divorce years ago, dwindled further. He
found it difficult to form new connections, always fearing that getting close to someone might

exemplary clinician, his colleagues noticed his increasing distance. He was always working, always

between himself and the world, perpetually on high alert, forever guarding against an unseen threat.

potential for catastrophe, perpetually unable to outrun the echoes of Sofia’s illness. What

from the shadow of that terrifying time.
