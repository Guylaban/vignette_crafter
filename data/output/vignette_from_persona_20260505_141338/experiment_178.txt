Minh, a 27-year-old journalist, had always considered himself resilient, but the past year had
eroded that belief, leaving him feeling adrift and profoundly unsafe. The turning point came during
a bitter argument with his then-boyfriend, a man Minh had loved deeply and trusted implicitly. The
fight had escalated quickly, not in their apartment, but on a late-night bus they were taking home
after a social gathering. What started as raised voices turned physical, with his partner grabbing
him, shoving him against the window, and eventually striking him repeatedly. The memory of the bus
rattling, the muffled sounds of the city outside, and the sudden, sharp pain in his ribs and head
was not a distant recollection for Minh; when it returned, it felt like it was happening right now,
the metallic taste of fear and the sting of blows as vivid as if they were fresh. He would often
find himself gasping for air, overwhelmed by emotion, and the physical pain in his ribs and the back
of his head would flare up, mirroring the injuries he sustained that night.

Since then, Minh’s world had shrunk, governed by a pervasive sense of dread. He found it impossible
to trust the assurances of safety from others, even his current partner, Lena, who was kind and
patient. He had come to believe that you could never truly know who will harm you, and a deep
mistrust of people had taken root within him. His work as a journalist, which often required him to
be out in the field, had become a minefield. The simple act of riding a bus, a necessity for many of
his assignments covering local stories, would send a jolt of panic through him. The confined space,
the rumble of the engine, the feeling of being trapped – it all brought back the terror of that
night. He would often find himself taking circuitous routes on foot or paying for expensive
rideshares, burning through his meager savings just to avoid public transport, even if it meant
missing deadlines.

Another profound challenge was being alone with men. His abusive partner had been a man, and now,
even professional interactions became fraught. Interviewing male sources for his articles, a common
part of his job, was particularly difficult. He’d find excuses to bring a colleague or insist on
meeting in public, bustling places, often making his editors frustrated with his perceived
inflexibility. He was certain that his fear of harm was justified, and his internal refrain was a
constant echo: "People can't be trusted." This belief also led him to constantly dwell on what he
might have done wrong, replaying the events of that night in his head, searching for a fault in
himself that could explain what happened. He was inadequate, he concluded, otherwise, he wouldn’t
have been vulnerable to such an attack.

Minh’s vigilance extended into every corner of his life. If Lena was late coming home, or if an
editor didn't respond to an email as quickly as expected, a cold knot of fear would tighten in his
stomach. He would assume something bad was happening, his mind racing through catastrophic
scenarios. Sleep offered no respite. He would wake frequently during the night, his heart pounding,
needing to check whether he was safe, scanning the room, listening intently to the quiet hum of the
apartment. He found himself suppressing any flicker of anger, terrified of its potential to
escalate, convinced that allowing himself to feel it would only lead to more pain or conflict. This
self-control, however, felt less like strength and more like a cage. He had also started restricting
his food intake, finding that undereating offered a strange sense of control in a world that felt
utterly unpredictable. It was a silent, desperate way of coping, a small act of defiance against the
chaos he felt internally. The weight loss was noticeable, and Lena had started to express concern,
which only added to his feeling of inadequacy.

It was Lena's quiet persistence, her gentle pleas for him to talk to someone, combined with the
growing difficulty of simply doing his job, that finally brought Minh to seek help. He was tired of
the constant fear, the physical pain that accompanied his memories, and the overwhelming sense that
he was fundamentally broken. What frightened him most was the thought of living like this forever,
trapped in a loop of fear and self-blame, unable to trust, unable to truly live. He hoped that
somehow, he could find a way back to a sense of safety, a way to reclaim the life that had been so
violently taken from him.
