hospital visits, the sterile, antiseptic smell of the wards clinging to her clothes and hair, a
scent that became synonymous with dread.

helplessness as she witnessed Layla’s vibrant spirit slowly diminish. Despite her professional
background in advocating for others, she felt utterly powerless to stop the progression of the
illness. She tried everything, researched every possible treatment, but the disease ultimately
claimed Layla. In the aftermath, Zahra became certain that she couldn't stop bad things from
happening to her, a belief that settled deep within her bones. She had dedicated her life to helping
others, to finding solutions and supporting the vulnerable, yet she couldn't save her own child.
This failure felt like a profound betrayal of her core values, a sense that she had acted in ways
that went against who she was. She had come to believe that she had permanently changed for the
worse, that the joyful, hopeful woman she once was had been irrevocably broken.

if it were yesterday, even without a clear, detailed picture in her mind. Other times, the full
