his hands. Married and living in the bustling suburbs, his life had seemed stable until a

around him. The memory of the event was not a clear narrative, but a collection of vivid, terrifying
fragments. When the memories came back, they weren't just images; it felt completely real across all
his senses at once—the acrid dust filling his lungs, the terrifying groan of twisting metal, and the
distant, muffled screams that sometimes he still heard clearly in his head. He found he could not
put the memories of the event in the right order, making it impossible to fully grasp what had
happened, leaving him with a constant, unsettling sense of disorientation.

In the aftermath, Karim had tried desperately to regain a sense of normalcy, forcing himself to
think only positive thoughts, but this strategy felt like a thin veneer over a simmering rage. He
found himself consumed by rumination, a burning desire to get even with those he felt were
responsible for the shoddy construction, the slow emergency response, and the systemic failures that
had turned a natural disaster into an even greater catastrophe. He was certain that somebody else
would not have gotten into this situation, feeling a profound sense of self-blame and shame that
gnawed at him. He used to be a happy person, but now he was always miserable, convinced that nothing
good could happen to him anymore.

Over the years, the world had become a minefield of potential threats. Karim’s software engineering
office, with its open-plan layout and constant movement, was a particular challenge. If a colleague

danger. He found himself constantly scanning his surroundings, whether in his office, a restaurant,
or even his own home, always identifying escape routes before he felt comfortable anywhere, a habit
born from the terrifying feeling of being trapped under tons of rubble. His wife, who had been a

unexpectedly, even a gentle hand on his shoulder, he would react with alarm, pulling away sharply.
This made intimacy difficult, and Karim often felt a profound sadness about the growing distance
between them.

His avoidance behaviors had also solidified into rigid routines. The sight of an ambulance siren,
the distinct smell of a hospital, or even the thought of a routine physical examination would send a
wave of panic through him. He had come to associate all medical cues with the overwhelming scenes of
chaos, injury, and helplessness he had witnessed in the earthquake's immediate aftermath. He avoided
doctor's appointments, even when he felt unwell, preferring to endure discomfort rather than face
environments that re-triggered his deepest fears.

Karim’s colleagues noticed his increasing withdrawal and jumpiness, though he offered no
explanation. His focus at work suffered, his productivity dipping as his mind was constantly
preoccupied with scanning for threats or replaying fragments of the disaster. The accumulated toll
on his marriage, his work, and his own internal world had become unbearable. The constant vigilance
was exhausting, and the pervasive sense that nothing good could happen to him anymore was
suffocating. It was this creeping despair, coupled with his wife's quiet concern, that finally led
him to seek help, hoping to find a way to escape the relentless grip of a past that refused to stay
buried.
