Samuel, a distinguished 77-year-old African American man, had dedicated his life to education,
culminating in a fulfilling career as a school principal in his vibrant community. His measured
demeanor and deep commitment to his students were hallmarks of his leadership, qualities that had
served him well for decades. However, a harrowing night six months prior had profoundly reshaped his
world, leading him to seek help for the unyielding sense of unease that now pervaded his every
waking moment.

The traumatic event occurred in his own home, a place that had always represented security and
peace. One evening, two intruders had forced their way in, demanding valuables. Samuel, caught off
guard, remembered the sickeningly sweet, acrid smell of alcohol emanating from one of the men as
they pushed him, tied his hands, and ransacked his belongings. The feeling of being utterly
helpless, of being forced to comply with their demands and having his autonomy stripped away, was a
profound violation that lingered long after they had fled. He replayed the incident in his mind
constantly, often overwhelmed by a deep, aching grief for the sense of safety he had lost.
Sometimes, when these memories surfaced, he could vividly hear the intruders’ hushed, urgent voices
echoing in his head, the terrifying sounds of his home being violated. At other times, a strange
numbness would descend, and he would recall the events as if watching a scene unfold on a screen,
detached and emotionless, as though it had happened to a stranger.

In the months that followed, Samuel found himself increasingly on edge. The smell of alcohol, even a
faint whiff from a passing stranger, could send a jolt of fear through him, instantly transporting
him back to that night. The sensation of a sudden, unexpected movement nearby, a student rushing
past in the hallway or a colleague gesturing abruptly, would cause him to react as if he were in
immediate danger, his heart pounding in his chest. He felt jumpy and easily startled, a stark
contrast to his usually calm disposition. This heightened sense of threat had transformed his daily
routine. As soon as he entered any room or building, whether it was his own office, the school
auditorium for an assembly, or even a local grocery store, his eyes would instinctively scan for
exits, assessing potential escape routes, a habit he couldn't seem to break. He was always looking
for a way out, always prepared for the worst.

These changes were accompanied by a growing internal narrative of self-reproach and distrust. Samuel
had come to believe that the event had happened because of the way he had acted, endlessly replaying
alternative scenarios in his mind, convinced he could have done something differently. A pervasive
sense that he could no longer rely on himself, that his own judgment or ability to protect himself
had failed, gnawed at him. This extended to others; he felt a profound inability to rely on other
people, a belief that left him feeling isolated and vulnerable.

The consequences of these shifts began to manifest in his professional life. His constant vigilance
and internal preoccupation made it difficult to fully engage in large school gatherings. He started
avoiding parent-teacher conferences in smaller, more enclosed offices, preferring to meet in larger,
more open spaces or delegating these meetings to his assistant principal whenever possible. This was
a significant change for Samuel, who had always prided himself on his direct involvement with the
school community. His staff noticed he seemed more distracted, his gaze often drifting, and his
formerly warm, approachable demeanor was now tinged with an underlying tension. He found himself
retreating, spending more time in his office with the door ajar, feeling a desperate need to
maintain a sense of control over his immediate environment. This increasing withdrawal and his
pervasive sense of distrust in others had strained some of his professional relationships, making
collaboration feel like an immense effort.

Samuel’s wife, observing his growing isolation and constant anxiety, gently urged him to seek help.
He arrived at the clinic feeling exhausted by the relentless vigilance and the internal battle
against his own thoughts. What frightened him most was the possibility that he might never feel
truly safe again, that the man he once was, the principal who commanded respect and felt secure in
his world, was irrevocably lost. He yearned for relief from the constant threat and the gnawing
self-blame, hoping to reclaim some semblance of his former self and the peace he once knew.
