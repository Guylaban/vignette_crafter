precise and his gaze watchful. His journey to this point began just over a year ago, during what was
meant to be a routine diplomatic visit to a conflict zone. He had been walking through a bustling

deafening explosion ripped through the street. The world became a blur of dust, screams, and the
acrid smell of burning debris. He remembered being thrown violently, landing hard near an overturned
vendor’s stall, where a bottle of cheap, potent alcohol had shattered, its cloying scent mixing with
the metallic tang of fear. In that moment, surrounded by chaos and unable to move, he felt an
overwhelming, terrifying vulnerability, utterly exposed and helpless as the sounds of the aftermath
unfolded around him.

Even now, the memory of that day remains remarkably vivid for Rohan. He can recall the exact colour
of the sky, the texture of the dust in his mouth, and the distinct, sickening aroma of that spilled
alcohol. Sometimes, when he thinks about it, he can actually smell it again, as if transported back.
Yet, when asked to articulate the full horror of what he witnessed, his words often fail him; he
knows what happened, the images are clear, but he finds himself unable to find the words to describe
it adequately, the narrative fragmenting into disconnected sensory details.

Since returning home, Rohan has found his life increasingly constrained by the shadow of that event.

the embassy, can instantly plunge him back into the terror, his heart pounding as if the stressful
experience were happening again. He’s become exquisitely sensitive to certain smells; the faintest
whiff of alcohol, even from a colleague’s drink at a diplomatic reception, can trigger a wave of
nausea and panic, making him desperately want to flee. But what truly haunts him is the persistent
feeling of vulnerability. He struggles to believe in his own safety, even when his partner reassures
him, or when he is in the secure confines of the embassy. He has come to believe that even in
ordinary situations, strangers approaching him pose an inherent threat, their intentions unknowable
and potentially dangerous.

This pervasive sense of threat has driven Rohan to adopt a series of coping mechanisms. He started
wearing heavier, more concealing clothing, even in warm weather, hoping to blend in, to become less

racing thoughts and help him sleep, he began taking drugs, initially just marijuana, but it
gradually became a nightly ritual, a way to numb the edges of his anxiety. He also throws himself
into compulsive exercise, pushing his body to exhaustion at the gym every evening. It's a way to
feel in control, to channel the restless energy, and to physically tire himself out enough that he
might get a few hours of sleep before the nightmares begin.

constant state of hypervigilance and fear. What truly torments him is the conviction that there is

further.

The cumulative effect of these changes has begun to impact his life significantly. His partner has

certainty of a bleak future, some relief might be possible. Yet, he remains deeply skeptical,
struggling to believe that anyone or anything can truly make him feel safe again.
