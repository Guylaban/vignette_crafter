nets a familiar comfort. But that changed one pre-dawn morning as she cycled towards the docks, the

screech of tires disappearing into the distance, was etched into her mind. She froze, unable to
move, too far away to do anything but witness the horrific scene unfold. The child lay motionless,

washing over her, before the distant wail of sirens began to cut through the silence.

Since then, the world had become a minefield. The memory of the accident forced its way into her
mind at unpredictable times, often when she was trying to focus on her work, mending nets or
navigating the boat. Sometimes it would arrive as a full, vivid replay, the child’s small form, the
twisted bicycle, the vanishing taillights. Other times, it was more abstract, a sudden, overwhelming
wave of emotion – pure terror, grief, or that searing guilt – without any clear image attached. When
she did remember the event, an intense, crushing guilt would flood her, the thought "I should have
protected others and I failed" echoing relentlessly in her head.

time to think, a primal alarm system constantly misfiring. The sight of a bicycle, even one leaning
innocently against a wall, or certain related sounds – the faint screech of tires, a distant car
horn, even the specific clang of metal – would trigger an instant surge of adrenaline, making her
scan her surroundings frantically, convinced something bad was happening again. She often felt a
profound sense of betrayal by her own physiology, believing, "My body keeps reacting as if I am
still in danger even when I am safe."

immediately assume something bad was happening, her imagination conjuring the worst possible
scenarios.

To cope, Leilani had developed new habits. She began to overwork, volunteering for extra shifts,

intrusive thoughts that plagued her when she was alone. When she was off work, she spent long hours
on her phone, playing games or scrolling the internet, using the digital world as a shield against
her inner turmoil. This screen escape became her primary way to avoid thinking, a desperate attempt

felt trapped in a constant cycle of fear and guilt, desperately hoping to find a way to quiet the
relentless alarms in her body and mind, and to stop believing that she was inherently flawed and
dangerous.
