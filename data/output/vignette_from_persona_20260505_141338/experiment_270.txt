beneath that calm surface, an old storm raged. His journey to America had been one of desperate
flight as a young man, caught in the brutal upheaval of displacement in his homeland. He could not
put the frightening events into words, not truly. Instead, his memory of that time was a collection
of disconnected pieces that never quite fit into a coherent narrative. He would recall the acrid
smell of burning fields, the stifling heat of an overcrowded truck, the terrified whimpers of a
child, and the sight of a small, hastily dug grave by the roadside. These fragments, unlike any
ordinary memory, felt more real, more vivid, as if they were happening right now, the sensations
still sharp decades later.

The trauma had etched itself into his daily existence. The sudden blare of a car horn or the squeal
of tires on the road outside his pottery studio could instantly transport him back to the frantic
escape, the constant fear of pursuit. His heart would pound, his breath catch, and his hands,
usually so steady on the wheel, would tremble uncontrollably. He had come to believe he had to be
especially careful because you never knew what could happen next, a deep-seated certainty that kept
him in a state of constant vigilance. This meant that even in the familiar comfort of his studio,
surrounded by his creations, he would constantly scan his environment for any signs of threat, his
eyes darting to the windows, his ears straining for unusual sounds.

situations that might make him feel happy or hopeful. Invitations to family celebrations, especially
weddings or birth announcements, were politely declined, not because he didn't care, but because the
surge of positive emotion felt foreign, almost dangerous, a vulnerability he couldn't afford. The
thought of attending a funeral or visiting a cemetery filled him with a profound dread, not just of
grief, but of the raw, visceral memories of loss and unmarked graves that would inevitably
resurface. He would make excuses, feeling a deep pang of regret, yet certain it was for the best to
protect himself from the overwhelming feelings.

intricate, ambitious pieces that used to define his skill, believing deep down that he was no longer
capable of doing the things that truly defined him as an artist. This conviction was reinforced by
his perceived inability to deal with even the slightest upset. A minor flaw in a pot, a small
disagreement with his wife, or a delayed supply order would send him into a spiral of self-doubt and
anxious rumination, confirming his belief that his coping reserves were utterly depleted.

This pervasive sense of fragility and the need for control permeated his marriage to his wife,
Kamala. She had tried to talk to him about his withdrawal, his increasing quietness, but he would
quickly shut down any potential disagreement. He had learned to avoid conflict at all costs, fearing
that even a small argument could unravel his fragile composure, proving his appraisal of "low
coping" correct. Kamala, seeing his distress, had gently but firmly encouraged him to seek help. It
was her quiet persistence, combined with a recent, particularly vivid flashback triggered by seeing

He came to therapy, not with hope, but with a weary resignation, primarily because he couldn't bear
to see the worry in his wife's eyes any longer, and a quiet, desperate wish for some respite from
the storm within.
