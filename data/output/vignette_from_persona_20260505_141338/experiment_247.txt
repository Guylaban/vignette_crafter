terrifying images without clear beginnings or ends. She remembered the sheer terror of being
separated from her friends, the desperate scramble for safety amidst a stampede of fleeing people,
and the overwhelming feeling of helplessness. When snippets of the chaos did surface, they often
brought with them an overwhelming wave of rage – a visceral anger at the senselessness, the
violation of safety, the sheer brutality. She knew what had happened, the terror she felt, but
trying to articulate it to anyone, even herself, felt like trying to grasp smoke. The words simply
wouldn't come. There were parts of the event that she simply could not remember, no matter how hard
she tried to reconstruct them.

The world she re-entered felt fundamentally altered. Where once there was a sense of normalcy, now
Isabella perceived a pervasive, lurking threat. She had come to believe, with a chilling certainty,
that the world was a dangerous place, and that she had to be on guard all the time. This constant
vigilance was exhausting. Nights became particularly oppressive; the darkness seemed to amplify her
vulnerability, making it harder to feel safe than during the day. Even months later, the sudden
clang of a dropped pallet or the abrupt movement of a coworker would make Isabella jump, her heart
hammering in her chest. Any sudden movements by others put her immediately on guard, her body
tensing instinctively, a feeling of being jumpy or easily startled becoming her new normal. Her own
body, once a dependable vessel for work and life, now felt like a fragile, untrustworthy thing,
prone to involuntary reactions and a constant state of readiness for danger. She could no longer
trust it to keep her safe or even to function normally under stress.

At the busy warehouse where she worked, Isabella found herself constantly scanning her environment
for signs of threat. Her eyes darted from the loading dock to the aisles, from the forklifts to the
faces of her colleagues, searching for anything amiss, any precursor to danger. This hyper-vigilance
often manifested in subtle ways. She started making less eye contact with other people, finding
direct gazes too intense, too revealing, or perhaps too confrontational in a world she now viewed as
hostile. When the noise and constant motion of the warehouse became overwhelming, or when a memory

mentally retreating to a safe, empty space within herself, even if it meant losing track of her
tasks for a moment.

These strategies, while offering momentary relief from the relentless fear, began to isolate her.
Her productivity at work sometimes suffered, and colleagues started to notice her withdrawn
demeanor, interpreting it as unfriendliness rather than terror. Her social life dwindled as she
avoided crowded places and the unpredictable nature of social interaction. The relentless exhaustion
of being constantly on guard, coupled with the growing chasm between her and her former self,
finally prompted Isabella to seek help. She felt trapped in a perpetual state of fear, yearning for
a return to a life where she didn't have to fight just to exist, but she was deeply afraid that such
a life might no longer be possible for her.
