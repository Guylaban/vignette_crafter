Linh, a 48-year-old woman, found her world irrevocably altered after a terrifying period of stalking
that culminated in an attempted home invasion a year and a half ago. She had been living alone since
her divorce, working long shifts as a waitress in a bustling diner, and cherishing the quiet
independence she’d built. The stalking began subtly – a repeated car on her street, a familiar face
in the diner more often than coincidental, then escalating to notes left on her car and whispered
threats through her mailbox. The night of the attempted break-in, she awoke to the sound of her back
door being tampered with, the muffled thuds echoing through her small home. She remembers the sheer,
primal terror, the feeling of being hunted in her own sanctuary. Though the police eventually
intervened and the individual was apprehended, the memory of that night, and the months preceding
it, refused to recede.

Now, the memory of the event itself often forces its way into Linh’s mind when she least expects it.
It comes in vivid, fragmented flashes – the glint of a tool by the door, the sickening creak, the
racing of her own heart – but she can’t seem to put the frightening event into words. When she tries
to recount it, the details blur, leaving her feeling frustrated and disoriented, as if the memory
feels like it belongs to someone else rather than to her. This sense of detachment has seeped into
her very being. Linh often feels dead inside, a profound emotional numbness that has replaced the
vibrant energy she once knew. She used to be a happy person, finding joy in simple moments, but now
she is always miserable, convinced that nothing in her life feels meaningful anymore.

Her home, once a refuge, has become a source of constant vigilance. Every evening, after locking her
doors, she listens intently for sounds that might mean someone is entering her home. A car door
slamming outside, the rustle of leaves against the window, even the settling of her old house, can
trigger a jolt of fear, her heart immediately beginning to race. These strong physical reactions, a
tightening in her chest and a surge of adrenaline, are a constant companion when anything, however
innocuous, reminds her of the stressful experience.

Linh’s job as a waitress often requires her to navigate a busy environment, but she finds herself
increasingly restricted. She avoids going to unfamiliar places, even new sections of the diner or
new routes home, because she feels she cannot assess the danger. Every new face is a potential
threat, every darkened corner a hiding spot. This pervasive fear has led her to withdraw
significantly. She now actively avoids seeing people she socialised with before the event, worried
they might notice the change in her or, worse, that her past interactions somehow made her
vulnerable. She often declines invitations from former colleagues, making excuses about being tired,
even though she misses the camaraderie.

The constant tension has made her deeply wary of any disagreement. She avoids any situation that
might involve conflict or confrontation, whether it's a difficult customer complaining about their
order or a minor disagreement with a co-worker. It’s as if any form of contention feels like an open
invitation for things to spiral out of her control, mirroring the powerlessness she felt during the
stalking. She also finds herself avoiding paying attention to or looking at her own body. She feels
a profound disconnect from her physical self, as if her body itself is a site of vulnerability and
unwanted attention, a vessel that betrayed her by being a target. She avoids mirrors, dresses in
loose, unremarkable clothing, and quickly dismisses any physical sensations, preferring to remain
numb.

The cumulative effect of these changes has been profound. Her once-active social life has dwindled
to almost nothing, and her relationships with her adult children have become strained as she
struggles to explain her withdrawal and pervasive unhappiness. The joy she once found in cooking or
gardening has vanished, leaving her days feeling hollow. It was this creeping sense of isolation and
the realization that she couldn't remember the last time she truly smiled, that finally prompted
Linh to seek help, hoping to find a way back to the person she used to be, or at least a path to
living without constant fear.
