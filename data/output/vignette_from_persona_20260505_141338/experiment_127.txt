her throat. Even now, when a memory of the incident returns, that distinct taste floods her mouth,
sharp and unwelcome, making her gag.

The aftermath of the accident left Linh with a profound sense of horror and responsibility. She
often found herself replaying the event, but the memory was fragmented, a disorienting collection of
flashes and sounds. She felt like she was only remembering parts of what happened – the sickening
crunch, the sudden scream, the pool of red – and the rest was missing, leaving a terrifying void in
her understanding. This incomplete picture only intensified her agony. When these fragments
surfaced, an intense, crushing guilt would wash over her, a deep conviction that she had done
something unforgivable. She would dwell on what she had done wrong, replaying hypothetical
scenarios, convinced that she should have noticed something, checked something, prevented it.

comfort, became unbearable. She started actively avoiding other people, eating her lunch alone in

workmates. She tried desperately to avoid thinking about the event, pushing the images and the self-
blame away, but they were relentless. She would distract herself with tasks, focusing intensely on
the smallest details of her current work, only for a sudden spark or the clang of heavy metal to rip
her back.

hold her torch steady. These episodes left her shaken and exhausted, further eroding her confidence
in her abilities. At night, the quiet offered no respite. Linh found herself struggling to fall

relentlessly. Even if she managed to drift off, she would wake frequently, her body tense, unable to
find sustained rest.

creating a vicious cycle.
