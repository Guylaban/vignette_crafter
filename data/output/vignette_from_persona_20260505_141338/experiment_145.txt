helicopters descending, the distinct, acrid smell of burning thatch and wood filling the air, and

beneath the surface of her life.

For years, Linh had carried the weight of that day in silence. She had learned early on that talking
about the event or its aftermath only brought a fresh wave of overwhelming emotions, not just for
her but for those around her who either hadn't been there or didn't want to revisit the past. She
had come to believe it was safer, for everyone, to simply keep it locked away. This avoidance
extended to any situation that made her feel strong emotions, whether joy or sorrow, leading her to
create a carefully constructed emotional distance in her relationships. When the intrusive images or
the crushing anxiety became too much, a desperate, private ritual would begin: she would hurt

desperate anchor, pulling her back from the precipice of her memories. It was a secret she guarded
fiercely, a desperate coping mechanism she was deeply ashamed of.

Over time, a persistent sense of threat became her constant companion. She felt threatened by
strangers approaching her, even in the most ordinary situations. A person walking too quickly behind
her on the street, a new face in the office corridor, or someone simply reaching for an item near
her in a grocery store aisle could send a jolt of fear through her. Her body would react instantly,
a surge of adrenaline, her heart pounding, her breath catching in her throat. This hypervigilance
had manifested into a habit she couldn't break: she frequently looked behind her, scanning her
surroundings, checking if someone was following her, a constant, exhausting internal patrol.

system could transport her back to that day in the village office. The smell of burnt toast from the
breakroom, the faint scent of exhaust from the street outside, or a faulty component giving off a
faint electrical tang, would instantly conjure the terrifying odor of burning. And the sound of
helicopters or other aircraft overhead, a common occurrence in the city, would send a wave of icy
dread through her, her body tensing, ready to flee or freeze.

missing details, and her colleagues sometimes perceived her as distant or preoccupied. She would

frightened her most was the thought that she might never truly escape that burning village, that she
might never feel safe, even in her own skin.
