become fraught with anxiety. Any sexual situation would trigger an almost unbearable distress,
making him pull away, often without explanation, leaving a chasm between them.

To cope with the intrusive thoughts and overwhelming feelings, Omar had developed a series of
internal strategies. When memories of the event would resurface, he would mentally step outside
himself, pretending it happened to someone else, narrating it in his mind as if he were a distant

for signs of danger or injury, a hypervigilance that belied his attempts at avoidance. A new ache, a
slight tremor, anything out of the ordinary would send him into a detailed self-assessment,
convinced it was a precursor to some impending threat.

as a physical therapist, where close contact and unexpected patient movements were routine. He found
himself flinching when a patient reached out to steady themselves or when a colleague lightly

confidence, not just personally, but professionally, making him second-guess his treatment plans and
interactions.

out. He came to therapy, hoping, despite his certainty that he would never feel normal emotions
again, to find some way to reclaim himself, to quiet the constant alarm bells ringing in his mind,
and perhaps, to trust himself once more.
