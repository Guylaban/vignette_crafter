injured, became an indelible mark on his mind. He knew, with a certainty that chilled him to the
bone, that he was responsible.

by a pervasive sense of dread and self-reproach. The specific song that had been playing that night,
if he heard even a snippet of it from a passing car or a shop, would seize him with an almost
physical jolt, plunging him back into the chaos of the accident. He would see flash-like images of
"cars hit by accident," the crumpled hood and shattered glass replaying in his mind's eye with vivid
and unwelcome clarity. These intrusive moments were relentless, often appearing unbidden when his
guard was down.

to follow choreography, his mind frequently drifting, making him miss cues or misstep. He often
caught himself simply staring into space, having "difficulty concentrating" on the intricate
movements that had once been second nature.

Arthur had tried desperately to regain some semblance of his former self, but the effort was
exhausting. He found himself constantly "dwelling on how I used to be before the event," a carefree
artist, unburdened by guilt. He made conscious efforts to "suppress thoughts" of the trauma, pushing
them away whenever they threatened to surface, but they always returned, often with greater
intensity. He meticulously planned his routes to "avoid going to or near the place where the event
happened," even if it meant significant detours, the very thought of that intersection filling him
with panic.

regret and blame.

encouragement, that finally brought Arthur to seek help. He arrived feeling trapped in a cycle of
relived trauma and self-punishment, desperately hoping to find a way back to a life not solely
defined by that single, catastrophic moment.
