powerless, feeling the sting of blows and the terror of being abandoned in the dark. In those
moments, a deep conviction began to form within her: the world was inherently a dangerous place, and
those who should protect you could easily become your tormentors. The people who should have
safeguarded her, her family, had let her down profoundly, a belief that had solidified into a core
appraisal of institutional failure.

they would surge forward not as clear pictures, but as a suffocating wave of pure terror or despair,

times, when she tried to consciously recall what had happened, she could recount the facts with a
disturbing coolness, as if narrating a story that happened to a stranger, feeling completely numb to

the frightening event flashed vivid visual images, stark pictures of the dark room or her aunt’s
raised hand, appearing unbidden in her mind.

To cope, Layla had developed a formidable ability to try hard not to think about the trauma. She

to relax, even when she knew she was in a safe place. She would often dwell on how the event could
have been prevented, replaying childhood scenarios in her mind, searching for a different outcome, a
moment where she could have escaped or someone could have intervened. She also frequently dwelled on
how she used to be before the event, longing for the carefree, trusting child she felt she had lost
forever. This constant rumination and suppression of thoughts created an exhausting internal
landscape.

the occasional clangs and distant voices, would sometimes trigger her internal alarm, making her
scan her surroundings with an intensity that went beyond her duties, searching for a threat that
wasn't there. The isolation of being at sea for extended periods also amplified her profound sense

compromise her ability to command her ship or truly connect with the man she loved.
