stone. Her life had flowed with a predictable rhythm until a sudden, brutal moment shattered its

terrifying speed and unpredictability, snatching a bag from a vendor and shoving her violently to
the ground. Lila remembered the sharp, hurried *thud-thud-thud* of his footsteps as he approached
from behind the stall, the vendor’s surprised cry, and then his hasty retreat, leaving a lingering
trail of that sickeningly sweet cologne. The entire event was a blur of rapid motion, a flash of
violence that ended as quickly as it began.

In the weeks and months that followed, Lila found herself struggling to piece together what she had
witnessed. When her husband, Raj, gently encouraged her to talk about it, she would start, but the
words would tangle, and she'd lose the thread, unable to finish her sentences. It wasn’t just that
she couldn’t explain it to him; she realized she couldn’t tell a clear story of what happened even
to herself. Fragments would intrude into her mind, vivid and unsettling, feeling more real and
immediate than memories of ordinary things, but they refused to cohere into a narrative. This
internal disarray led her to a quiet, gnawing conclusion: there was something wrong with her as a
person, a fundamental defect that prevented her from processing such an important event.

come to believe, was a dangerous place, a belief reinforced by the memory of the market's sudden
shift from peaceful commerce to violent chaos. This conviction made her deeply wary; people, she now
suspected, couldn't be trusted. Her creative process, which often involved exploring new
environments, became fraught with anxiety. The unpredictable nature of finding inspiration or even
interacting with clients at her studio now felt like a constant threat.

became a potent trigger. Once, a new client wearing a strong, sweet fragrance entered her studio,
and Lila felt a wave of nausea and panic wash over her, her breath catching in her throat. She had
to politely excuse herself, retreating to another room until the scent dissipated.

Over time, Lila began to avoid situations where these triggers might arise. She stopped visiting

stimulating environments, which was less personal and less effective for showcasing her work. This

began to take a toll on her career. New commissions dwindled, and she found herself less engaged

relentless fear and the feeling of her life shrinking, finally agreed, hoping to find a way to
navigate a world that now felt so profoundly unsafe, and perhaps, to understand why the event had
left her feeling so broken.
