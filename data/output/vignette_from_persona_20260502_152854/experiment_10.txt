Leilani, a 30-year-old plumber of Oceanian descent, carried a history that often felt more like a
collection of disjointed sensations than a clear narrative. Her childhood was marked by a
caregiver’s unpredictable and often violent outbursts. She recalled flashes: the terrifying sound of
shouting echoing through the small house, the chilling sensation of being watched intently before a
punishment, and the subtle, tightening grip of fear when she sensed another person’s anger, even if
it wasn't directed at her. These memories often felt alien, as if they belonged to someone else, and
she knew she was only remembering parts of what happened, the rest a blank, incomplete picture.

As Leilani grew, these fragmented memories continued to cast a long shadow. Now, as a married woman
navigating the demanding world of plumbing, her daily life was punctuated by moments of intense
distress. A sudden, sharp shout from a construction foreman would immediately send her body into a
state of intense readiness, her muscles clenching, and her heart racing as if danger were imminent.
This hyper-alertness meant that even an unexpected movement nearby, like a coworker abruptly turning
a corner in a cramped pipe crawl space, would cause her to startle violently, reacting as if she
were in grave danger. These jolts of fear were so disruptive that she often found herself struggling
to concentrate on intricate pipework, her focus fragmented. The pervasive sense that her body was
constantly tense and ready to react to danger further compounded her difficulties.

To cope with these overwhelming feelings, Leilani had developed a series of strategies. When a
trigger, like the foreman’s raised voice or the feeling of being observed by a client, hit, she
would immediately try hard not to think about the trauma, pushing the intrusive memories and
sensations away. This suppression of thoughts, while offering momentary relief, meant that the
memory of the abuse remained fragmented and unsettling, continuing to feel like a hazy, incomplete
picture that didn’t quite fit into her life story. She also found herself keeping her mind occupied
all the time, constantly planning her next plumbing job or mentally reviewing her tool list, a
relentless mental distraction that left little room for processing. This constant mental effort,
however, often maintained her body’s heightened state of alert, making her perpetually tense and
ready to react to perceived threats. In moments of particular stress, she would restrict her food
intake, finding a perverse sense of control in undereating as a way of coping.

These maladaptive strategies, rather than alleviating her distress, created a self-reinforcing
cycle. Her efforts to suppress thoughts about the trauma and keep her mind constantly distracted
actually prevented her from integrating the fragmented memories, leaving them feeling more alien and
incomplete. The constant mental distraction also left her physically and emotionally depleted,
making her far more susceptible to triggers. Because she was so often in a state of heightened
threat, her system became exquisitely sensitive; even a calm expression of anger from her husband,
which she knew intellectually wasn't a threat, would immediately register as danger, making her more
vulnerable to the very triggers she sought to avoid. Similarly, her undereating left her drained,
reducing her resilience and making her more reactive to everyday stressors that, in turn,
intensified her threat response. This constant state of vigilance, fueled by her unresolved memories
and coping mechanisms, made her concentration at work increasingly difficult, leading to mistakes
and frustrated clients.

The fragmented nature of her memories, the way they felt like an incomplete picture, also
contributed significantly to her current state. Because the abuse remained so disjointed and alien,
Leilani was left with a persistent sense of unease, making her body feel constantly on edge, ready
for danger, as if something unresolved was always lurking just beneath the surface. This meant that
any attempt to recall the past, particularly when she was already feeling threatened, would only
yield a jumble of disconnected images, reinforcing the sense of an incomplete picture rather than
offering clarity. The toll on her work and her marriage had become undeniable. Her husband had
noticed her withdrawal, her perpetual tension, and her struggles to focus. It was the realization
that her life felt increasingly defined by these unseen battles, by the constant need to escape her
own mind, that finally brought Leilani to seek help, hoping to piece together the fragments and find
a way out of the tightening loop of fear and avoidance.
