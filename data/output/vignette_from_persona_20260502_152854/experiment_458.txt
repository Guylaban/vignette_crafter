irrevocably shifted. Driving home late from a school fundraiser, utterly exhausted after a long
week, she was pulled over. What began as a routine stop quickly escalated into a terrifying ordeal
of aggressive shouting, blinding lights, and the distinct, unsettling smell of alcohol emanating
from one of the officers. She remembered the feeling of utter powerlessness, the humiliation of
being treated as a suspect when she had done nothing wrong. In the aftermath, Imani became convinced
that the incident happened because of the way she acted – perhaps she shouldn't have been out so
late, or perhaps her initial confusion was interpreted as defiance. She started to believe there was
something fundamentally wrong with her as a person, a flaw that made her a target.

This deeply ingrained belief that she was defective, coupled with her self-blame, led her to
instinctively avoid situations that might highlight her perceived flaws or bring about similar
distress. She began to proactively avoid emotional stress, finding the vulnerability too much to
bear, and slowly started to avoid people, fearing judgment or that her "defect" would be exposed.
Her conviction that she was inherently flawed also fueled a pervasive sense of unease; if something
was wrong with *her*, then she was perpetually unsafe. This translated into a constant state of
hypervigilance, where she was super alert and watchful, unable to truly relax even in the supposed
safety of her own home. The relentless state of being on guard, where sudden movements by others
would immediately put her on edge, only served to reinforce her belief that she was indeed isolated
and that the world was a dangerous place where she could never truly let her guard down. The
constant internal alarm became proof that her deepest fears about herself were true.

Imani’s attempts to manage her distress through avoidance had unintended consequences. By
increasingly avoiding other people, she found herself feeling profoundly isolated and set apart from
others, which, in turn, strengthened her belief that there was something wrong with her. Her
withdrawal solidified her conviction that she was unworthy of connection. These maladaptive
strategies also inadvertently heightened her sensitivity to triggers. For instance, her generalized
avoidance of emotional stress meant she rarely encountered situations where she could safely process
intense feelings, making her already profound sense of feeling very tired or exhausted a much more
potent trigger for distress, rather than just a physical state. Similarly, by avoiding social
situations, she deprived herself of opportunities to gradually habituate to loud noises in a safe

jarring and sending her into a spiral of anxiety. Furthermore, her avoidance behaviours, such as
actively avoiding positive events or social gatherings, meant she was constantly operating within a
reduced sphere of perceived safety. She never truly tested the waters of the world, which only
reinforced her hypervigilance and inability to relax, as her threat monitoring system remained
perpetually active, lacking new, disconfirming information.

At work, her role as a school counselor, which once brought her immense joy, became a daily
battleground. The constant stream of children, the unexpected bursts of laughter or shouts from the
playground, and the general hum of a busy school environment kept her perpetually on edge. The smell
of alcohol on a parent or even a hand sanitizer could instantly transport her back to that
terrifying night. She found herself cancelling group sessions, making excuses to avoid parent
meetings, and retreating to her office during lunch breaks, all to avoid people and the emotional
demands of her job. Her marriage, too, felt the strain; she struggled to connect, feeling isolated
even from her husband, and often found herself too drained to engage in activities they once
enjoyed. The joy of life seemed to be slowly siphoned away, leaving her perpetually exhausted and on
guard.

Imani finally sought help when the cumulative toll became unbearable. The cycle of hypervigilance,
avoidance, and self-blame had tightened its grip, leaving her feeling trapped and utterly alone. She

parts of herself that felt lost, but also deeply afraid that her perceived defectiveness was an
insurmountable barrier to healing.
