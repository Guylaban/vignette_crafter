when a sudden, violent robbery escalated into a hostage situation, leaving him feeling utterly
helpless as he watched events unfold around him. Though physically unharmed, the memory of the

perception of safety and himself.

In the aftermath, Kian found himself perpetually on edge, his internal alarm system stuck in the
'on' position. He had come to believe, with a chilling certainty, that he could no longer rely on
himself to anticipate danger or protect those around him, a profound shift from his previously self-
assured nature. This conviction that he was no longer capable of handling life’s demands made him
feel perpetually exposed, leading to a constant, gnawing sense of threat. He started waking during
the night, sometimes several times, compelled to check whether his apartment was secure, peering
through blinds and listening intently to the quiet hum of the building. During his daily commute and
even within the seemingly safe confines of the bank, he frequently looked behind him, a quick,
almost involuntary flick of his head, to check if someone might be following him or approaching him
unexpectedly. This relentless hypervigilance, born from his deep-seated self-doubt, left him
perpetually exhausted and irritable, leading to angry outbursts at minor inconveniences, a behaviour
that deeply distressed him.

The constant state of threat, with its accompanying irritability and the relentless need to check
his surroundings, began to chip away at Kian’s self-worth. It confirmed his fear that he was no
longer capable of the calm, composed demeanor he once prided himself on, or of navigating the world
without feeling utterly overwhelmed. To cope with the intrusive images and the gnawing anxiety, Kian
developed a series of strategies, most notably suppressing thoughts of the trauma. He would push the
memories down, trying hard not to think about the attack or its immediate aftermath. This effort
often manifested as overworking; he’d volunteer for extra shifts at the bank, meticulously re-
checking his cash drawer and reports long after his colleagues had left, desperate to fill every
quiet moment with activity so he wouldn’t be alone with his thoughts.

However, these maladaptive strategies, rather than alleviating his distress, often compounded it.

small errors, which only reinforced his belief that he was no longer capable of performing his job
competently. This, in turn, deepened his appraisal that he was no longer the reliable, focused
individual he once was. His increasing irritability, a direct consequence of his constant state of
threat, also contributed to a growing distance in his most important relationship. He began to avoid
intimacy, pulling away emotionally from his then-wife, unable to share the burden of his fear or
allow himself to be truly vulnerable. The emotional gulf widened until, eventually, his marriage
ended in divorce, a devastating outcome that solidified his painful appraisal that he was no longer
capable of maintaining the close, trusting connections he once valued, and that he had acted in ways
that went against his core values as a partner.

Now, Kian finds himself caught in a tightening loop. The more he feels incapable and violated in his

exhausted by the constant vigilance and the weight of his own self-condemnation, hoping to find a
way to reclaim the sense of safety and capability that felt so irrevocably lost.
