unsettling unease that had only intensified since her divorce. The turning point in her life, the
event that cast a long shadow, was the sudden death of her younger sister, Maya. Maya, only 23, had
been killed instantly in a hit-and-run accident on her way home from work. Lina, reporting on a
local political rally that evening, had received the call from a frantic police officer. The details
she later pieced together from police reports and news coverage — the mangled car, the anonymous
driver who fled the scene — solidified a sense of brutal randomness and profound injustice.

In the months that followed, Lina found herself consumed by an overwhelming sense of guilt. She
would replay moments from their last conversation, searching for anything she could have done
differently, any warning she might have missed. A profound fear often washed over her, a chilling
reminder of how quickly life could be snatched away. These strong negative feelings, a mixture of
horror, anger, and deep shame for not having protected Maya, seemed to well up inside her without
warning.

As time progressed, Lina noticed a constant state of hypervigilance had become her default. As a
journalist, she was often out in the field, navigating busy city streets or interviewing sources in
unfamiliar locations. She found herself constantly scanning her surroundings, her eyes darting to
every car, every pedestrian, every shadow, as if expecting danger to materialize at any moment. The
slightest unexpected noise – a car backfiring, a sudden shout from across the street – would cause
her to jump, her heart pounding in her chest. She was perpetually on guard, her senses heightened to
an exhausting degree.

Even in the perceived safety of her own apartment, a quiet refuge in the bustling heart of the city,
Lina found herself unable to relax. She would try to unwind after a long day of chasing stories,
perhaps by reading a book or watching a film, but her body remained tense, her mind racing. The
feeling was a constant hum beneath the surface, preventing any true sense of calm or peace. She
knew, intellectually, that her apartment was safe, yet the profound inability to let down her guard
persisted, making sleep elusive and rest an alien concept. This ceaseless internal tension permeated
every aspect of her life.

arena where her heightened state of alert was both a burden and a necessary evil. She was always

she could turn off. Her inability to relax meant that genuine connection with others felt
increasingly difficult. She had recently started to consider whether this pervasive internal tension
was why her marriage had ultimately fractured, leaving her feeling even more isolated. She found her
constant state of watchfulness made her feel apart from others, always on guard even when she longed

encountered stories of other unexpected tragedies, bringing fresh waves of fear and anger about
Maya’s fate. Lina presented for therapy, exhausted and desperate for an understanding of why she
couldn’t simply "get over" what had happened, and why the world still felt so perpetually dangerous.
