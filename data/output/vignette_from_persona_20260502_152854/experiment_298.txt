over her. It was a shame so potent that any flicker of recall brought with it a burning humiliation.

This struggle to articulate her experience, coupled with the profound shame on recall, quickly began

something wrong with me as a person." The event, she reasoned, must have happened "because of the
sort of person I am," a devastating piece of character blame that settled deep within her. Her body,
too, became an adversary; it would tense, jolt, and flood with adrenaline at unexpected moments,
making her feel as though "my body keeps reacting as if I am still in danger even when I am safe."

flawed.

Driven by these painful negative appraisals, Sofia instinctively adopted a series of maladaptive

The sight of a male guest with a certain gait or a colleague whose voice had a particular tone would
send a jolt through her, prompting her to quickly avert her gaze or find an excuse to be elsewhere.
This constant vigilance and avoidance, while exhausting, offered a fleeting sense of safety. To
escape the relentless churn of these self-blaming thoughts, Sofia also started to "work excessively
to avoid being alone with thoughts." She’d stay late, volunteer for extra shifts, filling every
waking moment with tasks, terrified of the quiet introspection that might allow the shame and self-
blame to surface.

The fragmented nature of her memory, the inability to form a coherent story, and the deep shame on
recall, actively fueled her maladaptive strategies. She found herself overworking even more

that might force her to confront or process the event, thus leaving her memory unresolved and

face. The very act of avoiding made it harder to challenge her internal narrative of self-blame.

As weeks turned into months, the cycle tightened. The more Sofia struggled to piece together the
event, the more the shame intensified, reinforcing her belief that "there is something wrong with

made it even harder to articulate the trauma, keeping her trapped in a state where she "cannot find
words to describe it." In quieter moments, when the overwork failed to fully distract her, Sofia
would find herself consumed by "rumination about how to get even with the assailant," a dark current
of revenge fantasies that provided a fleeting sense of control but ultimately left her feeling
agitated and unresolved.

The relentless overwork began to strain her relationship with her partner, who worried about her
constant exhaustion and emotional distance. Her increasing social withdrawal at work, driven by the
need to avoid potential triggers, also started to affect her team dynamics, though she managed to
maintain her professional façade. Sofia finally sought help, her motivation spurred by a terrifying
realization: the harder she tried to escape, the more tightly the trauma seemed to bind her, leaving
her exhausted, isolated, and increasingly convinced that she was irrevocably broken. She desperately
hoped to find a way to silence the internal critic and reclaim a sense of wholeness she feared was
lost forever.
