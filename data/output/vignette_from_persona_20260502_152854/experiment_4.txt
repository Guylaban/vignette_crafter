dread. The sound of someone crying, whether himself or another, was not a call for empathy but a
harbinger of further punishment, a signal that his world was about to be upended by a fresh wave of
physical abuse. These early experiences had etched deep grooves into his psyche, shaping his
perception of safety and threat.

Now, as an adult, these childhood echoes reverberated loudly. Arguments with his wife, even minor
disagreements about household chores, would immediately plunge Karim into a state of intense threat
perception. His heart would pound, his breath would quicken, and he’d find himself scanning the
room, needing to know where every exit was, and instinctively seeking a chair with his back to a
wall, a seemingly innocuous habit he’d developed over years. This pervasive sense of threat then
drove his attempts to control his environment, manifesting as various maladaptive strategies. He

conversations that hinted at confrontation, believing that any engagement would inevitably lead to
harm.

ever-present. He would meticulously study people's faces and body language, searching for the
slightest hint of aggression or disapproval, convinced that any shift in expression signaled
