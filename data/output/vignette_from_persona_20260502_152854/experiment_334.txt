In the months that followed, Zara found her understanding of the world fundamentally altered. She
had come to believe, with an unshakeable certainty, that the world was a dangerous place. This deep-
seated conviction, coupled with her new belief that she had to be on guard all the time and be
especially careful because you never know what can happen next, began to dictate her every move. Her
conviction that constant vigilance was her only shield directly shaped her daily actions. She found
herself actively avoiding situations that put her body under stress, like rushing up the stairs or
navigating crowded sidewalks, fearing any sudden physical demand might leave her vulnerable. She
became hypervigilant towards people, scanning every new face for signs of malice or intent, always

constantly scan her environment for any signs of threat, her eyes darting from exits to shadowy
corners.

This pervasive sense of threat, particularly the heightened vulnerability she felt after dark,
making her find it harder to feel safe at night than during the day, solidified Zara’s conviction
that she lived in a perpetually dangerous world. The constant, gnawing need to always identify
escape routes before she could feel comfortable anywhere, whether in a restaurant or a colleague's

unfamiliar places altogether, convinced she could not accurately assess the danger they might hold,
which in turn strengthened her appraisal that the world was unpredictable and unsafe.

she was constantly finding minor cues – a sudden movement, a raised voice, an unfamiliar vehicle –

protect her, inadvertently kept her in a state of high alert, making her feel even more threatened

unfamiliar places, while offering a temporary sense of security, meant she never had the opportunity
