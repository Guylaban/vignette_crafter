left an indelible mark.

pervasive sense of threat manifested acutely in his daily life; he found he simply could not relax,
even when in the familiar comfort of his own home with his wife, Maya. The constant internal alarm
meant he felt profoundly unsafe unless his back was against a wall, allowing him to survey the
entire room, a habit that made social gatherings and even family dinners deeply uncomfortable. This
heightened vigilance also meant that when something took longer than expected—a delivery at work,
his wife running late from an errand—he immediately assumed something bad was happening, his mind
racing to worst-case scenarios.

The relentless pressure of this internal threat, the feeling of being constantly exposed and on
edge, profoundly influenced his coping. Desperate for relief from the unyielding anxiety, Ravi found

incessant self-blame was exhausting, and to numb the constant churn of his thoughts, he started

acknowledge it, it couldn't hurt him.

constant self-blame kept him trapped in a loop of regret and perceived vulnerability, which in turn

while offering fleeting calm, left him foggy and less capable of managing his emotions,

belief that he could not relax even in safe places. His deliberate avoidance of thinking about the

impending doom over his every moment, reinforcing his belief that danger lurked in every delay and
every open space.

inconvenience, now sent him into a spiral of anxious predictions, affecting his productivity and
