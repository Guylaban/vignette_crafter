and the distant, then rapidly approaching, whine of enemy motorcycles – a sound that had meant
certain death.

his home could instantly plunge Kian back into that moment. These triggers, once subtle, had become
potent conduits to the past. He would find himself suddenly hearing the frantic shouts and the
distinct crunch of boots on dry earth in his head, an auditory recall that made the memory feel
terrifyingly real. As a result, his memories of the frightening event were no longer just
recollections; they were like a film that played over and over in his mind, vivid and relentless.

memories, coupled with the suddenness of the triggers, had instilled in Kian an almost constant
state of hypervigilance. He felt on guard even inside his own home, scrutinising every shadow, every
unfamiliar sound. This heightened sense of threat meant that whenever the memories surged, Kian was
convinced danger was imminent, driving him to seek safety. Consequently, he had developed a range of
maladaptive strategies to cope. He stopped watching TV programmes or films that depicted any kind of
combat or violence, fearing they might evoke more memories.

him, but what frightened him most was the possibility that he might never truly feel safe again.
