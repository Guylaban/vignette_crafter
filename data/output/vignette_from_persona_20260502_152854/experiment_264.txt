piercing screams, and the acrid smell of burning insulation and flesh remained seared into his mind.
He spent days operating under makeshift conditions, surrounded by the overwhelming smells of smoke
and burnt debris, witnessing unimaginable suffering.

Now, ordinary life had become a minefield. Javier found that even the most innocuous sensory cues
could instantly plunge him back into the horror. A sudden, loud noise, like a car backfiring on the
street outside his suburban home, or the sharp clang of surgical instruments dropping in the
operating room, could without warning bring back the full, terrifying memory of the earthquake.

cauterized tissue during surgery, would instantly transport him, his heart pounding, to the chaos of
those days. These potent triggers were not just fleeting reminders; they were gateways to vivid,
visceral flashbacks that felt terrifyingly real.

What complicated Javier’s internal world was the peculiar nature of these recollections. When a

remembering parts of what happened, with the rest frustratingly missing or blurred. This incomplete

the trauma, left him in a constant state of internal agitation. This persistent internal turmoil,

as danger cues, effectively generating new triggers and reinforcing the cycle of distress.
