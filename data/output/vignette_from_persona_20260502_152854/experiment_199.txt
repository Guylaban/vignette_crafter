gathering she had helped organize, when a trusted acquaintance, someone she considered a friend and
a "known person" within her ministry circle, had cornered her in a quiet back room. The assault had
been swift and disorienting, leaving Leilani with a profound sense of violation, not just of her
body, but of the very fabric of her trust and security. The experience had been particularly
insidious because it occurred in a moment when she had allowed herself to feel happy and relaxed,
surrounded by people she thought cared for her.

In the years that followed, Leilani found herself increasingly haunted. Her memories of the
frightening event were not fading; instead, they were like a film that played over and over, vivid
and detailed, forcing their way into her mind when she did not want it to. This uncontrolled access
to her memories kept her body in a perpetual state of readiness for danger, a constant internal
thrum of alertness. What truly weighed on Leilani was a deep-seated conviction that there was
something fundamentally wrong with her as a person, a "defective self" that had somehow allowed the
assault to happen. This belief, alongside her persistent thought that somebody else would have
stopped the event from happening, fueled a constant sense of impending danger, keeping her body on
high alert, always tense and ready to react. Her conviction that she was no longer capable of doing
the things that defined her, particularly her ministry work, also fueled this constant sense of
threat.

Because Leilani believed there was something fundamentally wrong with her, she found herself
increasingly turning to maladaptive strategies. The relentless feeling of her body on alert, always
ready to react, drove Leilani to adopt new coping mechanisms. When the vivid memories forced their

sight of familiar faces, especially those reminiscent of her past interactions within the ministry,
would often prompt Leilani to subtly move closer to an exit, ensuring she could quickly escape if
discomfort arose. Even a moment of feeling happy or relaxed could instantly snap her into a state of
hypervigilance, her body tensing and her gaze darting around the room, scanning for threats. The
mere discomfort of a crowded room, a common occurrence in her social ministry work, could
unexpectedly bring forth a flash of the event, replaying vividly in her mind.

strategies, like always scanning for exits during community meetings or overeating in her office,
seemed to reinforce her internal sense of danger and hypervigilance, keeping her body on a hair-
trigger. Her habit of overeating to numb distress often left her feeling sluggish and self-
conscious, which, in turn, made her more sensitive to situations where she felt uncomfortable or
around known people, tightening the cycle of avoidance and distress. Sometimes, her attempts to
manage distress by overeating would leave her feeling vulnerable, paradoxically making the unwanted
memories more likely to intrude. Leilani also found herself applying heavy make-up before going out,
a way to create a shield, to hide what she perceived as her brokenness from the world. Her constant
vigilance, though subtle, also made her more attuned to any discomfort in her surroundings, subtly
sharpening what she perceived as a trigger. At home, she began to repeatedly check that doors and
windows were locked, sometimes several times an hour, convinced that an unlocked door meant imminent
danger.

The cumulative effect of these changes began to take a toll on her once vibrant life. Her husband
noticed her increasing withdrawal, her reluctance to engage in social activities, and the way her
eyes constantly scanned a room. Her ministry work, once a source of immense joy, had become a
minefield of potential triggers, making her feel increasingly "no longer capable" of serving her
community effectively. The constant tension and the intrusive memories were exhausting, eroding her
ability to connect authentically with the people she was meant to help. It was the growing distance
between herself and her husband, and the painful realization that her cherished work was suffering,
that finally brought Leilani to seek help, desperate to reclaim the woman she once was, before the
world became a place of constant threat and her own self a source of shame.
