now, weary of the invisible war he continued to fight long after returning home.

smell of damp earth and something metallic. An unexpected ambush had turned the world into a
cacophony of gunfire and shouts. Ricardo remembered a sudden, blinding flash, the earth heaving
beneath him, and the metallic taste of blood filling his mouth as he hit the ground, disoriented and
lightheaded. He fought through a wave of overwhelming dizziness, the sensation of his head spinning
a visceral reminder of that moment of utter helplessness.

In the years that followed, this internal landscape began to shape his external world. Whenever he
felt that familiar internal sensation of lightheadedness or caught a faint metallic taste, it would
immediately trigger a profound sense of impending doom. These triggers, whether a somatic echo or a
phantom taste, would instantly plunge him into a state of intense threat. He would find himself on
high alert when he heard unexpected or loud sounds in his daily life, interpreting them as a
potential danger, a direct echo of the ambush. This constant vigilance meant that any sudden noise,
like a dropped book in his classroom or a car backfiring outside, would make his heart race and his
muscles tense.

To manage this pervasive sense of threat, Ricardo developed a repertoire of maladaptive strategies.

felt necessary for survival. In his classroom, he always positioned his desk to ensure he was near
the door, making sure to stay close to the exit at all times. He would meticulously identify escape
routes before he felt comfortable anywhere, whether in a restaurant, a school assembly, or even a
friend’s living room. This planning, while offering a fleeting sense of control, only reinforced his
underlying belief that danger was always lurking. What frightened him most was the thought of being
caught unprepared again, as he had been in the jungle.

The more he engaged in these maladaptive strategies, the more his sense of threat deepened.

inherently dangerous, reinforcing his belief that he needed to be perpetually on guard. This

dizziness, instantly activating his defensive behaviours. He also found himself avoiding situations

stress led to a growing distance in his relationships, culminating in his divorce several years ago,
as his ex-wife found him increasingly detached and unreachable.

shouts of a student or the sudden slam of a door could shatter his fragile peace. He sought help
