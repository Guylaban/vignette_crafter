altered her perception of safety and connection.

deep within her. This conviction quickly translated into a need for "constant vigilance"; she felt
she had to be especially careful because "you never know what can happen next." Her ability to trust
in the inherent safety of public spaces eroded, leaving her feeling profoundly disconnected and
isolated, as if she were permanently an "outsider" looking in on a world that no longer made sense.

conversation topics" among her younger colleagues—discussions about urban crime or local news
incidents—would instantly activate her deep-seated belief that "the world is a dangerous place,"
making her heart race and her hands tremble as she struggled to maintain composure.

began to actively employ various maladaptive strategies to manage her overwhelming anxiety. She

While initially providing some relief from immediate distress, Patricia's determined efforts to

constantly monitor her own thoughts to prevent the "bad" ones from surfacing. Her consistent

instantly trigger a wave of panic, a strong reminder of her trauma and her need to remain vigilant.

more overwhelming and triggering than it might have been otherwise, further cementing her conviction
that "the world is a dangerous place."

triggers, as her mind would quickly connect them to worst-case scenarios. This constant internal

finally sought help, exhausted by the relentless effort required to simply exist, hoping to find a
way to reclaim the sense of safety and connection that had been stolen from her.
