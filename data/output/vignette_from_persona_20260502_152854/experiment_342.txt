was unable to find words to describe it, leaving a silent, internal wound. This early experience
cemented a deep-seated mistrust that people couldn't be trusted, along with a gnawing self-doubt
that she couldn't rely on herself in moments of crisis.

Layla’s adult life. Her deeply ingrained conviction that people couldn't be trusted meant she was
perpetually on high alert; any sudden movement by a patron at the bustling bar where she worked
immediately put her on guard, her eyes darting to assess the perceived danger. This constant
vigilance, the feeling that she was only safe in a very limited number of places, meant her mind was
perpetually primed for danger, often making her susceptible to intrusive memories.

of threat, feeling as if the danger was unfolding in the present moment, overwhelming her with
emotion to the point where she had to stop whatever she was doing. The overwhelming, nonverbal
nature of these recollections deeply reinforced Layla’s self-doubt, making her feel she couldn’t
rely on herself to process or articulate her own experiences. This made her dread any conscious
engagement with her past; the thought of revisiting those memories felt like confronting her own
inadequacy.

This pervasive sense of threat drove Layla to develop elaborate maladaptive strategies to cope.

conflicts or aggressive customers, hoping to feel prepared for the inevitable. She also actively

intensified her sense of threat. Her incessant door and lock checking each night, a ritual that
could consume an hour, rather than reassuring her, only reinforced the pervasive belief that danger

as she tried to suppress them, her mental rehearsal of worst-case scenarios often inadvertently
triggered blurry, nonverbal fragments of the original trauma, making the memories feel even more
overwhelming and unmanageable, as if she was constantly being reminded of her inability to cope.

Layla’s marriage crumbled under the weight of her constant vigilance and withdrawal, a direct
consequence of her escalating symptoms. She rarely socialized outside of work, and when she did, she

remained her deepest fear.
