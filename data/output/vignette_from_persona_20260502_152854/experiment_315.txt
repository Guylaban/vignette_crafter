violation** of his sense of justice, delivered by the very **authority presence** he had always
worked alongside.

feel subtly menacing. He found himself in a state of **hypervigilance**, constantly on edge, his
senses acutely tuned to his surroundings. This pervasive sense of **threat** manifested particularly
as **watching others**; in court, in grocery stores, even at family gatherings, he would
meticulously observe people’s movements, expressions, and intentions, searching for any sign that
they might harm him or those around him. The constant internal scanning made it almost impossible
for him to truly relax, and he began experiencing significant **sleep problems**, struggling to fall
asleep and waking frequently throughout the night, his mind racing.

These unsettling internal states, the pervasive **threat** and the constant **hypervigilance**,
inexorably led Robert to adopt a series of **maladaptive strategies** in an attempt to regain some
control. When the anxiety became overwhelming, he would retreat to his study, putting on **loud
music or TV** to create a wall of sound, hoping to drown out the intrusive thoughts and the
perceived dangers lurking just beyond the noise. He also began **checking his surroundings** with
increasing frequency, a habit that became particularly noticeable in his professional life. In the

were safe, that the world was not about to erupt into chaos.

vigilant. Similarly, the sight of a patrol car, an **authority presence** he once respected, now

making his **sleep problems** even worse and further entrenching his belief that he lived in a
perilous world.

trapped in a relentless cycle of fear, desperately hoping to find a way back to the man he once
was—a man who felt safe in his own skin, and in the world he had dedicated his life to protecting.
