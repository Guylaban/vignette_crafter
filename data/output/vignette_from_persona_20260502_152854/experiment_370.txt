threatening illness landed him in the intensive care unit. For weeks, his world was reduced to the

antiseptic in the air and the sight of medical equipment became synonymous with fear and
vulnerability, deeply embedding themselves in his memory as potent reminders of his brush with
death.

Even after his physical recovery, Liam found himself trapped in a different kind of illness. The
distinct olfactory trigger of antiseptic or the visual trigger of medical equipment, even on
television, would send a jolt through him, instantly triggering a cascade of threat responses. His
heart would pound, his palms would sweat, and he’d experience strong physical reactions as if he
were still fighting for his life. A sudden, unexpected movement nearby, like his girlfriend turning
quickly in the kitchen, would cause him to startle violently, reacting as if he were in immediate
danger, his body tensing for a threat that wasn't there.

These intense physical reactions, whenever he encountered such triggers, significantly reinforced
Liam’s growing negative appraisals about himself and his body. He’d come to believe, "I can no
longer trust my own body," feeling betrayed by its sudden collapse and its continued overreactions.

danger even when I am safe," deepening his conviction that his body was now an enemy. This profound
sense of betrayal and the constant feeling of internal threat made him increasingly wary of anything
that reminded him of his illness. The sight of medical equipment or the smell of a hospital, which
now instantly affirmed his body’s untrustworthiness, prompted him to adopt a range of maladaptive
strategies, such as actively avoiding any medical cues—hospitals, ambulances, or even physical
examinations—to try and prevent these overwhelming reactions.

an unreliable enemy, Liam started to delay sleep, staying up until very late scrolling through his
phone, as if he could outrun the night terrors or the vulnerable feeling of lying still in the dark.

belief that he could no longer trust his own body, viewing it as an enemy, made him exquisitely

Living in a constant state of heightened threat, where unexpected movement nearby caused him to
react as if in danger, made Liam’s environment feel perpetually hostile. This hyper-vigilance meant

a cleaning cart—were instantly perceived as potent triggers, confirming his internal state of
danger. The persistent trouble falling or staying asleep, coupled with the strong physical reactions

of danger.

to articulate the depth of his fear. He finally sought help, exhausted and isolated, hoping to
escape the tightening grip of his own body and mind, desperate to find a way back to the person he
