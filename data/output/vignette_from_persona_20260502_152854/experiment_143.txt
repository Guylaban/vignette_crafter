tightened her chest. Then she heard it: heavy, ragged breathing directly behind her. The sound,
amplified by the stillness, instantly triggered a surge of intense fear and horror. She started to
walk faster, every instinct screaming that she was being followed or tracked. Though she never
clearly saw her stalker that night, the potent combination of the heavy breathing and the pervasive
feeling of being followed became deeply ingrained triggers.

In the aftermath, Clara found her memory of the event to be a fractured landscape. Some moments,
like the flash of police lights arriving later, were extremely clear, yet other crucial parts
remained completely blank. These very triggers would often bring back sudden pieces of the memory,
brief flashes of terror that appeared without warning and then disappeared, leaving her disoriented.
Each time these fragments resurfaced, they immediately reignited the intense negative feelings of
fear and horror, making her feel profoundly threatened. This constant re-experiencing, coupled with
her struggle to find words to describe what had truly happened, led her to a devastating conclusion:
her life had been destroyed by the trauma.

This belief, that her life was irreparably damaged, became a core negative appraisal that profoundly
shaped her world. It fueled a relentless sense of hypervigilance; she felt she had to be on guard
all the time, constantly scanning her environment. This hypervigilance, in turn, made her acutely
sensitive to potential triggers. A colleague clearing his throat loudly in the staff room could
sound like heavy breathing, sending a jolt of anxiety through her. The sight of a police car, one of
those moments that were extremely clear in her fractured memory, now brought a rush of anxiety,
immediately reinforcing her belief that people couldn't be trusted and that she had to be on guard
all the time. Moreover, this pervasive sense of threat only served to make her more susceptible to
triggers. The sound of a car idling outside her apartment, or an unexpected knock, would immediately
evoke the feeling of being followed, plunging her back into a state of panic.

Clara also found that her constant state of threat made it harder to gain a coherent understanding
of the past; the more threatened she felt, the more fragmented and overwhelming her memories became.
Specific, partially clear moments of the event, like the sight of the police responding, became
triggers themselves, further intensifying her feelings of mistrust and hypervigilance. She found
herself increasingly convinced that people couldn't be trusted, a mistrust that deepened her
isolation and made her interpret innocent interactions as potentially menacing; a stranger
approaching her on the street, even to ask for directions, would make her feel threatened. It was
this tightening cycle, where her constant state of threat made her more sensitive to triggers, which
then brought back fragmented memories and reinforced her conviction that her life was destroyed and
she couldn't trust anyone, that ultimately led Clara to seek help. She was exhausted, terrified that
this pervasive fear would never lift, and desperately hoped to reclaim some semblance of the life
she felt had been stolen from her.
