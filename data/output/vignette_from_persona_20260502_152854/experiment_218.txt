air, and she remembered the sickening thud of bodies, the acrid smell of smoke, and the terrifying

resurfaced. She often felt it wasn't even her own memory, but something alien, detached, as if
observed from a distance, making it incredibly difficult to process or integrate.

incomprehensible, made her desperate to escape its clutches. She began to employ various maladaptive
strategies to push these terrifying thoughts away, primarily through thought suppression and an
almost relentless mental distraction. She would throw herself into her urban planning projects,
working long hours, meticulously designing cityscapes and public spaces, anything to keep her mind
occupied and prevent the images from breaking through. Yet, this constant effort to suppress her
thoughts and remain mentally distracted had an insidious effect; it paradoxically kept her nervous
system on high alert, creating a persistent sense of impending danger.

This heightened state of threat manifested acutely in her daily life. Unexpected movement nearby,
like a colleague suddenly turning in the office or a pedestrian stepping into her path, would cause
her to startle violently, reacting as if she were in immediate danger. What was worse, this constant
vigilance bled into her nights, making sleep a battle she often lost. She had difficulty falling
asleep because she felt an incessant need to stay alert, her senses scanning her quiet apartment for
any sign of a threat. This pervasive hyperarousal, a direct consequence of her attempts to suppress
and distract, profoundly impacted her memory. When the memory of the ambush did manage to break
through, it wasn't a coherent narrative, but a chaotic rush of flashbacks – suddenly feeling and
acting as if the stressful experience were actually happening again, the sounds and smells

constant state of alarm, only reinforced the sense that the memory was fragmented and impossible to
fully grasp, making it even harder to explain clearly to others, even to her partner.

distract and suppress thoughts about the trauma inadvertently made her more vulnerable to triggers.

the familiar faces and landscapes would transport her back to the war zone, the rage bubbling to the

terrifying memory of hiding in the foliage, causing a wave of panic. Even a sudden burst of shouting

preparing for another ambush. These triggers, once manageable, now felt like unexpected assaults,
and in moments of overwhelming emotional distress, when thought suppression and mental distraction

unbearable feelings.

The cycle had become self-reinforcing, tightening around her. The alien, rage-filled memory drove
her to suppress it, which in turn kept her hypervigilant, making the memory even more disjointed and
frightening when it did surface, and leaving her more susceptible to triggers. Her relationship was
strained, her partner feeling increasingly shut out by Lina's emotional distance and late nights
spent at the office. She finally sought therapy, exhausted and frightened, admitting that she was
terrified the memories would consume her entirely, leaving her forever trapped in a past that felt
increasingly like a foreign body within her own mind.
