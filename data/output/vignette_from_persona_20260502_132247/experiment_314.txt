In the weeks that followed, Kofi told no one. He returned to work, sat at his desk, and processed

percussion of doors opening and closing down the hallway, a raised voice from a phone call in the
next room — each of these now carries the sensory signature of the assault, and when they occur,

tightens, and he scans the room with a vigilance that exhausts him by midday. Being alone with any
male colleague — even briefly, even in a glass-walled conference room — produces a level of alarm
that he struggles to conceal behind the posture of professional composure.

These triggers do not merely frighten him in the present; they pull him backward. When a door slams

arrives as a thought. Sometimes there is no image at all, only the full emotional weight of that
night pressing down on him without context, a wave of terror and shame that he cannot locate in any
specific memory and therefore cannot reason his way out of. Most disorienting of all is the taste:
something metallic and organic that surfaces without warning, most often when he is sitting quietly
at his desk, and that makes the past feel physically present in a way that no amount of logical
reassurance can undo. These intrusive returns of the memory, in turn, make the triggers more potent
— each recurrence deepens his association between ordinary sensory events and catastrophic harm, so
that sounds and presences that once meant nothing now mean everything.

The heightened threat he feels in his body has its own momentum. When he is already in a state of
alarm — heart up, muscles braced — any new sensory input is processed through that lens, and the
memories come faster and more vividly. An unexpected hand on his shoulder from a supervisor, meant
kindly, sent him out of the office and into the stairwell for twenty minutes. He has stopped

What finally brought Kofi to seek help was a performance review in which his manager noted his

he was before that night no longer exists. What he is most hoping for is some way to sit in his own
office without feeling like it is still happening.
