happening, the nauseating surge of adrenaline that did not subside even after the officers walked
away. He drove home shaking, pulled over twice to be sick, and told no one.

not feel like recollections — they feel more real and vivid than anything else in his life, more
present than his daughter's last birthday or his first solo flight, as though that parking lot
exists in higher resolution than the rest of his history. And yet the event itself feels

remember it, the terror returns wholesale, not as an echo but as the thing itself, and it is this

making ordinary moments feel like precursors to something catastrophic, and it is in this way that

own mind has become the thing he cannot trust.
