site when a sedan ran a red light at speed and struck the passenger side of the vehicle — her side —

however, was not only the memory of the collision itself but the aftermath: no charges were filed
against the other driver, no accountability followed, and Kayla was left to manage her injuries and
her fear largely alone while the person responsible faced no consequences.

In the months that followed, certain moments began to carry an unbearable charge. Seeing cars in
proximity — particularly any vehicle that drifted or moved unexpectedly in her field of vision —
would send a flash of the impact through her mind with a vividness that felt less like remembering
and more like reliving. Riding in a vehicle with other people became nearly impossible; sitting in
the passenger seat of any car returned her to the truck cab, to the helplessness of having no
control, no warning, and no way to protect herself. Even conversations that turned toward the future
— plans, goals, where she might be in a year — would trigger something that felt like dread rather
than anticipation, as though the future itself had become an unsafe place.

What made these moments so destabilising was not only their sensory intensity but what they
confirmed to Kayla about herself and her situation. Each time a trigger broke through, it seemed to
her like evidence of something she had come to believe with quiet certainty: that her body could no

her control, and now it seemed to act independently of her will — flooding with panic, freezing,
responding to stimuli before she had any chance to reason through them. She was certain that she had
lost some fundamental reliability in herself, that in a moment of real danger she would not do the
right thing. And beneath all of this ran a current of fury and grief about the injustice of what had
happened — that something so violent and so preventable had been visited upon her, and that the
world had simply moved on. Nothing had been done. No one had been held responsible. That sense of
unresolved wrongdoing sat in her chest like a weight that never fully lifted.
