the train stopped. What he encountered was not something he had been prepared for: a man lying on
the gravel embankment, badly injured, with visible wounds that Karim could not look away from fast
enough. He remembers the cold most of all — the sharp, biting air against his face and hands as he
crouched beside the man and radioed for emergency services. The temperature that morning was in the
low thirties, and that particular quality of cold has become inseparable, in his nervous system,
from everything that followed.

In the weeks after the incident, Karim found that certain sensory details had taken on a life of

unremarkable, even invigorating — now arrived as a signal of something wrong, something dangerous.

something other people felt, not something available to him.
