was unable to narrate the experience even to himself. It existed in his body rather than in
language, and this wordlessness made it feel permanent and unprocessable, as though it were not
something that had happened to him but something that was still happening.

not safe in most places, that ordinary sounds — a door slamming in the barracks, a colleague's

faces and uniforms he might otherwise have tolerated became unbearable reminders of what his body

mattered to him, and he withdrew from colleagues at the unit beyond what was professionally

real man did; surviving what he had survived was something else entirely.

barely articulate it, was to find a way back to himself — or to discover whether there was still a
self to return to.
