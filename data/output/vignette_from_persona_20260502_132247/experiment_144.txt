Arjun is a 29-year-old South Asian man who owns a small convenience store in a mid-sized American
city. He lives with his long-term partner and, until recently, managed the daily rhythms of his shop
and his relationship with what he described as quiet competence. He presented for treatment
following a period of escalating distress that he could no longer attribute to stress alone,
reporting a PCL-5 score of 80 and describing a life that had quietly contracted around a wound he
had not yet named clearly to anyone.

The abuse was perpetrated by a former partner over a period of approximately eight months, ending
roughly two years before Arjun sought help. The violence was primarily physical — restraint,
strikes, and on several occasions sustained assault in enclosed spaces. What Arjun returns to most
often, though he struggles to frame it as significant, is the smell: his former partner's sweat
during those episodes, close and overwhelming in a way that became inseparable from the terror
itself. The assaults often began without warning during ordinary conversations — about money, about
plans, about nothing — so that the texture of certain exchanges became indistinguishable, in his
nervous system, from the moment before impact.

In the months following the relationship's end, Arjun noticed that specific sensory and situational
cues would instantly reconstitute the full memory without any transitional warning — not a gradual
recollection but an abrupt return, as though the present had simply been replaced. The smell of
sweat, most reliably, but also the drift of certain conversation topics, would pull the memory
forward in its entirety. When that happened, what arose alongside the images was not only fear but a
fierce, disorienting rage — rage at his former partner, but also, and more corrosively, at himself.
He had come to believe that he had failed in some fundamental way: that he had not done enough to
stop what happened, that he had stayed too long, that a man who could not protect himself from this
could not be trusted to protect anything. This conviction — that he could not rely on himself — had
become the interpretive lens through which he processed almost every subsequent difficulty.

That appraisal of himself as fundamentally unreliable did not stay contained. Because he could not
trust his own judgment or responses, he had grown hypervigilant to the possibility of threat from
outside — particularly from strangers who approached him in ordinary situations, as customers
routinely do in his shop. A man leaning across the counter to point at something, a raised voice
during a transaction, a customer who smelled of exertion after a workout — any of these could
activate the same alarm his body had learned to associate with real danger. At night, behind the
locked door of his home, the sense of safety he might reasonably have expected never fully arrived;
he lay awake most nights, alert to sounds, unable to cross into sleep without effort.

The cycle had grown self-reinforcing in ways that exhausted him. The memory, when it returned,
confirmed his worst appraisal of himself — that he was broken, reactive, unreliable — and that
appraisal in turn seemed to lower his threshold for triggering, as though believing himself to be in
permanent danger made the world more reliably dangerous. His body's continued reactivity — the
startle, the racing heart, the sweat of his own that he had begun to notice and find disturbing —
felt to him like evidence rather than symptom: proof that he was still, at some level, the person
who had not done enough. The sense of threat this self-appraisal generated kept him scanning, and
the scanning kept the triggers close. Sometimes, with no image attached at all, he would feel the
full weight of those months wash through him — the dread, the shame, the rage — without being able
to point to what had brought it on.

Arjun's partner has noticed the changes — the withdrawal from conversation, the guardedness with
customers that has begun to affect the warmth his shop was known for. It was his partner's quiet,
careful observation that he seemed to be suffering that finally moved Arjun to seek help. What he
fears most is that the self he knew before — capable, steady, someone others could count on — is
permanently gone. What he hopes, tentatively, is that he might be shown evidence to the contrary.
