pocket.

In the weeks that followed, the memory was a constant, unwelcome guest. It wasn't just a thought; it

and sharp, would flash behind his eyes. He would double over, a phantom ache blooming in his ribs,

needed to share it. He felt as though the experience was trapped inside his body, speaking only in
pain.

city, his home for a decade, was an fundamentally dangerous place, and that the people in it were

he now felt a constant, humming anxiety. A guest offering a friendly "good morning" in the hallway
would send a jolt of adrenaline through him. He found himself watching their hands, their eyes,

started taking circuitous routes through the service corridors to avoid the lobby, a change that

could not be trusted; their kindness was a mask, just like the smile on the young man’s face right
before the attack.

that created a quiet, persistent friction between them. He stopped going to the grocery store,
sending Priya with a list instead. He felt safest in their small apartment, with the door double-
