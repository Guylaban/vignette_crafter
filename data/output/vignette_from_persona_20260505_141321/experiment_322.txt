the very earth proved untrustworthy. He finished his work, filed his report, and went home, but some
essential part of his internal architecture had been fractured.

shoulder blades as if he were physically trying to hold the world together. The city, once a
familiar grid, now seemed filled with hidden threats. The rumble of a passing subway felt like a

hammering against his ribs. He began to feel a profound unease in any building that wasn't his own
home or his familiar office. He had come to believe the world was a fundamentally dangerous place,

feel intolerable. He could not assess the danger, could not see the structural reports, could not

hadn't personally vetted. The avoidance became a pattern. He turned down fieldwork, volunteering for
tedious data analysis instead, confining himself to the predictable geometry of his lab. He saw his
younger colleagues head out to scenes with an ease he no longer possessed, and a bitter shame took
root. His reactions since the event, he was certain, showed that he was a lousy coper. The man who
