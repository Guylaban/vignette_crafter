decades, now found his life governed by a rigid, unspoken set of rules. It had been two years since
his son, Manu, had recovered, but for Kelepi, the crisis had never truly ended. The night it
happened plays on a continuous loop in his mind, not as a coherent story, but as a series of sensory
fragments. The worst part, the part that ambushes him in the quiet of his office, was the sound—a
thin, high-pitched wheeze every time Manu, then sixteen, fought for a breath. Kelepi remembers the
frantic drive, his own knuckles white on the steering wheel, and the paralyzing feeling of
uselessness in the emergency room. He had stood by the door, a spectator to his own son's fight for
life, while a team of strangers in scrubs worked with an urgency that felt like an accusation. He
was the father; he was supposed to protect his son, and in that critical moment, all he could do was
watch. He had come to believe he should have seen it coming, that he had missed some sign, that his
failure to act sooner was the reason Manu had come so close to death.

This sense of failure now permeates every corner of his life. At work, where he was once known for
his decisive leadership, he is plagued by a profound self-doubt. He second-guesses every decision,
rereading emails a dozen times before sending, certain he can no longer trust himself to do the
right thing. Delays, once a simple logistical challenge, have become sources of acute dread. When a
contractor’s email takes an hour longer than expected to arrive, Kelepi doesn't feel impatience; he
feels a cold, sickening certainty that something catastrophic has occurred, and that it is, once
again, his fault for not foreseeing it. He has built his professional life around control and
planning, but he now feels he has no control at all. To cope, he fills every waking moment. His work
calendar is a fortress of back-to-back meetings, and his evenings are spent meticulously planning
home improvement projects he never starts. The goal is constant mental motion, a relentless forward
pace to outrun the silence where the images and sounds of that night reside.

encounter with one of Manu's respiratory therapists in the grocery store last year sent a jolt of

avoid the sight of the hospital. The most terrifying trigger, however, comes from within. Any hint

him back into the horror of that night. His own chest tightens, his heart pounds, and for a moment,
he is no longer a man in his sixties but a helpless father listening to his son suffocate.

keep his family safe again.
