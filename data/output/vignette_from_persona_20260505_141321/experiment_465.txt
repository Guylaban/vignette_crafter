the silver blade catching the fluorescent light. That glint of metal is one of the few things that
remains clear to her. The rest is a chaotic jumble of sensations she cannot assemble into a coherent
story: the cold of the linoleum floor against her skin, the smell of stale coffee, the weight of his
body. She knows what happened, but when she tries to form the words, they dissolve in her throat,
leaving only a thick, choking rage that feels as violent as the act itself.

In the weeks that followed, Sofia’s world shrank. The initial belief that she could push through,

safe," but his words were like a foreign language. Her body knew better. She had come to believe
that safety was an illusion, a story people told themselves. This feeling was most acute after
sunset; as darkness fell, the house that was once her sanctuary transformed into a place of hidden
corners and threatening shadows, and she would lie awake for hours, listening, waiting.

like a monumental failure. The self-recrimination was relentless. A stronger person, she was
certain, would never have stayed at the office so late alone. Somebody else would have fought back
harder, would not have frozen. She saw this weakness in herself as a fundamental flaw, a stain that

the sun goes down.
