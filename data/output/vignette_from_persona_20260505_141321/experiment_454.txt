in your own mind."

began with Mr. Henderson in apartment 2B. At first, it was just his eyes, how they would linger on
her a moment too long in the hallway. Then came the waiting. He would be there when she left for
work in the morning and there when she returned, always with the faint, sweet-sickly smell of
whiskey on his breath. The terror culminated one evening in the basement laundry room. He had
cornered her between the humming machines, grabbing her wrist with a grip that felt like it could

slurring a crude comment, the only thing she could register was the overpowering taste in her own
mouth: the cardamom from the chai she’d just finished, now curdled with the metallic tang of pure
panic.

Even after the police were called and Mr. Henderson was gone, the feeling of being hunted remained.

predatory gaze, the sudden, electric pain in her wrist, and always, that cloying, sickening taste
that can rise in her throat without warning. She finds herself pushing the memory away before his
words can fully form in her mind, a mental flinching from a blow that has already landed. She cannot
construct a coherent narrative of what happened, not even for herself.

Her world has since been remapped according to threat. At home, a place that was once her sanctuary,

groan of the pipes. She is certain that any unusual stillness is a prelude to danger. Before bed,
she performs a ritual, checking the deadbolt on the front door three times, then ensuring every
window latch is firmly in place.

the sour chai taste flooded her mouth. A sharp, phantom pain shot through her wrist, and she had to
grip the counter to steady herself. Any man’s lingering glance on the bus or in the grocery store
feels like a violation, a stripping away of her safety. She has come to believe that the world is a
fundamentally dangerous place, especially for the people she loves. She worries obsessively about
her son at school or her husband on the road, her mind conjuring vivid, catastrophic scenarios until

one day, she might be able to walk out her own front door and feel nothing but the warmth of the
