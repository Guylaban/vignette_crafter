shelter, only the constant, terrifying possibility of ambush or collapse.

This history intrudes upon her present in jarring and specific ways. A few months ago, her

and open fields were not a place of recreation; they were a place of exposure, of being hunted. The
sound of wind through tall grass is, for her, the sound of imminent danger. She feigned a sudden

body or future.

daughter, but what frightens her most is the quiet, persistent belief that she will never truly be
