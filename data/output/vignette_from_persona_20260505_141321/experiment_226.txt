Two years later, and a world away, Layla works as a database administrator. Her life is a fortress

digital world where nothing is out of place. This need for control, however, spills out beyond her
monitor. Her small apartment is immaculate, not just clean but obsessively organized. When a wave of
anxiety hits, often in the late afternoon as the light begins to change, she finds herself on her
hands and knees, scrubbing the baseboards, or reorganizing her spice rack into alphabetical order.
She knows, on some level, that it is a way to feel in command of something, to impose a predictable
order on a world that had once proven to be terrifyingly random.

Her most rigid rule, the one that has reshaped her entire existence, is her curfew. Layla does not

after-work drinks or dinners. The isolation is a dull, constant ache, but it feels like a necessary
price for safety. To her, the darkness is not simply an absence of light; it is a malevolent

caught outside after dark, the world would once again reveal its capacity for sudden, violent
upheaval.

carrying the heavy bags. Any physical strain feels like a betrayal by her own body, a signal that it
is once again on the verge of being crushed and broken.

skin without believing it was the last breath she would ever take.
