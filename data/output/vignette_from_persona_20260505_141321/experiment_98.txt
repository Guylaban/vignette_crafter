across the street, shifting his weight from foot to foot. Adekunle felt a prickle of unease but

liquor reached Adekunle as he fumbled with his keys.

The attack was a blur of motion and sharp, disjointed sensations. What sticks with him are not the
events in order, but the searing, still images. He sees the man’s face, twisted in a sneer, the

memory isn’t a story he can tell; it’s a broken film that plays on a loop in his mind, silent but
for the man’s words. After taking his wallet and the day’s cash box, the man had kicked his leg.

find the words. He knows, he can see it all perfectly, but the path from the images in his head to
the words in his mouth feels impossibly broken.

In the weeks that followed, the world became a landscape of hidden threats. The salon, once his

breath catch, his hands beginning to tremble so violently he had to pretend to search for a
different comb until the shaking subsided. He began to watch people with a new, exhausting

the most innocuous. When a younger stylist gently corrected him on a new colouring technique,
suggesting a different approach, the shame was immediate and absolute. He heard not her helpful
tone, but the mugger’s sneering voice calling him pathetic.

To manage the terror, Adekunle began to retreat. He tried with all his might to suppress the
thoughts, to push the looping film of images out of his head, but it was like trying to hold back
the tide. So he started avoiding the triggers instead. He began cancelling appointments, especially

pictures and a stranger to his own life.
