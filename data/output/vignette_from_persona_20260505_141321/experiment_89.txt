memories in the typical sense; they are invasions. Last week, while walking to the local market, he

threats. The shop is on a busy industrial road, and the sound of a truck downshifting or the sudden

himself bracing for an impact that never comes, his muscles coiling tight. More than once, he has

actively welding, trying to build a wall of silence against the world, but it only makes him feel
more isolated.

barbecues, but a few months ago, his cousin opened a beer near him, and the faint smell of alcohol
sent him spiraling. He felt the phantom pain flare in his leg so intensely he had to limp away,

and to feel, for the first time in six months, that he is living in the present, not in the wreckage
of the past.
