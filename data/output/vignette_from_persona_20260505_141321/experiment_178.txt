coiling in his stomach with every stop, knowing an argument was inevitable. The explosion happened

his ribs and a profound sense of shock.

In the months that followed, the world began to shrink. The bus became an impossible space; the

heart pound. He now takes expensive ride-shares or walks, adding an hour to his commute and
significant strain to his finances. As a journalist, his work requires him to meet and interview
people, often one-on-one. He now finds any meeting where he is alone with a man to be an exercise in
controlled panic. He focuses on the exit, maps the distance to the door, and can barely retain the
details of the conversation, convinced that you can never truly know who might harm you. This has
made his work feel precarious, his once-sharp interview skills blunted by overwhelming fear. His
editor has commented on the declining quality of his recent pieces.

now, complete with the phantom ache blooming in his side, a somatic echo of the real impact. The
wave of shame and terror that accompanies it is so overwhelming that he has to physically stop what
he is doing, often retreating to the bathroom to grip the sink until the world solidifies around him
again.

street below. His new partner, Minh, is gentle and reassuring, but Bao finds that even when Minh
tells him he is safe, the words feel hollow. He has come to believe that people simply can't be
trusted, that a calm surface can hide anything. This conviction plays out in agonizing ways; if Minh
is ten minutes late coming home from work, Bao’s mind doesn’t conjure images of traffic but of
betrayal or disaster. He has learned to smother any flicker of his own anger, viewing it as a
dangerous, volatile force that must be suppressed at all costs.

This vigilance is fueled by a relentless internal monologue of self-blame. He spends hours
ruminating on what he must have done wrong to provoke the attack, convinced that he is fundamentally
inadequate. This belief manifests in a quiet, punishing form of control: restricting his food. Some
days he eats very little, the feeling of hunger a familiar, grounding distraction from the emotional
chaos. He came to therapy after a weekend away with Minh, a trip that was meant to be restorative

confusion in Minh’s eyes and realised he was destroying the first safe relationship he had known.

one day feel safe in his own body, in his own home, again.
