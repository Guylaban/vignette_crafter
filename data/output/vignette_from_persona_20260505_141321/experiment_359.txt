his life.

welcome distraction from the silence of his mind. He told himself this relentless busyness was
dedication, but he knew it was a desperate attempt to outrun the memories. The strategy had cost him

utterly real, a present-tense horror rather than a recollection of something past. He would have to

provided a sharp, grounding anchor back to reality.

compulsive assessment. His eyes would dart around, mapping every exit, noting the placement of fire

engage in a meticulous ritual, checking the lock on the main door, then the windows, then the fire
escape, circling back to the main door two or three more times before he could finally force himself
to leave.

Amir sought therapy after a particularly severe episode at his studio. A sudden memory, triggered by
