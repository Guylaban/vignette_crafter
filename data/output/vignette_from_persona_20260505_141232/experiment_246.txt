someone shouting to run. Yet even as those scenes replay, there are stretches he cannot recover at
all. He has tried, repeatedly, to force himself to remember the missing pieces, but they remain
blank. At other times he can describe parts of the event in a flat, distant way that unnerves him,
as though he is reciting something that happened to another man.

In the weeks afterward, Kwame did not experience the event as something finished. The same images
kept returning, sudden and repetitive, often when he was alone at night or when the shop was quiet.

immediately look for exits before he noticed anything else. In waiting rooms, restaurants, even
church gatherings he stopped attending regularly, he could not settle unless he positioned himself
with his back against a wall and his eyes on the room. He told himself this was just being smart
now, the kind of thing a person should do if he wanted to stay alive. What made him most uneasy was
how automatic it had become. He was always on guard, always checking who had come in, what sound had
changed, where he could run.
