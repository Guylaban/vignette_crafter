happening to someone she loved and believed she should have kept safe. She had been very close to

day of the event, the two of them had gone together off church grounds for an ordinary errand.

instant the scene changed. She can describe particular details with exactness—the angle of her
companion’s body, the look on her face, the sickening awareness that something irreversible had
happened—but when she tries to recount the next several moments, there are blanks she cannot fill.

is the conviction that she did not do what she should have done.

religious life, but internally her days narrowed. She had come to believe the event happened because
of the way she acted that day. If she had suggested a different route, if she had walked on the
other side, if she had been more alert, if she had somehow anticipated danger, then the other sister
might still be alive. She says this not as speculation but with the certainty of a moral verdict.

longer trusts her own judgment, because in her mind she has already seen what her judgment can cost.
