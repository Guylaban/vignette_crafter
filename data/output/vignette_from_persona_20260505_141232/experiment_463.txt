then, what has settled in her mind is not only that he died unexpectedly, but that she had one last
chance to protect him and failed.

with the rigid self-control she has relied on all her life. Friends commented on her composure, but
privately she took no comfort in that. She had already begun measuring herself against an

lost her train of thought during a conversation, if she woke in the night with the same scene in her
mind of his back disappearing through the front door, she interpreted these reactions as evidence
that she was, in her words, “a lousy coper.” At her age, with her professional stature, she believed
she should have been able to bear grief differently. Instead, each moment of emotional overwhelm
seemed to confirm for her that something in her was weak and inadequate.

What has remained most prominent is her conviction that the death happened because of the way she
acted. She does not claim she caused the crash in any literal legal sense; as a judge, she is

house. She returns repeatedly to small details as if a different choice might still be found inside
