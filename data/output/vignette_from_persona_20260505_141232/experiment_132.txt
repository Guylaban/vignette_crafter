instant of danger and his own furious certainty that he should have done more.

studio building could make his whole body jolt before he knew what had happened. He hated how
visible this was. While assistants or clients laughed off a noise, Minh was already tense, scanning,
and ashamed of reacting so fast. Even in settings he knew were safe, he could not settle. He would
sit editing photos late at night in his room, shoulders locked, listening for sounds in the hallway
or outside the window, telling himself there was no threat and finding that his body refused to
believe him.

the past surged forward and the current scene lost substance. With that return came intense rage:
rage at what had happened, at the people involved, at himself for not having prevented the losses he
believed were his to prevent. He was often more frightened by the force of that anger than by the
memory itself.

whole scene again. What frightened him most was the possibility that this was permanent—that he
would remain stranded between the vividness of that moment and a present life in which he no longer
