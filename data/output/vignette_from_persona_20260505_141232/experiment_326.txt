disciplined, practical way he had built his life after emigrating with his family as a child and
later settling into an American adulthood shaped by work, marriage, and routine. The event that now
occupied so much of his inner life happened not in his station at home but during a period when he

arrived at the scene before his words could. He knew what happened, he said, but he could not find
language that did not fail it.

blast itself was what he believed he had done and failed to do afterward. He had come to see his own
actions in that setting as going against the person he thought he was. He had entered the work with
a firefighter’s ethic of protecting the vulnerable, and what tormented him now was not only the

tightening jaw and eyes fixed away from the therapist, but the shame in him was unmistakable.

In the months after he came home, reminders appeared in ways that felt humiliatingly unpredictable.

he had even identified the sound. On those days the old pain came back with the memory, as though
the event were not recollection but re-entry. He found himself bracing when a room fell unusually
quiet, becoming certain that silence meant something terrible was about to happen. At the station,
during the strange still moments between calls, he would scan hallways and doorways, unable to
settle. The younger men sometimes read this as impatience or moodiness. Minh experienced it as
dread.

anger out of proportion to the moment. To Minh it did not feel out of proportion. It felt like proof
that people were capable of anything, and that the world he had returned to was not governed by the
values he once trusted.
