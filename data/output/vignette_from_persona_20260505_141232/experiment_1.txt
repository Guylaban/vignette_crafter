suspicious package, but the memory of months spent in pediatric hospital corridors while his child

the ward, the transparent drip chamber hanging beside the bed, the flash of a needle in a nurse’s
gloved hand, his son’s small body under white blankets. He remembers the smell as vividly as the
images, the antiseptic odor of cleaned floors and hand gel that seemed to cling to his clothes and
skin even after he went home. When these memories come back, they do not arrive as distant
recollections. He says they descend all at once, without warning, as if he is back in the room and
everything is happening again across all his senses.

During the worst period of his son’s illness, Rajesh kept himself moving by watching monitors,
tracking medications, and trying to anticipate what the doctors might say next. He stood near the
bed studying the IV lines and the equipment, certain that if he let his attention slip, something
catastrophic might happen. He could not shake the feeling that his son’s life was hanging on details
he might miss. Even after the immediate medical crisis passed, that posture of readiness did not
leave him. At night he began having difficulty falling asleep because it felt unsafe to fully let
go. He told his wife that he needed to stay alert, though he knew they were home and not in the
hospital. Lying in bed, he would notice every sound in the house and remain tense, as if he were
still waiting for a monitor alarm or footsteps in the hallway.

What catches him now is often ordinary and immediate. If he walks into a medical building with his

poles, or drips can do the same. Even outside hospitals, being around children can suddenly pull him

when he goes. He does not explain much in the moment. What he says later is that he cannot trust
himself not to react.

after a sudden, immersive flash of the hospital room when he worries that these experiences mean he
is going crazy. He knows, at one level, where he is, but the speed and totality with which the

matter how disciplined or prepared he is.

he came in, what he most wanted was not simply to “calm down,” as he put it, but to understand why
his mind keeps thrusting him back there and to find some way of being with his family, and around
children, without feeling that catastrophe is about to happen again.
