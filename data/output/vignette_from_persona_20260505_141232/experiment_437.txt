left a mark on him that was still present when he came for treatment.

lingered over loading tasks and looked for extra things to handle around the truck because being
still felt dangerous in a way he struggled to explain. When his mind started drifting back toward
the assault, he pushed it away quickly. He had come to believe that if he let himself think about

midstream, turned on the television, went out to the driveway, reorganized tools, or found some
reason to leave the room.

usual, especially in the evening after work, moving from dinner to snacks and then back into the
kitchen again without really being hungry. He described it less as enjoyment than as something to do
with himself, a way to settle the agitation in his body and blunt the edge of his distress. He did
not present this to himself as a problem so much as a practical measure: if he could keep his hands
busy and his mind on something immediate, maybe the rest would stay contained.
