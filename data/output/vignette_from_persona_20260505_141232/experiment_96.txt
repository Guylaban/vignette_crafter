vehicle had slowed near a crowded checkpoint when an explosion tore through the area ahead of them.
Minh remembered the blast first as light and force before anything made sense: a violent flash,
shattered glass, people dropping, smoke swallowing the road. What stayed with him were fragments
rather than a smooth story—images of a body crumpled beside a barrier, a child’s shoe in the dust,
the windshield starred with cracks—and, just as sharply, sound. In his mind he still heard the
concussive boom, then screaming, then the metallic clatter and shriek that followed as vehicles
stopped and people scrambled.

In the days after, Minh told himself he should have done something different. He repeatedly returned
to the same thoughts: he should have noticed something suspicious on the road, should have insisted
they take another route, should have reacted faster. Even when others reminded him that the bombing
had been sudden and indiscriminate, he blamed himself for what happened. He went over the sequence
again and again, searching for the moment where, in his mind, he had failed. Yet when he tried to
describe the event aloud, he would sometimes stop and look confused, as if he were recounting
something that belonged to someone else. The memory felt so extreme that it was hard for him to
believe it had happened to him, Minh, a man in his seventies who had spent a lifetime helping others
survive upheaval. He could recount certain details vividly, but the whole thing still had an unreal
quality, as though it were a scene from a film that had somehow attached itself to his life.

the floor at the resettlement office would make him jump hard in his chair. He became constantly
watchful, scanning entrances, windows, and the faces of strangers in any public place. He no longer
felt broadly safe in the world. Safety shrank to a few tightly defined places—his own bedroom, one

than risk confrontation. To Minh, this made sense: conflict no longer felt like a normal part of
life but like the beginning of something that could suddenly escalate beyond control.
