### Clinical Vignette for Mr. Tevita Finau Mr. Tevita Finau, a 65-year-old widowed Oceania man and
former Project Manager, has been struggling with the aftermath of his son's life-threatening illness
that occurred several years ago. The event was a harrowing experience for him, as he witnessed his
son’s deteriorating health and the frantic medical procedures that followed. Since then, Tevita has
found himself constantly on edge whenever reminders of those traumatic moments arise. Tevita’s
triggers include people related to the event—medical staff and family members involved in his son's
care—and specific somatic sensations such as breathlessness or undergoing a medical examination.
These triggers have become particularly potent because they remind him of the intense fear and
helplessness he felt during that time. Whenever Tevita sees someone who looks like one of the
doctors from the hospital, he immediately assumes something bad is happening, which intensifies his
physical reactions like shortness of breath or rapid heartbeat. He fears these sensations are
indicators of another impending crisis. Tevita's negative appraisals have deeply affected how he
handles these triggers and threats. He believes that if only he had acted differently during the
illness, perhaps things could have turned out better for his son. This self-blame has led him to
suppress thoughts about the trauma as much as possible. He constantly distracts himself with work or
other activities to avoid dwelling on those painful memories. However, this suppression and
distraction often lead to feelings of guilt and doubt, making it harder for him to trust his own
judgment in critical situations. The self-doubt Tevita harbors has also caused him to become
increasingly avoidant of medical cues. He avoids hospitals, ambulances, and physical examinations at
all costs because these places are potent reminders of the helplessness he felt during his son’s
illness. This avoidance only exacerbates his negative appraisals, as he feels that his inability to
face these situations confirms his doubts about himself. Tevita's perception of threat is heightened
when something takes longer than expected. He assumes the worst in such scenarios, which often
triggers a flood of strong physical and emotional reactions. For instance, if he notices someone
walking slowly or if there’s a delay at work, he immediately begins to worry that another crisis
might be unfolding. Over time, these maladaptive strategies have become deeply ingrained habits for
Tevita. His constant need to keep his mind occupied all the time has led to significant occupational
stress. He often finds himself micromanaging tasks and constantly checking in on projects, which has
caused tension with his colleagues and even affected his performance at work. Additionally, these
behaviors have strained his relationships with friends and family, who find it hard to understand
why he can't relax or enjoy leisure time. What finally brought Tevita to seek help was the
realization that his avoidance and self-doubt were affecting every aspect of his life. He is most
afraid of losing control again and being unable to protect others in critical situations. However,
what gives him hope is the possibility of understanding and managing these fears better so he can
regain a sense of stability and trust in himself. Through this journey, Tevita hopes to find ways to
cope with the trauma without it controlling his life entirely. He wants to learn how to face the
triggers that have been holding him back for years and rebuild his confidence in making decisions.
