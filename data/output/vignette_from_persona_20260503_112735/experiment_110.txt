### Vignette for Ms. Maria Lopez Ms. Maria Lopez, a 60-year-old divorced politician from North
America, has been grappling with intense distress following an incident where she inadvertently
caused serious injury to another individual. The traumatic event left her feeling permanently
humiliated and consumed by guilt over not preventing the accident. As a result of this experience,
Ms. Lopez has developed a complex set of symptoms that have significantly impacted her daily life.
The incident occurred during a political rally in her constituency, where she was addressing an
audience of supporters. During her speech, a sudden, loud shout from someone in the crowd startled
Maria, causing her to veer off course and accidentally hit another individual with her vehicle. The
injured person suffered severe neck injuries that required immediate medical attention. This event
has become seared into Maria's memory, particularly through vivid crash imagery of cars colliding.
Since then, any sound similar to shouting triggers strong physical reactions in Maria, such as heart
palpitations and sweaty palms. These triggers not only evoke the traumatic memory but also deepen
her sense of humiliation and self-blame, reinforcing her belief that she was responsible for the
accident due to her actions. As a result, every time she hears a shout or experiences neck pain
reminiscent of the event, she feels overwhelmed by fear, guilt, and anger. Maria's distress is
further compounded by her difficulty in deliberately recalling specific details of the incident.
When she does manage to access these memories, they are accompanied by auditory hallucinations of
the shouting and intense physical pain in her neck. These memory intrusions often occur
spontaneously during the day or while driving, leading her to avoid all forms of vehicles
altogether. Her efforts to avoid stressors have only exacerbated her condition. She has become
extremely cautious about any situation that could potentially be stressful, avoiding not just
driving but also social gatherings and public appearances where she might face similar triggers.
This avoidance strategy has taken a toll on both her professional life as a politician and her
personal relationships. Her colleagues often notice her reluctance to engage in public events or
even attend routine meetings, which has strained her work environment. The negative appraisals Maria
holds about herself have become deeply entrenched over time. She is certain that she will never
forgive herself for not doing more to stop the accident, and this guilt fuels her constant vigilance
against any reminders of the event. Her self-blame and sense of humiliation lead her to avoid any
feelings altogether—both negative and positive. This avoidance has created a cycle where her fear of
triggers intensifies her physical reactivity and negative emotions. In recent weeks, Maria's
symptoms have become so severe that she finds it increasingly difficult to concentrate on her work
or maintain social connections. Her constant state of hypervigilance means that any sound or sight
reminiscent of the accident can trigger a cascade of overwhelming emotions and intrusive memories.
These experiences make her feel like she is trapped in a cycle of distress, where her efforts to
avoid stressors only reinforce her negative appraisals and intensify her threat responses. The
culmination of these factors finally brought Maria to seek help when she realized that her avoidance
behaviors were significantly impacting her ability to function as a politician and maintain
meaningful relationships. She is most afraid of being reminded of the accident and feeling
permanently humiliated, yet she also hopes for a way to regain control over her life and find peace
with what happened. Maria's journey toward healing will involve addressing these intertwined
symptoms—understanding how triggers activate her negative appraisals, which in turn lead to
maladaptive strategies that further exacerbate her condition. By working through these
interconnections, Maria can begin to break the cycle of distress and regain a sense of control over
her life.
