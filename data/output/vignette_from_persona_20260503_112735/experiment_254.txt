point in John’s life. Since then, John has found himself reacting to specific triggers that remind
him of the traumatic day. Certain conversation topics, such as discussions about terrorism or

the attack is especially difficult for him; it brings up feelings of dread and a sense of reliving
the trauma all over again. John has developed several maladaptive strategies to cope with these
triggers and intrusive memories. He tends to avoid situations that might make him feel happy or
hopeful, as he fears such moments could suddenly be interrupted by vivid recollections of the
attack. He also finds himself drifting off into a world of his own—a form of fantasy escape that
allows him to momentarily detach from reality. Additionally, John avoids crowded places like
shopping centers or public events, where the chaos and unpredictability remind him too much of what
he experienced during the terrorist act. The vividness of these memories plays a significant role in
shaping John’s avoidance behaviors. The clarity with which he remembers the event means that even
small triggers can bring back the full intensity of the memory. For instance, hearing people
misbehaving or witnessing disorderly conduct at work often leads him to feel as though he is
reliving the attack. This sense of reliving the trauma further reinforces his tendency to avoid
situations and conversations that could trigger such memories. Over time, John’s avoidance behaviors
have started to impact his daily life and relationships. His wife has noticed how frequently he
avoids social gatherings or even minor outings, which can feel isolating for both of them. At work,
avoiding positive events and crowds means that he often stays in the library alone during lunch
breaks, further reinforcing his sense of isolation. John’s fear of being triggered is a central
concern in his life. He has come to believe that any moment could suddenly remind him of the attack,
causing an overwhelming flood of memories and emotions. This constant vigilance has led him to
develop additional avoidance strategies, such as steering clear of conversations or environments

distressing anniversary of the event. The memories were so vivid that they disrupted his daily
routine, causing significant anxiety and making it difficult for him to focus on work or enjoy time
with his family. He is most afraid of losing control over these intrusive memories and hopes that
therapy will provide some relief from the constant fear and vigilance. In his current state, John is
acutely aware of how deeply this trauma has impacted his life. He is seeking help not only to manage
his symptoms but also to regain a sense of normalcy and hope for the future.
