Ava, a 29-year-old dancer from Oceania, was attending a performance in the city center when a
terrorist attack struck. The event left her with fragmented memories and profound emotional distress
that she struggles to articulate. Ava had come to believe that nothing in her life felt meaningful
anymore—a sentiment reinforced by the fact that no one responsible for the incident faced
consequences. This sense of loss extended to her connection with her community, leaving her feeling
isolated. Since the attack, hearing certain names or engaging in specific conversations would
trigger intense fear within her. These triggers reminded her not just of the event itself but also
of the entrapment she felt during it. The fragmented memories of that day often surfaced as
disconnected pieces, sometimes without any clear image but with an overwhelming sense of emotional
intensity. This fragmentation only deepened her feeling of being lost and alienated from both her
culture and community. The triggers further exacerbated Ava's heightened state of threat. She became
acutely aware of strangers approaching her even in mundane situations, and loud or unexpected sounds
kept her on high alert. The constant vigilance was exhausting, and she found herself constantly
listening for any signs that someone might be entering her home. These persistent threats made it
difficult to focus on dancing, which once brought her joy but now seemed devoid of meaning. In
response to these overwhelming feelings, Ava had developed various maladaptive strategies. She
avoided enclosed spaces entirely, fearing they could bring back the entrapment she experienced
during the attack. To cope with the distressing memories and heightened sense of threat, she often
forced herself to think only positive thoughts—a form of positive reframing that provided temporary
relief but did not address the underlying issues. Ava frequently sought meaning in her experience by
thinking about why this event had happened to her. This search for meaning was fueled by her
negative appraisals, particularly her sense of loss and disconnection from others. These beliefs led
her to further avoidance behaviors and reinforced her fragmented memories of the event. Over time,
these patterns created a self-reinforcing cycle that tightened around Ava. Her maladaptive
strategies, such as avoiding enclosed spaces and forcing positive thoughts, often failed to
alleviate the intrusive memories and heightened sense of threat. Instead, they only served to
exacerbate her feelings of isolation and meaninglessness. The cumulative impact on her life was
significant. Dancing, which once brought her joy and a sense of community, became a source of
anxiety. The constant vigilance and avoidance behaviors affected her performance and interactions
with other dancers. She found herself withdrawing from performances and rehearsals, further
isolating herself both professionally and socially. Despite these challenges, Ava’s fear of being
overwhelmed by her memories and the persistent threat kept her in a state of chronic distress. It
was this unrelenting cycle that finally drove her to seek help. What frightened her most was not
just reliving the trauma but losing touch with what once gave her life purpose and connection. In
therapy, she hopes to regain some sense of control over her thoughts and emotions. Ava is most
hoping for a way to reconnect with her community and find meaning again in her life, while also
learning healthier coping mechanisms that don't lead to further avoidance and isolation.
