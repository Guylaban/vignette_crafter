began when Amir was around 5 years old. His father, driven by unchecked anger and frustration, would
lash out physically during arguments. These incidents were unpredictable and often left Amir feeling
helpless and terrified. The most vivid memories involve his father's face contorted with rage, the
sound of slaps or punches, and the intense need to escape. Amir’s experiences have left him highly
sensitive to triggers such as arguments, affective expressions like crying, and unexpected tactile
sensations. These triggers often remind him of those terrifying moments from childhood. For
instance, a heated debate at work can immediately bring back memories of his father's rage, causing
Amir to feel unsafe and overwhelmed. Amir’s sense of threat is pervasive and shapes much of his
behavior. He feels that he must always be aware of potential dangers, ensuring his back is against a
wall where he can see everything around him. This hyper-vigilance extends to choosing seats based on
how safe they make him feel and constantly studying people's faces for signs of danger. The more
threatened Amir feels, the more intensely he avoids situations that might trigger these feelings.
His maladaptive strategies have evolved over time as a means to cope with his overwhelming sense of
threat. Amir tends to avoid conflict at all costs, steering clear of any situation where there could
be disagreement or confrontation. This avoidance extends to avoiding being alone in rooms or
buildings, and even avoiding people altogether when possible. These behaviors are not just about
immediate safety but also serve as a way to prevent the emotional distress that can arise from
reminders of his past. For example, during a recent team meeting at work, a colleague raised their
voice while discussing project timelines, which quickly triggered Amir’s memories of his father's
yelling. He felt an acute sense of threat, and his need for safety became overwhelming. As a result,
he left the meeting abruptly, citing discomfort as a reason. Amir’s behaviors have begun to impact
his work and personal life. His avoidance has made it difficult for him to engage in collaborative
efforts at the lab, leading to tension with colleagues who feel he is not fully participating. At
home, his tendency to avoid conflict has sometimes left his wife feeling unheard or unimportant when
they need to discuss issues. What frightens Amir most is the possibility of being caught off guard
by a trigger and losing control over his emotions. The idea that he might not be able to manage
these feelings in public settings terrifies him deeply. This fear drives him to continue avoiding
situations where triggers are likely to arise, creating a cycle of increasing isolation. Despite
these challenges, Amir is seeking help because the weight of his symptoms has become too heavy to
bear alone. He hopes for a way to navigate his daily life without constant anxiety and a sense of
impending threat. His goal is to find strategies that allow him to feel safe enough to engage fully
in both his work as an environmental scientist and his marriage. In summary, Amir’s clinical
portrait illustrates how his childhood trauma has deeply influenced his current experiences. The
interplay between triggers, maladaptive strategies, and perceived threats forms a tight loop that
continues to reinforce his fears and avoidance behaviors. Seeking help represents a critical step
for Amir in breaking this cycle and reclaiming a sense of safety and control over his life.
