whenever he recalled the traumatic incident. These memories often felt alienated from his own

recall and physical pain he experienced during these flashbacks were vivid reminders of the trauma.
The memory of what happened continues to haunt Samuel’s daily life. He would often hear the loud
commands from that night ringing in his head as if it were happening again, and the physical pain in
his shoulder where he was struck seemed almost real. These memories triggered a relentless cycle of
maladaptive strategies: he started avoiding anything that could cause negative or positive feelings,
believing that any strong emotion might bring back unwanted memories. He also avoided stressful
situations entirely, leading him to miss important dental conferences and social gatherings.
Samuel’s intense fear of strangers approaching him in ordinary situations was another critical
component of his post-traumatic stress disorder (PTSD). The constant threat he perceived from the
outside world made it difficult for him to feel safe, even within his own home or office. This

irrevocably destroyed by the trauma and that the injustice of what happened to him was never
