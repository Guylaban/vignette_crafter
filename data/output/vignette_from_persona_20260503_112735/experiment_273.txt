Ahmad is a 37-year-old Middle Eastern man who works as a firefighter and lives with his wife in a
suburban neighborhood. Ahmad’s life changed dramatically after he became the target of an intense
stalking incident that left him feeling unsafe and overwhelmed. The traumatic event occurred about
six months ago, when a former colleague began to stalk Ahmad relentlessly, following him to work and
even showing up at his home unannounced. Since then, Ahmad has been acutely aware of specific
triggers that remind him of the incident. For example, whenever he rides his bicycle or visits
locations where he had previously encountered his stalker, he feels an intense sense of
vulnerability. These spatial triggers often lead to vivid and overwhelming memories of being
followed and tracked. One day while riding his bike home from work, Ahmad suddenly found himself in
a place where the stalker had confronted him before. The memory came back unbidden, leaving him
feeling so overwhelmed that he had to stop cycling and sit by the roadside until the intensity
passed. These triggers have also reinforced Ahmad’s negative appraisals about himself and his
situation. He believes he has permanently changed for the worse since the stalking began. This
belief in his permanent damage often manifests as fear that his reactions mean he is going crazy,
and it makes him feel weak and unable to cope with everyday life. For instance, he has started
scanning every room he enters for exits and watching people closely for any signs of potential harm.
These actions are driven by his deep-seated belief that he must be vigilant at all times. The memory
itself adds another layer of distress. Ahmad’s inability to find words to describe what happened
leaves him feeling isolated and misunderstood. When the memories return, they often do so without
warning, triggered by something in his surroundings. Once triggered, the memories overwhelm him with
a flood of emotions that he struggles to articulate or process. These overwhelming memories not only
reinforce his negative appraisals but also increase his sense of threat. For example, whenever Ahmad
finds himself in locations where he has been followed before, the memories flood back, heightening
his fear and making him feel even more threatened by unexpected sounds or sudden movements from
others around him. This heightened sense of threat further solidifies his belief that he is weak and
unable to handle these situations. Over time, Ahmad’s occupational life as a firefighter has been
impacted significantly. He struggles to perform his duties with the same confidence and
effectiveness due to his constant vigilance and fear of being followed or tracked in public spaces.
His performance at work has declined, leading to concerns from his colleagues and supervisors about
his ability to handle emergencies effectively. Ahmad’s marriage has also suffered as a result of
these symptoms. He often withdraws from social activities with his wife, fearing that he might be
triggered by something she says or does. This has led to increased tension in their relationship and
left Ahmad feeling more isolated than ever before. Despite the intensity of his symptoms, it was
only after a particularly distressing incident at work that Ahmad decided to seek help. He had been
assigned to a call near one of the locations where he had encountered his stalker, and the memories
overwhelmed him so much that he became unable to focus on his tasks. This realization that his
reactions were not just affecting his personal life but also endangering his professional
