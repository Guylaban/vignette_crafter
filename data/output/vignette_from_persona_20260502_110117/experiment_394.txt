crisis in her mind; it had become proof of something about herself. Mariam had come to believe that

the world feel arranged around danger. She became watchful for signs that something terrible was
about to happen again, bracing herself whenever a baby cried in a store, whenever she saw a child
coughing, whenever her phone rang late at night. What frightened her most was not only remembering
the hospital but the instant rush of fear, anger, shame, and guilt that came with it, as if the
danger had returned before she had time to think.

In the weeks that followed, Mariam noticed that her mind would not hold onto one task for long. At

lists, and staring at a conveyor belt while her thoughts drifted back to the sight of her nephew

that she truly could not stop bad things from happening.
