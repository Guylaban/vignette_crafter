graveyard later, or perhaps a funeral, he was never sure where one piece ended and the next began.

back more real than ordinary life, not like recalling a past day but like being pulled back into it.

of a cemetery, the solemn drift of grief-related gatherings, the sudden engine of a car passing too
near, or a police cruiser idling at the curb. Each of these jolted him into the conviction that he
was no longer capable of the life that had once defined him, that he could not deal with even the
slightest upset, and that he had to be especially careful because you never know what can happen
next.

Over the years, those appraisals narrowed his world. Rafiq began to judge every unsettling encounter
as proof that he was no longer the man who had once worked through hardship without flinching. When
a neighbor mentioned a memorial service, he felt a familiar sinking certainty that any brush with
mourning or authority might overwhelm him. The same fear rose around the sounds of cars outside his
home or shop; a backfiring engine could seem like the start of something dangerous. Because he

also avoided positive events that might make him feel happy or hopeful, as if letting himself enjoy

doorway, constantly scanning for signs of threat, and that constant vigilance fed back into the same
belief that he could not cope. The more he organized his days around avoiding upset, the more he
came to see himself as diminished, less and less capable of the work and steadiness that had once
given him dignity.
