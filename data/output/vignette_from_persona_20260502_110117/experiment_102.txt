not being able to undo what had happened. Since then, Aisha had found that anything at her workplace
could pull her back into that moment: the same conference room, the layout of her desk, the buzz of

like the injured client could make her stomach drop, and any reminder of victims in news stories or
in passing conversations would bring up the same hard image of the person she hurt.

In the weeks that followed, Aisha began to live as though she could keep the memory at bay by trying
to control it. She would dwell for hours on how the event could have been prevented, replaying every
decision and telling herself that if she had been more careful, slower, or less distracted, nobody
would have been hurt. At the same time, she tried hard not to think about the trauma at all, forcing
herself to push the images away whenever they surfaced. But the more she fought them, the more
readily they seemed to intrude, especially once she was back in the environment where it happened.

a reminder, and being surrounded by the same desks, screens, and meeting spaces made the event feel
close enough to happen again. The reminders of the victim and the similarity of certain faces only

to suppress the memory or mentally reconstruct the accident in hopes of finding the moment she
should have stopped it.

dependent she had become on him. The avoidance brought brief relief, but it also narrowed her world,

more the workplace and the memory of the injured person stood out in her mind as threats she could
not safely face. She would catch sight of someone in a lobby or on the street who resembled the
victim and feel a wave of panic that sent her back into rumination, asking herself again what she
might have done differently, then trying harder not to think at all. Even after she left the office,
she could not really leave the event behind; the effort to shut it out only seemed to make the
reminders sharper when they appeared.

By the time she sought help, Aisha was exhausted by how tightly her life had shrunk around these
efforts. She had been pushing through at work but felt increasingly on edge in the very setting
where she needed to function, and she was worried that her fear of reminders, her constant self-

living as though every workplace conversation, every face, and every car ride might return her to
