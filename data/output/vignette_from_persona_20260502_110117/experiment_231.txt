a noise that resembled a gunshot—would pull him back to the scene with immediate force. Those
moments did not just frighten him; they seemed to confirm what he had already begun to believe, that
he could not stop bad things from happening to him and that the world was, at its core, dangerous.

undone, and shrinking his world to the safest routines he could manage. At night he kept the lights

rather than remain with the images that surfaced in the dark. On some evenings he drank, or took

night. He told himself these were temporary measures, but they slowly became the main way he kept
going. The more he let fear set the terms, the more convinced he became that he was no longer
recognisable to himself. The steady, thoughtful man he had once assumed himself to be felt replaced
by someone tense, avoidant, and altered in ways he did not know how to explain to his wife.

could not keep catastrophe away, he had to at least keep it from reaching him emotionally, and so he
retreated further from difficult conversations, busy schedules, and anything that might unsettle
him. When the stress of managing work, sleep, and his own vigilance built up, he reached for the
same numbing strategies again, not because they solved anything but because they offered brief

them, the less confident he felt, and the more the world seemed dangerous and unpredictable. He
started scanning for risk even in places that should have felt familiar, and that constant alertness
made the nighttime and the slightest sudden noise feel even more potent than before.

times, drifting into private mental worlds to get through moments he did not trust himself to
handle. His wife noticed that he was more distant and less available in the evenings, and he felt

brought him in was the growing fear that he could not continue living so narrowly, and what he most
hoped for was to feel like himself again without waiting for the next shock in the dark.
