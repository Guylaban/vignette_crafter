the sense that escape depended on other people’s commands.

In the months that followed, Asha found that anything echoing that day could pull her back into the

and the faint smell of smoke from a neighbor’s cooking, a fireplace, or something burning in the
distance could turn her stomach and make her feel suddenly as if danger were close. Being placed in
any situation where someone else held the power over her—waiting for instructions, being told where

safest response was to avoid anything that might set them off. She stopped going to the area where
the bombing had happened, even though it was part of the route to places she once visited without

spaces she would sit with her back to a wall, scan the room over and over for signs of threat, and
choose seats or appointments that let her leave quickly if needed.

what she had lived through, she no longer trusted the world to announce danger in a polite or
predictable way.
