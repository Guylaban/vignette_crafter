increasingly on edge in ways that seemed to circle back on themselves. Years earlier, when he had

seconds he was trapped in intimate proximity to other bodies while trying to keep moving. He
survived, but afterward he could not stop replaying the moment in his mind. Over and over he went
back to the question of why it had happened to him, as if there were a hidden answer he could find
if he kept turning it over. What troubled him most was that his own sense of alarm had not only
followed obvious threat, but had also become tangled with moments that should have been good or
relieving. In the months that followed, even positive emotions felt wrong to him; if he laughed with
his wife, enjoyed a family dinner, or felt a brief lift at work, he would become uneasy, almost
suspicious of the feeling, as though something terrible might be waiting behind it.

Those reactions began to shape how Carlos moved through his days as a business owner. He kept a
weapon with him, telling himself that if he stayed prepared, he would never again be caught helpless
the way he had been abroad. He also found himself picking arguments, needling customers, and
creating drama with employees when the atmosphere was calm, partly because agitation felt more
familiar than peace and partly because it gave him somewhere to put the energy he could no longer
contain. The moments that set this off were often small and ordinary: a loud pop from a car
backfiring in the parking lot outside his shop, someone standing too close in line, a coworker
reaching past him unexpectedly, or the sudden warmth of a family celebration that should have felt
good. Each time, the body memory of the war zone seemed to rush in with it, and his immediate
response was to sharpen his vigilance, keep the weapon close, and stir up conflict before his
thoughts could settle on the trauma again. Yet the arguments and the constant readiness only fed the
very sensations he feared. The more he armed himself and picked fights to distract himself, the more
alert and reactive he became, scanning for the next sound, the next person moving too near, the next
surge of confusing good feeling that made him brace for danger.

when things were too peaceful. He told himself that being on guard was the only reason he had made
it through the war, and that letting his attention soften would leave him exposed again. That belief

invent a dispute to break the tension. But the strategy never gave him real relief for long. Instead
it made him more likely to notice every loud noise, every sudden closeness, and every moment of

of unease, he assumes something bad must be coming, and the old cycle of checking, arming, and
arguing begins again.

prepared. He had come after a particularly frightening afternoon at his business, when a sharp bang
from the back lot sent him rushing to the door with his weapon in hand, and he realized how quickly
he was being pulled back into the war zone in his mind. He is most afraid that if he cannot stop
himself from staying armed, arguing, and replaying the past, he will lose his marriage and
eventually become the kind of man who can no longer trust even a good moment. At the same time, he
is hoping for a way to feel safe without having to keep the whole world at arm’s length.
