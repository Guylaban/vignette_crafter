one moment ended and the next began. Even now, his memories of that night returned in disconnected
pieces: the smell of the apartment, a hand grabbing his arm, the pounding in his ears, the flash of

overtaken by those memories, he sometimes heard those same sounds in his head as if they were
happening again, and the recollection could be brought on instantly by something in his surroundings
without warning. A dim corridor, a shadowed stairwell, or the sound of a helicopter or low-flying
aircraft over the neighborhood could pull him back before he had time to prepare.

himself and other people. He said he had been betrayed by someone he trusted, and that betrayal
seemed to confirm for him that the world no longer worked the way he had believed it did. He spoke
with shame about the thought that the abuse happened because of the sort of person he was, as if
there were something about him that invited mistreatment. Those beliefs did not stay abstract; they
colored the memories themselves. When an image of the attack surfaced, it came attached to self-
condemnation and to the conviction that trust was foolish, so the memory felt not only vivid but
morally loaded, as if each fragment proved that he had misjudged both his partner and himself. Over
time, the same remembered pieces deepened his sense that everything he once relied on had been
destroyed.

Kareem’s attempts to cope slowly hardened into habits that shaped his days. He became vigilant for

place, taking longer routes home and declining invitations that would bring him near that street. He
also began wearing concealing clothing, favoring loose layers and darker jackets that helped him

unexpectedly call up the old memory. In that way, the very effort to protect himself made the world
feel full of possible dangers and made his trigger list grow broader.

intruding fragment seemed to confirm that he was marked by what had happened, that he had failed to
read the danger correctly, and that the world could no longer be trusted. Those appraisals
intensified his sense of threat, and his scanning became more relentless. At work, in his role as a
community organizer, he found himself preoccupied with who might be unsafe in the room, while the

to tolerate. He began arriving early to choose seats, leaving before others, and turning down

reinforced the belief that danger was everywhere and that he himself was somehow the reason it had
found him.

By the time Kareem sought help, he was exhausted by the loop of being set off, reliving the assault
in fragments and sounds, and then retreating further into suspicion and self-blame. He feared that

neighborhood could bring the whole memory crashing back again. What brought him in was the sense
that he could no longer keep organizing for his community while living as if every room might
contain harm, and what he most hoped for was to stop feeling betrayed by the world and by himself
long enough to trust his own life again.
