could not tolerate, and even a sudden bang from construction outside his apartment or a slammed door

moments he felt as if the event were happening again; strangers approaching him in ordinary
situations, even in the lobby of his building or on the street near his office, could make him brace

safe, safety could not be trusted, and the more he replayed the night, the more certain he became
that there was something about him that had made it happen, that he had somehow failed in a way that
left him no future and little faith in people, life, or anything larger holding things together.

What frightened Arjun most was how quickly those memories turned into a sense of immediate threat. A
smell, a bang, or the feeling of being emotionally exposed could set off the same alarm in him, and

altered state from drugs, and the habitual hiding all left him more aware of his body and more

relied on these strategies, the more convinced he became that danger was still close, because he was
constantly preparing for it. He found himself unable to believe reassurance from his partner or

one already closing in around him.
