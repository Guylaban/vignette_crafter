similarly painful quality for her, because it pulled her mind toward the possibility that something
precious could be taken without warning and left her feeling exposed and unsettled.

After the assault, Marisol began to judge herself harshly. She told herself that the way she
reacted—jumping at ordinary footsteps, avoiding certain streets, freezing when she felt the

contaminated by what had happened, as if the assault had left a stain on her that other people could
somehow sense. That sense of being dirty and inadequate deepened her unease around other people.

Over months, Marisol’s world contracted around these responses. She stopped lingering in crowded
