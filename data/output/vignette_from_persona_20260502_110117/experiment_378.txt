carrying memories of a childhood marked by physical abuse from a caregiver, memories that had

while he was at his desk, or when he noticed his own heart starting to pound in the middle of an
otherwise ordinary day. At those moments, the body sensations themselves became alarming: the heat
or chill of a room against his skin, the sudden racing in his chest, even a passing touch on the arm
could pull him back into the same fear he knew as a boy. He said that when that happened, he could
not trust his body not to betray him, and he would scan his surroundings for anything that might be
used as a weapon, as if staying alert might keep him from being caught off guard again.

Over time Robert had come to believe that he had to be especially careful because you never knew
what could happen next, and that belief shaped the way he moved through daily life. He had also come
to think of himself as permanently an outsider now, someone set apart from the ease other people
seemed to have, and he was increasingly convinced that people can’t be trusted. Those appraisals fed
into a set of habits that narrowed his world. He avoided paying attention to or looking at his own
body, because checking in with himself often led him straight to the sensations he feared. He
steered away from seeing people he had socialised with before the event, even old acquaintances from
work or neighbors from earlier years, because being around them stirred a sense that others were too
close, too readable, too likely to judge or hurt. He also avoided any form of physical contact with
others, including simple greetings that might put him in someone else’s reach. These habits brought
short-term relief, but they also left him more alone and more certain that he did not belong among
other people. The more he pulled back, the more outsider-like he felt, and the more his mistrust
hardened into a rule for living.

That rule also sharpened his sense of danger. If he saw a kitchen knife on the counter, a tool in

tightened and his heart started racing before he could think through whether there was any real

stay on guard, and the fear of being caught unprepared brought back the same vivid, involuntary

then avoid his own body even more, trying not to notice the pounding in his chest or the tension in

danger at bay, yet the effort was exhausting and isolating. He felt ashamed that he had withdrawn
from people he once knew, and troubled that even at work he was less at ease and less available than
he used to be. What finally brought him in was the realization that his vigilance was never really
letting him rest; the moment he noticed his heart racing, or caught an old memory rising up, or felt
a hand on his arm, he was right back in the same world of threat. He was hoping for some way to feel
safe in his own body again, and for help understanding why, even now, he still lived as though the
danger from childhood could appear at any moment.
