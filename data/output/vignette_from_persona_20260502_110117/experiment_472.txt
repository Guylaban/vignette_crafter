ringing in her ears, someone shouting to run, the thud of her own heart, and the raw surge of rage
she felt at the helplessness of it all. Since then, when the memory surfaces, she sometimes hears
those same sounds in her head, as if the attack is still unfolding somewhere just beyond her.

In the months after returning to the United States, Maya began to view the world through the lens of
that day. She had come to believe that people cannot be trusted, because the attack had turned even
ordinary routines into something dangerously fragile. Her thoughts about herself were just as

it hard for her to feel anything approaching steadiness or meaning. She described her life now as

wrong, certain that if danger had once appeared so suddenly, it could appear anywhere again.

That conviction followed her home. Maya now feels on guard even inside her own house, checking the

ready to leave at once. The more she thinks about how untrustworthy other people are, and the more

trapped; that fear keeps her vigilant and makes ordinary places feel barely safe enough to enter.

the voices and sounds from the attack in her mind are often followed by renewed alarm, and once her

guarded she becomes, the more the fragments return: the sequence remains scrambled, the sounds break

way out.
