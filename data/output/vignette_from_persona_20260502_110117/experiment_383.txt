her most was that it happened in ordinary, public settings, in daylight, in places where she had
always assumed she would be protected by busyness and by other people. She described a growing
conviction that if someone wanted access to her, they could find it.

building or outside the clinic does not settle her; it reminds her that the situation had become
serious enough to involve authority, and yet it also leaves her with the sense that danger can still
be real right in front of everyone. Each of these moments seems to confirm what she fears most: even
when others tell her she is safe, she finds it impossible to believe it.

cornered. At work, she has become noticeably less relaxed with unfamiliar visitors and more alert

message on her: danger can reappear without warning, and she cannot rely on reassurance from other
people to override that sense.
