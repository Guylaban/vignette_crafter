seatbelt, the smell of burned rubber, his wife’s voice somewhere beside him, and then a frightening
blankness that made it hard even to describe the scene afterward. In the weeks that followed, he
found he could not put the event into words in any orderly way, only fragments and sensations that
came back on their own. When he tried to remember, he sometimes heard the screeching metal and the
crack of glass in his head as if the crash were happening again, and the memory would flood him with
intense shame, as though surviving somehow marked him as weak or flawed.

What frightened Javier most was not only the wreck itself but what it seemed to prove about him. He
had come to believe that the accident happened because of the sort of person he was, that he was
somehow the kind of man bad things could happen to, and from there he became certain that he could
not stop bad things from happening to him. That belief made the world feel dangerous in a constant,
immediate way. He started scanning for exits whenever he entered a room or building, checking doors
and hallways before he could settle, and his body remained on alert for any sudden threat. Even
casual contact could set him off: if someone brushed his shoulder unexpectedly in the studio, he
reacted with alarm, his whole frame tightening before he could catch himself. The fear, horror,
anger, guilt, and shame that rose in him were not limited to memories of the wreck; they spilled
into daily life, shaping how he moved through the studio, the neighborhood, and his own home.

engaging with them, and he stayed up until very late so he would be too exhausted to dream or think.

emptiness around the memory. The more he pushed it away, the less he could organize what had
happened into words, and the fragments that did return came with even more sensory force: the sounds
in his head, the sudden shame, the feeling that his body had betrayed him. In that way, the effort
to escape the memory made the memory more disjointed and harder to contain, and it also hardened his
belief that something was fundamentally wrong with him. If he needed to blank out just to get
through the day, he concluded, then perhaps he really would never be able to feel normal emotions
