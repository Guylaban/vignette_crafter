came the same terror she had felt during the assault. In those moments she would hear, in her mind,
fragments of what she had heard then, as if the sounds of the attack were still echoing inside her
head. She had come to believe that her body kept reacting as if it were still in danger even when
she was safe, and that what happened to her was deeply unfair and nothing had been done about it.
The assault also left her convinced that she could not rely on other people, not when danger had
arrived in the presence of others who had not prevented it.

way, and she became constantly vigilant for dangerous people in crowds, parking lots, and on the

that her fear meant she was losing control, and that self-surveillance narrowed her world further.

ominous, so that the racing heart, the sight of violence, or the need to ride in a car with others

threat more closely, and that narrow focus gave the memories more opportunities to surface.

beneath the surface of every ordinary day.
