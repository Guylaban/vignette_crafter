some way to blunt the feeling. At funerals, in cemeteries, or at grief-filled family gatherings,

beneath the surface.

What Rajan could not escape was the way the memory itself kept intruding. Some parts of that evening
remained locked in with punishing clarity — the rain on the windshield, the angle of the other car,
the echo of voices — while other stretches were blank, so incomplete that he felt he was only
remembering fragments and the rest was missing. When he tried to recall it, he sometimes heard in
his head the same sounds he had heard at the scene, as if the moment had never really ended. Those
broken, sensory memories fed his conviction that he had destroyed something irreparable. He had come
to believe that his beliefs about how the world works had been completely destroyed, because a world

same loop of self-condemnation, and in response he would clamp down harder, trying not to think
about the trauma and forcing himself to move on. Yet each attempt to suppress it made the memories
return with greater force, often in the middle of work at the tax office when he was reviewing files
