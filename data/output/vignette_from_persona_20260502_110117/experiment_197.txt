long before the routines of classrooms and staff meetings, there was the night in Vietnam that still

who stood where and how quickly he could get out. At work, he positioned himself close to classroom
doors and noticed the route to the hallway before he could relax enough to focus on a child’s needs.

it had narrowed his world; he declined social invitations, cut conversations short, and kept people
at a manageable distance, especially if they might ask about the war.

superalert and watchful, and unexpected or loud sounds had him on edge before he could think. A

across the floor could send his attention snapping outward, and in that instant he was no longer in
a classroom but back in a place where the next explosion might kill him. He had come to believe that

emerge from any corner. That constant planning for flight was not separate from his vigilance; it

ordinary places.

then redouble his efforts to control where he sat, how he moved, and what he allowed himself to

room for danger and to speak about what happened without his body acting as though the blast were
