particular strain of hearing anger in another person’s voice even when they were only speaking
“normally.” Those moments had trained him to brace for harm before anything outwardly obvious
happened.

kitchens, and utility rooms as a plumber, he would catch a whiff of something burnt from a furnace,

why. If a homeowner sounded irritated about the bill, or if two coworkers argued near the truck, he
would immediately become guarded, reading danger into every raised tone. He had come to believe that
anger was the first sign that things could turn physical, and that belief pushed him into habits

from being pulled back into the old terror.

as much as his days. If he knew he had passed a place that smelled burnt, or if a customer had

said that sleep made him vulnerable because that was when his mind could turn toward what he had
been trying not to think about, and once he was alone with it he could feel the whole scene crowding
in. The late nights left him exhausted, but he still preferred that to the feeling of being ambushed
by images of the apartment, the anger, and the sense that he had not known how to stop it.

evening around avoidance and every morning around dread, but he was equally afraid that therapy
would require him to look directly at the memories he had spent decades pushing away.
