particular quality of the light through a stand of Douglas fir, the sound of a door being pulled
shut and locked from the outside, the sensation of his wrists against restraints. Others are
entirely blank — stretches of time he cannot account for, which disturb him perhaps more than the
memories he does carry. He cannot, he says, really put any of it into words. It is less a story he
can tell than something that lives in his body.

In the weeks following his rescue and medical discharge, Caleb returned to his apartment and largely
did not leave it. His injuries had been assessed as serious but non-life-threatening, yet he had
come to believe something more permanent had been done to him — that his body was fundamentally
compromised in ways the doctors had either missed or not told him. He monitors physical sensations

trembled during a routine task, he was certain it was evidence of something neurological. What

to a sound outside or a shadow crossing the window. He had begun to fear that this gap between
stimulus and awareness meant something was fundamentally broken in his mind, that the reactions he

during the abduction, and the enclosed space of a car — the inability to exit, the sense of being

rangers in his district are required to conduct vehicle patrols across large areas of terrain, and

something he does deliberately, as a kind of management, though he acknowledges that what he is left

something that looked like careful restraint, is to be told that is not true.
