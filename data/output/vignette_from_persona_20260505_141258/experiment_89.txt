while fuel leaked and other drivers shouted from the roadside. Paramedics noted a strong smell of

clavicle and two ribs, and he remained in that wrenched, compressed position for nearly forty
minutes before rescue workers could extract him.

past where he expected it to go. Instead, it returned to him with a quality he struggled to describe
to his wife, Leilani — not like remembering, he told her, but like being pulled back inside it. When
the memories arrived they were not visual alone. He could smell the alcohol again, sharp and
unmistakable, a smell that now reached him across distances that should have been safe: a colleague
cracking open a beer at a weekend barbecue, the cleaning aisle at the hardware store, even Leilani's

frightened Rangi most in these moments was what happened to his body. He would feel his torso twist

articulate but impossible to shake, that his body had not fully left the wreckage.
