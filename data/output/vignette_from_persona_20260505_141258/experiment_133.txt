his closest living relative — face-down and unresponsive in chest-deep, debris-filled water in what

brother was fully discharged, a fact that had settled into him like a stone.

swelled toward its banks, he found himself unable to sit at his desk. The physical reaction came
before any conscious thought: his heart was already hammering, his hands already damp, before his
mind had registered what his eyes had seen. He described this as deeply disorienting for a man who
had built his professional identity around careful, deliberate reasoning — his body, he said, had
stopped waiting for him.

stung because it was not entirely wrong.
