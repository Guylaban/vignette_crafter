in a way Rangi describes as impossible to unhear. He was close enough to see everything and far

serious injuries. The men responsible were removed from the scene, but in the months that followed,
no formal charges were pursued. The church leadership, where Rangi had worshipped for over twenty

In the weeks that followed, something shifted in him that he struggled to name. He had come to feel,
with a quiet but absolute certainty, that the institution he had trusted most had simply closed its
doors on what happened — and on him. He did not speak of this readily, but it sat in him like a

reconstruct. When he tried to explain what had happened — to his wife, to his doctor, to the intake

ways he did not fully understand.

their gaze, alert for something he cannot quite name but is certain he would recognize if it
appeared. Shoppers with infants in carts present a particular difficulty; the sound of a crying baby
— even a brief, ordinary cry — can stop him mid-transaction. He has twice needed to ask a colleague
to cover his register, offering no explanation.
