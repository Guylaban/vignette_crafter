despite Aisha’s desperate attempts to pull him out, the tide had already claimed him. The loss of
her brother and the image of the boat’s twisted hull on that day are still etched into her mind.

could not hold her own fishing net. In the months that followed, every anniversary of the crash
became a looming trigger. When the calendar marked the day of the accident, Aisha would
instinctively feel a tightness in her chest, her heart racing even before she left her apartment.

passing sight of a vehicle colliding with a docked ship would set her on edge. These triggers
directly drew her into a state of intense threat: she would experience a rush of anger, guilt, and
shame; her pulse would spike, her hands trembled, and she often found herself snapping at Malik or
the crew on the boat. The very thought of being reminded of the crash—whether through a photo, a
news story, or the sound of engines—set her body and mind on high alert, creating a heightened sense
of danger that she could not shake. Aisha’s negative appraisals compounded the distress. She held
herself to a standard that was impossible to meet, convinced that if she had acted differently,

guilt and shame she carried made her feel more vulnerable, as if a threat was always lurking, ready
to strike again. Each time she replayed her perceived failure, her body would react—her heart would
