instructions to follow up with occupational medicine.

cycled through a series of shorter-term HVAC jobs, always finding reasons to leave—a new building, a
new company, never staying long enough to feel settled. His wife, Linh, says he is not the same man
she married. He is fifty-two now, and his body has become a stranger to him, a thing that cannot be
trusted to keep him safe. Even lying in bed at night, his muscles remain coiled, his breathing
shallow and ready. He wakes sometimes in the dark convinced that something toxic is seeping under
the bedroom door, that the walls themselves are poisoning the air.

Linh tries to reassure him—when she says the house is safe, the air is clean, nothing bad is going
