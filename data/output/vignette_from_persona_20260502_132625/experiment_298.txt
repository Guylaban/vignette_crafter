without moving. Then she locked the door, finished her shift, and drove home in the dark.

contained specific threats that she had to manage through constant vigilance and control. She had

that justice was impossible and that she was powerless. What finally brought her to seek help was
not a crisis but a slow recognition that she could no longer sustain the machinery of her own
survival. She was afraid that if she stopped working, she would collapse entirely. She was afraid
that if she tried to speak about what happened, the shame would consume her. But she was also,
beneath all of that, hoping that someone might help her understand why her own mind and body had
become her greatest threats, and whether she could ever feel safe again.
