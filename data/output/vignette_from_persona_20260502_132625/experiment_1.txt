The worst part is the conviction, which has grown harder to shake with each passing week, that his
reactions prove something fundamentally broken inside him. A strong man would have moved past this.
A capable man would not be haunted by a trauma that happened fifteen years ago, when his daughter
survived. He lies awake at three in the morning, his mind cycling through the same thoughts: he is
weak, he is going mad, he cannot stop bad things from happening. And because he believes these
things about himself, he finds himself more alert, more vigilant, more certain that danger is always
seconds away. The vigilance feeds the belief, which feeds the memory, which feeds the conviction
that he is coming apart at the seams.

Last week, Rajesh finally made an appointment. He told Meera only that he was having trouble
sleeping, but the truth is more complicated and more frightening: he is running out of room inside
himself to contain what happened. He is afraid that if he does not find help soon, he will lose not
just his career but his family—that the distance he has created between himself and the people he
loves will become permanent, and he will have failed them again.
