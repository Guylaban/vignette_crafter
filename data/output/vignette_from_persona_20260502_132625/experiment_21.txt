him, and the pretense had become exhausting.

The trauma that had shaped Tane's life had occurred five years earlier, during what he thought would

the sight of his own blood on Marcus's knuckles—had burned itself into Tane's nervous system. When
it was over, Tane had lain on the kitchen floor for nearly an hour, too afraid to move, convinced
Marcus might return. He never reported it. He never told anyone, not really, until now.

In the five years since, Tane had built a life around the architecture of fear. The moment someone

would pull the memory forward unbidden, and Tane would find himself back in that kitchen, his body
flooding with the same helplessness he'd felt then. When his sister had tried to talk with him about

didn't need, expensive dinners eaten alone, anything to push the conversation and the memory away.
The compulsive spending became a reflex, a way to interrupt the cascade of shame and terror that
talking about the event triggered. He had maxed out two credit cards this way, but the debt felt
like a small price for the temporary silence in his head.
