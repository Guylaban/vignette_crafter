right. The uncertainty about what could have been done differently gnawed at her. As time went by,
these triggers only intensified. Arguments with her husband, Tuan, over seemingly mundane things
would escalate into full-blown fights, leaving Leila feeling lost and anxious. She'd try to reason
with herself that it was just a normal disagreement, but the uncertainty about whether she could
have prevented the accident seeped into every aspect of their relationship. "What if I'm not good
enough?" she'd wonder. To cope with these overwhelming emotions, Leila started avoiding physical

fighting for air. She stopped attending yoga classes and took the elevator instead of stairs at

to work from home some days, which became more frequent as she found it increasingly difficult to
face the crowded office spaces. Avoiding friends was another strategy Leila employed - seeing them
only made her feel like an outsider, watching their carefree lives through a distorted lens of guilt

route, none of this would have happened. Her mind kept replaying the accident, each iteration
leading to more what-ifs and could-haves. In darker moments, Leila resorted to self-harm as a
distraction from the relentless thoughts. A few scratches on her wrist or a burned hand seemed like
small prices to pay for temporary reprieve from the constant barrage of "if onlys". Her husband

existence couldn't go on. She thought of all the times she'd hurt herself and felt only temporary
relief; the arguments with Tuan that left them both drained; and the lost friendships - she couldn't
keep living in this half-existence. The weight of it finally brought Leila to seek help, her
greatest fear being that she might never break free from this self-reinforcing cycle, forever
trapped in a world of what-ifs and could-haves. Leila sat on the couch, surrounded by the familiar
walls of her home, yet felt like an outsider looking in. As she waited for her appointment with the
therapist, she couldn't help but wonder - would anyone be able to show her that this wasn't all
there was?
