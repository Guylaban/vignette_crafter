Maria, a 47-year-old Latina woman and social ministry worker, has been grappling with the aftermath of a traumatic rape that occurred over six months ago. The event left her feeling violated not only physically but also emotionally and spiritually. Maria's daily life is now dominated by intense fears and maladaptive behaviors that have significantly altered how she interacts with the world. The assault took place late one evening when Maria was walking home from her church volunteer work in a dimly lit alley. The assailant, who emerged silently from the shadows, attacked her without warning. In the moments before the attack, the silence was particularly unsettling; it made Maria feel that something terrible was about to happen. This unusual silence has become a potent trigger for her since then. In the immediate aftermath of the assault, Maria found herself unable to fully articulate what had happened. The event felt like a series of snapshots in her mind-moments frozen in time but impossible to narrate coherently. Each snapshot is vivid and haunting, triggering intense guilt every time she remembers it. This inability to find words to describe her experience has been compounded by the profound shame she feels about what happened to her body. Maria's faith, once a source of comfort and strength, was severely shaken by the event. She began to feel that something had fundamentally changed within herself and in the world around her. The assault left her with a deep sense of loss-loss of happiness, loss of trust in others and life itself, and even a loss of faith. This pervasive negativity has led Maria to adopt various maladaptive strategies as coping mechanisms. One such strategy is avoiding relaxation because it feels dangerous to her. Relaxation reminds her too much of the vulnerability she experienced during the assault, making her feel unsafe despite being aware that she is in a safe place. She tells herself that she deserves to suffer because of what happened, which reinforces her belief that she has lost happiness and faith. Maria's eating habits have also changed drastically; she controls her food intake strictly, using it as another means to exert control over her life. The fear of losing control is tied directly to the trauma of feeling violated during the assault, leading her to adopt this maladaptive behavior to regain a sense of order in her life. Unusual silence and delays continue to trigger intense feelings of danger for Maria. These triggers are linked to the specific moments from her memory that replay vividly. Each time she experiences these sensations, it reinforces her negative appraisals and increases her reliance on avoidance behaviors. The cycle is self-reinforcing: the more frequently she avoids relaxation or controls her eating, the stronger her belief becomes that she deserves punishment and has lost faith. Her occupation as a social ministry worker has been particularly challenging due to these symptoms. Maria often finds herself avoiding certain community events where silence might be present, leading to strained relationships with colleagues who do not understand her sudden absences. She also feels increasingly isolated at work because of her reluctance to discuss what happened, further deepening her sense of guilt and shame. What finally brought Maria to seek help was a recent incident where she had an acute panic attack during a community meeting in silence. This triggered a flashback that left her feeling completely overwhelmed and unable to function normally at work. She is most afraid of losing control again and of never being able to fully recover from the trauma. At the same time, Maria is hoping for relief from her overwhelming sense of guilt and shame, and she seeks ways to regain some semblance of peace in her life. In summary, Maria's current state is a complex interplay of maladaptive strategies, heightened threat perceptions, fragmented memories, and deeply rooted negative appraisals. The traumatic event has left indelible marks on her psyche, creating a self-reinforcing cycle that she finds increasingly difficult to break without professional intervention.
