Mohammad is a 56-year-old Middle Eastern man who works as a social media manager. Married and living in a suburban area, he has been experiencing significant distress following an encounter with police violence several months ago. The traumatic event involved him being wrongfully arrested during a peaceful protest for civil rights. During the arrest, he was physically restrained and verbally abused, which left him feeling deeply violated and fearful. Since then, Mohammad's life has been marked by a series of maladaptive strategies that have gradually isolated him from both his personal and professional environments. He avoids any form of media that might trigger memories or emotions related to the event, which means he no longer watches TV programs or reads news articles about similar incidents. Instead, he constantly scans his surroundings for signs of threat, always identifying escape routes before he feels comfortable in any setting. The vividness of his memory often overwhelms him, especially the taste and rage associated with the traumatic experience. When these memories surface, they are so intense that Mohammad can almost taste the dust and sweat from that fateful day. However, he finds himself pushing the memory away before he can fully recall it, as the intensity is too much to bear. Mohammad's negative appraisals have played a significant role in his current state of distress. He believes that people are not what they seem, and this distrust has led him to avoid many social interactions. Additionally, he feels that he acted in ways that go against who he is-a sense of violation that has eroded his self-worth. The belief that the world is a dangerous place exacerbates his constant state of vigilance and fear. These negative appraisals have directly influenced Mohammad's maladaptive strategies. His deep distrust and feeling of being violated make it hard for him to engage in any activity that might trigger negative feelings, both positive and negative. He avoids anything that could evoke strong emotions, even positive ones like joy or excitement, as he fears they will be overshadowed by the haunting memories. The vividness of his memory also feeds into his sense of threat. The intense rage he feels when remembering the event triggers a cycle where he becomes increasingly vigilant and unable to relax, even in safe environments. This constant state of alertness has made it difficult for him to focus on work, leading to strained relationships with colleagues. Mohammad's maladaptive strategies have further reinforced his negative appraisals. By avoiding media and emotions, he inadvertently limits his ability to process the traumatic event fully, which keeps the memory fragmented and intense. This fragmentation only deepens his distrust of others and intensifies his belief that the world is dangerous. The weight of these connections has gradually tightened into a self-reinforcing cycle. Mohammad's daily routine as a social media manager has become a battlefield where he must navigate around potential triggers, avoiding conversations or posts that remind him of the traumatic event. This avoidance has led to a noticeable decline in his work performance and strained relationships with colleagues who have begun to notice his withdrawal. What finally brought Mohammad to seek help was the realization that he could no longer function effectively at work or sustain meaningful interactions with friends and family without feeling overwhelmed by fear and rage. He is most afraid of losing control, especially when memories resurface unexpectedly. At the same time, he hopes for a way to regain a sense of safety and trust in his relationships and environment. Through this narrative, it becomes clear that Mohammad's maladaptive strategies, negative appraisals, memory disturbances, and heightened threat perceptions form an intricate web of interconnected elements that have progressively eroded his quality of life.
