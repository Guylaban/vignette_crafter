Sara, a 32-year-old South Asian woman and city council member, experienced a traumatic event that has left her with persistent symptoms of post-traumatic stress disorder (PTSD). The event was a man-made disaster-a major industrial accident in the heart of the city where she worked. On the day of the disaster, Sara was attending an important meeting at City Hall when she received news about the incident just blocks away from their building. She witnessed chaos as emergency vehicles rushed to the scene and later learned that several people had been severely injured. In the immediate aftermath, Sara found herself struggling with hypervigilance towards people and constantly scanning her environment for signs of threat. Her experience at City Hall made her hyper-aware of potential dangers lurking in crowds and unfamiliar places. She began avoiding situations that put her body under stress, such as attending public events or physical meetings, opting instead to work from home whenever possible. Sara's trauma has also influenced her nighttime experiences. She found it increasingly difficult to feel safe at night compared to the daytime. This was partly due to the darkness and reduced visibility, making it harder for her to maintain a sense of control over her surroundings. Sara started planning escape routes wherever she went, feeling safer only after identifying multiple exits or ways out. Her work as a city council member has been profoundly affected by these symptoms. She avoids attending public meetings or visiting new locations due to the fear of unpredictable dangers. This avoidance led to strained relationships with colleagues who began to notice her absence from key events and meetings. Her commitment to her job remained strong, but she felt increasingly isolated in her efforts. Sara had come to believe that the world was a dangerous place and that vigilance was necessary at all times. She lived with an underlying fear that something could happen again at any moment, reinforcing her need to be especially careful. These beliefs manifested as constant checking of her surroundings, ensuring she always knew where the nearest exits were located. The cumulative effect of these symptoms began to take a toll on Sara's mental and emotional well-being. Her hypervigilance and avoidance behaviors resulted in social isolation and professional strain. Despite her best efforts, she felt increasingly disconnected from her community and colleagues. Ultimately, it was the realization that her quality of life was suffering and her ability to fulfill her responsibilities as a city council member was compromised that prompted Sara to seek help. She was most afraid of losing control again and being unable to protect herself or others in a similar situation. What she hoped for was regaining a sense of safety and normalcy in her daily life, without the constant fear and vigilance that had become her new reality. Sara's journey toward recovery began with acknowledging these fears and understanding how they shaped her actions and perceptions. Through therapy, she aimed to find ways to manage her symptoms more effectively, reclaiming her sense of control and peace.
