Marcus, a 51-year-old male from Oceania, has been married for over two decades and works as a welder. His life took an abrupt turn nearly six months ago when he was involved in a life-threatening motor vehicle collision. The trauma of the accident has left indelible marks on his psyche, manifesting through vivid triggers and intense memory recall. The traumatic event unfolded late one evening when Marcus was driving home from work. As he approached an intersection, another car hit him by accident, sending his vehicle into a spinning skid. The impact was severe, leaving him dazed and disoriented as the world around him shattered into a cacophony of sounds and smells. The alcohol fumes from the other driver's breath mingled with the metallic scents of crushed metal and burning rubber, creating an overwhelming sensory assault that would haunt him long after the crash. Since then, Marcus has found himself reliving the event repeatedly through his memories. When these memories come to mind, he feels as if his body is reenacting the position it was in during the accident. He often reports feeling physical pain in the same parts of his body where he was hurt that night. The sensory recall is so intense that when a memory returns, it feels completely real across all senses at once-Marcus can smell the alcohol and hear the screeching of tires as if they were happening in the present. These memories do not occur in isolation; instead, they are triggered by specific events and stimuli from his daily life. The sight of cars being hit by accident on TV or while driving to work can instantly transport him back to that fateful night. Similarly, the smell of alcohol during social gatherings brings a rush of distressing sensations as he relives the impact and aftermath. Even the sounds of car engines and brakes can provoke a visceral response, making it difficult for Marcus to engage in activities where these triggers are present. The narrative of his trauma has become an unrelenting cycle. When memories return, they feel so real that they intensify the triggers, leading to a recurrent loop of distress. This cycle has significantly impacted his daily life as a welder, where he often encounters vehicles and machinery with engines and brakes. The constant vigilance and avoidance behaviors have caused strain in both his work and personal relationships. Marcus's wife has noticed a marked change in him since the accident. He avoids going out with friends or attending family events that might expose him to cars or alcohol, which only exacerbates his sense of isolation. At work, he finds it challenging to focus on tasks without being distracted by intrusive thoughts and memories, leading to errors and a decline in performance. What finally brought Marcus to seek help was the growing realization that his life had become unmanageable due to these symptoms. He is most afraid that he will never be able to escape the haunting memories and triggers, but he also harbors hope that with support, he can regain control over his life and find a way to process his trauma without it overwhelming him daily. The vividness of his memory recall and the intensity of his triggers continue to dominate Marcus's experience, making each day a struggle. However, he is determined to break free from this cycle and reclaim his sense of self and peace.
