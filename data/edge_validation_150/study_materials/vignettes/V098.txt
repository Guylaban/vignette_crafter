Mr. Kwame Johnson, a 62-year-old African American married man and bomb disposal technician, experienced a traumatic event that has left lasting psychological scars. The incident occurred six months ago when he was exposed to toxic chemicals during the dismantling of an old munitions cache in a remote area. Despite wearing protective gear, Mr. Johnson's mask malfunctioned, leading to significant inhalation of the noxious fumes. This experience has profoundly impacted his daily life and work. Since that day, loud noises at his workplace trigger intense anxiety for Mr. Johnson. The sound of heavy machinery or sudden alarms brings back vivid memories of the toxic exposure. He recalls feeling suffocated as he frantically tried to remove his malfunctioning mask in a panic-stricken state. This has made it difficult for him to concentrate on his job, and he often feels overwhelmed by the auditory triggers that seem to be everywhere around him. Additionally, Mr. Johnson is hypersensitive to smells reminiscent of antiseptic environments found in hospitals. The strong, sterilizing scent immediately transports him back to the medical facility where he was treated after the exposure incident. He describes feeling trapped and panicked at the mere thought of being in a hospital setting again. As a result, any medical cues like ambulances or physical examinations trigger significant distress. To cope with his overwhelming anxiety, Mr. Johnson has adopted several maladaptive strategies. One notable behavior is his tendency to avoid being alone. This fear stems from the intense loneliness and isolation he experienced during the period of forced confinement in quarantine after the exposure. He now feels safest when there are others around him, whether it be colleagues or family members. Another coping mechanism is overworking himself. By keeping himself constantly busy at work, Mr. Johnson can avoid the overwhelming thoughts and memories that surface when he's alone. This has led to a significant increase in his workload, often working late hours to ensure he remains occupied. However, this behavior has begun to strain his relationship with his wife, who feels neglected as she seldom sees him due to his excessive work schedule. Mr. Johnson also engages in constant threat monitoring behaviors. He always identifies escape routes before feeling comfortable anywhere, and he is hyper-vigilant about potential dangers. This hyper-awareness means that he constantly scans his surroundings for any signs of imminent threat or danger. Unexpected physical contact from others now elicits a startle response; even a simple tap on the shoulder can cause him to jump and feel intensely alarmed. The accumulation of these symptoms has taken a significant toll on Mr. Johnson's life. His overwork has led to numerous conflicts at home, leaving his wife feeling increasingly isolated and concerned about his well-being. At work, colleagues have noticed his declining performance and heightened anxiety levels. The combination of stress from both personal and professional domains has finally compelled him to seek help. What frightens Mr. Johnson most is the prospect of losing control over his life and being unable to perform his job effectively. He fears that his condition will progressively worsen, leaving him unable to care for himself or support his family. Despite these fears, he remains hopeful that with professional guidance, he can regain a sense of normalcy and reclaim his life. In seeking help, Mr. Johnson hopes to learn coping strategies that do not involve excessive work or avoidance behaviors. He is eager to address the triggers that have significantly disrupted his daily functioning and relationship with his wife. By understanding and managing his symptoms more effectively, he aims to regain control over his life and continue performing his vital role as a bomb disposal technician without being hindered by overwhelming anxiety and fear.
